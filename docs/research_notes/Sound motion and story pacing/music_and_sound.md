# Music and sound that move people to tears: evidence, craft, and a plan for "Text me when you get home"

Labels used below: findings under "Cited Findings" are sourced (peer-reviewed evidence is marked [E], composer or practitioner craft opinion is marked [C], technical documentation is marked [D]). Everything under "Inferences" is this researcher's reasoning, including all story-specific recommendations, and should be read as proposals, not facts. Code references point to `site/stories/text-me/js/score.js`, `sfx.js`, `main.js` and `script.js` as read on 2026-10-30.

## 1. The science of chills, tears and being moved

### Takeaway
Chills and tears are two different peak responses: chills ride on arousal, surprise, loudness rises, widening register and new voices entering, while tears come with slow, calm, sad music, melodic appoggiaturas and descending sequences, and last about twice as long. The pleasure of sad music runs through the feeling of "being moved", which research ties to a sudden intensification of closeness between people; nostalgia, memory and familiarity amplify it, and a silence after an unresolved phrase holds tension while a silence after closure releases it.

### Cited Findings
**Tears versus chills**
- [E] Sloboda (1991, Psychology of Music 19, 110 to 120) surveyed 83 listeners who named specific passages that reliably moved them physically. Tears were most reliably linked to melodic appoggiaturas, with melodic sequences and harmony moving around the cycle of fifths as smaller factors; shivers (chills) were most reliably linked to new or unexpected harmonies, and also to sudden dynamic or textural changes. [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/28754222606c17abaae30191e67bc59d); [summary with factor detail](https://www.typeset.io/papers/music-structure-and-emotional-response-some-empirical-366hteuxxx)
- [E] Caveat on Sloboda: a critique notes the study cannot show how many appoggiatura passages failed to move anyone, that the term "appoggiatura" gets used loosely in later retellings, and that Sloboda himself was careful about what the results mean. [New Music USA](https://newmusicusa.org/?p=275769)
- [E] Mori and Iwanaga (2017, Scientific Reports 7, 46063) separated chills from tears physiologically. 154 Japanese undergraduates were surveyed; a chills group (32) and a tears group (34) listened to self-chosen songs (198 songs in total). Chills raised skin conductance and felt arousal; tears came with slower breathing while heart rate rose, and no rise in skin conductance, which the authors read as calming. Tear songs were rated sad and calmer; chill songs were rated both happy and sad. Tears lasted longer: 6.60 s versus 3.47 s on average (t(131) = -3.85, p < 0.001). The authors argue tears carry a cathartic, calming function, while noting tears did not raise self-reported relaxation. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5384201/); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/psychologists-have-studied-whats-happening-when-music-gives-us-chills-or-makes-us)

**What triggers chills (frisson)**
- [E] Grewe, Nagel, Kopiez and Altenmuller (2007, Music Perception 24(3), 297 to 314): chills did not follow a simple stimulus-response pattern and were not explained by personality; the patterns that triggered the most chills were the entry of a voice and changes in loudness, confirmed on retest. [Abstract record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_222216288)
- [E] Nagel et al. (2008, Musicae Scientiae): chill passages showed a significant loudness increase specifically in the 920 to 4400 Hz range. [Sonicfield record](https://sonicfield.org/library/psychoacoustical-correlates-of-musically-induced-chills)
- [E] Guhn, Hamm and Zentner (2007, Music Perception 24(5), 473 to 484): a 27-person pilot picked chill passages, then 27 new participants were measured in real time. Chills clustered in passages with similar dynamic, harmonic and structural features (crescendos, a solo voice or instrument entering, solo against accompaniment, widening of the frequency range), which coincided with rises in heart rate and skin conductance. [Sonicfield record](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/physiological-and-musico-acoustic-correlates-of-the-chill-response)
- [E] de Fleurian and Pearce (2021, Psychological Bulletin 147(9), 890 to 920) reviewed 167 research reports (1980 to 2020) and proposed three mechanisms for chills: arousal, expectation and emotion. [QMUL repository](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/75159)
- [E] de Fleurian and Pearce (2021, i-Perception, doi 10.1177/20416695211024680): in a corpus of 988 tracks people had reported as chill-inducing, compared with matched controls, chill tracks were sadder and, on exploratory measures, slower, less intense and more instrumental. [X-MOL record](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1420513943825244160)
- [E] Panksepp (1995, Music Perception): chills tracked perceived sadness in music much more strongly than perceived happiness, and females reported more chills than males. Panksepp proposed that sad music engages the neurochemistry of social loss (the "separation call" idea). [Sonicfield record](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/the-emotional-sources-of-chills-induced-by-music)
- [E] A 2025 Durham master's thesis tested the separation-call hypothesis directly and did not support it: chills were more common for music than for real infant cries, the opposite of the prediction. (Thesis, not peer-reviewed.) [Durham e-theses](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/15886/)
- [E] Individual differences are large: Sachs, Ellis, Schlaug and Loui (2016, SCAN) found people who get chills have larger white-matter connections from auditory cortex (superior temporal gyrus) to the insula and medial prefrontal cortex; non-chill reporters had the same pathways but smaller. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4884308)
- [E] Colver and El-Alayli (2016, Psychology of Music): in about 100 college students, frisson frequency correlated with Openness to Experience and five of its six facets; the cognitive side (predicting how the music unfolds, musical imagery) mattered more than the emotional side. (Press coverage reports 113 recruited, 97 analysed.) [MetaTOC abstract](https://metatoc.com/papers/48701-getting-aesthetic-chills-from-music-the-connection-between-openness-to-experience-and-frisson); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/2016/06/getting-chills-great-music-connected-new-cognitive-dimension-personality-43577)

**Why sad music is enjoyed: being moved**
- [E] Vuoskoski and Eerola (2017, Frontiers in Psychology 8, 439): with 308 participants and five sad excerpts, the link between liking and felt sadness (r = 0.22) was fully mediated by feeling moved; a second experiment with 27 excerpts varying sadness and beauty independently supported "being moved" over "beauty" as the mediator. Felt sadness seems to add to enjoyment by intensifying the feeling of being moved. [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00439/text)
- [E] Kama muta (the research term for "being moved"): across 19 nations, 15 languages and 3,542 participants, it is evoked by experiencing or seeing a sudden intensification of communal sharing (closeness, belonging), and typically comes with warmth in the chest, moist eyes or tears, chills, a lump in the throat, and buoyancy. Stimuli were short heartwarming videos, not music. [ISCTE record, Emotion 2019](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/kama-muta-conceptualizing-and-measuring-the-experience-of-being-moved-across-19-nations-and-15/47393?lang=en); [5-country video study, 2018](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/kama-muta-similar-emotional-responses-to-touching-videos-across-t/)
- [E] Taruffi and Koelsch (2014, PLOS ONE, N = 772): nostalgia, not sadness, was the emotion most often evoked by sad music, and memory was rated the most important route by which it evokes sadness. Rewards included imagination, emotion regulation, empathy and the absence of real-life consequences. Appreciation was higher in people high in empathy. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4203803)
- [E] Huron's prolactin hypothesis (sad music triggers consoling prolactin, "sham grief") has not held up: a test with 39 participants found no significant prolactin increase from sad music and no link between enjoyment and prolactin; Huron later described the theory as speculative. [Proposal](https://sonicfield.org/library/why-is-sad-music-pleasurable-a-possible-role-for-prolactin); [Test of the theory](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/enjoying-sad-music-a-test-of-the-prolactin-theory/)
- [E] Huron's ITPRA model (Sweet Anticipation, 2006) splits the response to any event into pre-outcome Imagination and Tension and post-outcome Prediction, Reaction and Appraisal responses; a correct prediction is itself rewarded. [Music and Letters review](https://academic.oup.com/ml/article/89/3/396/1135734)

**Nostalgia, memory and the return of a theme**
- [E] Barrett, Grimm, Robins, Wildschut, Sedikides and Janata (2010, Emotion 10(3), 390 to 403): songs evoked more nostalgia when tied to the listener's own life, when familiar, when arousing, and when they stirred positive, negative and mixed feelings together; nostalgia-prone people reacted more strongly. A 2024 replication with 582 US adults largely held, controlling for familiarity and musical features. [Paper PDF](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/%7Ecrsi/Barrett,%20grimm,%20Robins,%20Wildschut,%20Sedikides,%20&%20Janata,%202010,%20Emotion.pdf); [Replication atlas](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1037/a0019006/)
- [E] Boltz, Schulkind and Kantra (1991, Memory and Cognition 19, 593 to 606): when music accompanied a scene's outcome, mood-congruent music improved recall; when music foreshadowed the outcome, mood-incongruent music (an expectancy violation) improved recall. Scenes viewers could not recall were still recognized when their music cued them. [Haverford record](https://scholarship.haverford.edu/psychology_facpubs/260)

**Silence**
- [E] Margulis (2007, Music Perception 24(5), 485 to 506): in two experiments, listeners judged identical silences differently depending on context; silences after tonal closure were identified faster and felt less tense than silences after music without closure. [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/silences-in-music-are-musical-not-silent-an-exploratory-study-of-/); [University of Arkansas summary](https://news.uark.edu/articles/9745/exploring-the-sounds-of-silence)

### Inferences
- Two different peaks need two different musical treatments. For chills: a new voice entering, a crescendo with energy added in the 1 to 4 kHz band, widening register, a surprise harmony. For tears: slow, quiet, calm, sad, an appoggiatura that leans and resolves, a descending sequence, and time (tears build over seconds, not a single hit). The story has both kinds of moment, and they should not be scored the same way.
- "Being moved" is driven by a sudden rise in closeness. In this story the two closeness spikes are the kid's "home." (the bond answered) and the old you sending "home" to Mom (the bond answered across death, years late). These are where the music should spend its biggest cards; everything before should save them.
- The leitmotif works on memory and recognition (Barrett; Boltz), so it must be learned early and heard often enough to be recognized when it returns changed. Taruffi and Koelsch's finding that nostalgia is the main emotion of sad music suggests the score's best tool is the childhood timbre (the music box) coming back, not a new sad sound.
- Margulis predicts that cutting the music in mid-phrase leaves a tense silence and that ending on a closed cadence leaves a calm one. Use the first for Dad's unanswered "home", the second for "You sleep fine."
- Expectation (Huron, de Fleurian and Pearce) means a resolution is strongest when the listener has learned to expect it and been denied it. See the code observation below: the theme currently resolves to the tonic too early in the story.

#### Code observation: the theme already resolves too early
- THEME (score.js) is D E F# | E D B | A B D E | D (MIDI 74 76 78 | 76 74 71 | 69 71 74 76 | 74), one bar per chord from the start of each four-chord round.
- In `morning` and `afternoon` the fourth bar's long D lands on the A chord (pad E A B C#): a suspended, unresolved ending. In `noon` it lands on Bm (a deceptive, half-closed ending). Good: the phrase stays open.
- In `dawn` (chords Gs Ar Bmr Dr) the fourth bar lands on D over D: the phrase fully comes home in the middle of the story (hillDawn, linesSunrise), every two rounds. Then `home()` resolves it the same way (D Bm G D).
- Recommendation: reserve the fully closed form (melody D over a D chord with D in the bass) for the ending only. Reorder `dawn` to `['Dr', 'Gs', 'Ar', 'Bmr']` (bar four lands on Bm, deceptive) or play only three bars there. Then the first time the player ever hears the theme close is the end, which turns 25 minutes of open endings into a learned expectation that finally pays off.

#### Story-specific recommendations from the evidence
- Kid's "home." (script.js final wait, `{ them: 'home.', music: 'home' }`): chills route. Piano alone for bar one, strings entering at bar two or three (a new voice entering is the most reliable chill trigger, Grewe 2007), a gentle swell that adds brightness (open the pad filter toward 2 to 3 kHz rather than only raising volume, Nagel 2008), ending closed on D.
- The old you sends "home" to Mom (the last phone event, "Today 22:14"): tears route. Slow, soft, calm, solo music box, no strings, no swell; an appoggiatura on the last bar (start bar four on E over the D chord and let it fall to D after a beat or two). Let it take time: tears were twice as long as chills in Mori and Iwanaga.
- The deaths (Grandpa, Biscuit, Grandma, Mom): do not add new loud material. Thin the texture (see section 2, Up) and use a descending bass by fifths or steps (Sloboda's secondary tear factors).

### Gaps
- Huron's "contrastive valence" (that a fearful or negative anticipation followed by a benign outcome amplifies the positive feeling, the claimed basis of frisson and relief) is from Sweet Anticipation; no fetched source verified the exact wording, so treat it as unverified here.
- No study found measures tears to music inside an interactive game; all tear studies above use listening only.
- Sloboda's exact counts (how many passages per response type) were not verified in full text; a secondary summary says 38 passages.
- No numbers found on how many repetitions a leitmotif needs before recognition is reliable.

## 2. Composer craft: how themes carry a life story

### Takeaway
The pieces people cry at share a pattern: one simple theme, learned early in a specific instrument, varied by instrument, tempo and density as life changes, thinned to almost nothing at a death, and left to silence at the hardest moment. Character-instruments (Florence), a single theme as the DNA of all music (Journey), and restraint (Firewatch, The Last of Us) are the recurring craft choices.

### Cited Findings
- [C] Up, "Married Life" (Giacchino): director Pete Docter asked for a tune that feels like it comes from a grandmother's music box, after Giacchino's first attempt; it was the first cue written because the team expected it to be the hardest scene and to shape the rest of the film. The music slows at the doctor's office when the couple learns they cannot have children, nearly stops when Carl realizes the promise he never kept, and as Ellie dies instruments drop out of the harmony until only a piano remains; the music ends as the screen fades to black. Its themes return through the film with timbre changed to suit each context. An early comedic version of the sequence drew silence from test audiences. [Wikipedia: Up opening sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_opening_sequence)
- [C] Up soundtrack: the waltz theme is introduced spare and slow on "We're in the Club Now", fleshed out in "Married Life" with a violin statement, and developed further later; critics describe the score as built by thematic transformation. "Married Life" won the 2010 Grammy for Best Instrumental Composition. [Apple Music album notes](https://music.apple.com/album/316618105); [Wikipedia: Up (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_(soundtrack))
- [C] Journey (Austin Wintory): the theme "Nascence" was written on the first day, about three years before release, and its notes underpin nearly every note of music in the game, though that exact version never appears in-game; solo cello (Tina Guo) and bass flute recorded the first version the same day. Wintory says the cello was largely instinct and that the interactive score could not have been written in less time. [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=126775); [Berklee archive](https://archives.berklee.edu/_flysystem/fedora/2022-10/49730-Original%20File.pdf); [8dio interview](https://8dio.com/blogs/news/interview-with-austin-win)
- [C] Florence (Kevin Penkin): a chamber score with piano as Florence and cello as Krish (a cellist in the story); the main theme starts on piano and the cello joins as the romance begins. Penkin says flutes, clarinets, violins and electronics accompany the two lead instruments to give context to how the characters interact. The game has no dialogue, so the score carries much of the emotion. Track titles follow the relationship (First Dates, Fight, Let Go, Wake Up, Move On). [WSHU / NPR Music Respawn](https://www.wshu.org/culture/2018-06-21/kevin-penkins-intimate-conversational-soundtrack-for-florence); [Apple "Games Are Art"](https://apps.apple.com/story/id1371611263)
- [C] Firewatch (Chris Remo): music is triggered by game state; a piece can have many possible start points, each with conditions such as objectives done and time since the last conversation; the same piece never plays twice; motifs tied to characters return with different instrumentation as the story changes; the instinct throughout was to tamp the soundscape down (footsteps kept lowered so they would not become a percussive score). [Designing Sound interview](https://designingsound.org/2017/07/19/firewatch-solitude-and-companionship-through-minimalism-an-interview-with-chris-remo/)
- [C] The Last of Us (Gustavo Santaolalla): silence is a deliberate tool in the score; he keeps "human" imperfections such as fingers sliding on nylon strings. In his words, "Silence sometimes can be louder than the note." [NBC News / AP](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/last-us-composer-gustavo-santaolalla-silence-key-drawing-audience-rcna102364); [NME](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/the-last-of-us-soundtrack-composer-gustavo-santaolalla-interview-3756841)
- [C] To the Moon (Kan Gao): the piano piece "For River" exists inside the story (written by a man for his wife) and recurs through the game in various forms; the soundtrack has several versions of the main theme. [RPGFan soundtrack review](https://www.rpgfan.com/music-review/to-the-moon-original-soundtrack/)
- [C] Gris (Berlinist): the score (piano, strings, choir, ambient textures) is meant to work as one continuous piece with returning motifs; the band kept their first drafts and let the landscapes push their instrument choices. [gamemusic.net interview](https://gamemusic.net/?p=20097); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/1845704917)
- [C] Max Richter, "On the Nature of Daylight" (The Blue Notebooks, 2004; written in response to the 2003 Iraq war): a slow theme in low strings, a higher violin melody over a repeating bass line, and a gradual build over about six minutes; NPR describes audience members visibly moved at its Tiny Desk performance. Richter talks about music as a place to think. It opens the film Arrival. [NPR Tiny Desk](https://www.npr.org/2020/01/22/796801746/max-richter-tiny-desk-concert); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Nature_of_Daylight)

### Inferences
- The existing design (one "home" theme from music box to piano to full statement) matches the strongest precedent (Up's music box waltz, Journey's single theme, To the Moon's in-story piece). The changes worth making are about where the theme is withheld, thinned and completed, not about adding more themes.
- Up's death technique translates directly: at each death, remove layers from the running act in a fixed order (keys sprinkle, then pad, then bass) until one piano voice is left, then silence. Today `grief` is a separate act (Bms Gs Em Gs, 9 s per chord) that starts on a chord boundary; the thinning version would happen inside the current act at the moment of the line, which is closer to Up.
- Florence's character-instruments suggest a cleaner mapping for this story: music box = Mom (childhood, her lullaby), piano = you, a low cello-like string voice = Dad (enters in his scenes, absent after his death), strings together = the family at the end. Then the instrument that is missing in the last scenes tells the loss without words.
- Firewatch's "never the same piece twice" is already partly met by `sprinkle()` randomness; the risk is that loops of one act for several minutes become wallpaper. Keep acts short or let the density decay over time within an act.
- A "Daylight-style" act for the final porch wait (very slow chords, a stepwise descending bass, one high line entering late) would build the long, quiet tension that the kid's reply then releases. Treat this as craft taste, not evidence.

## 3. Sound design for emotion: small diegetic sounds, room tone, absence, UI sounds, ducking, haptics

### Takeaway
The craft consensus is restraint: sparse, location-specific ambience that rarely calls attention to itself, a few deliberately "chunky" object sounds, and silence used as a sound. For this story the biggest wins are object sounds the script already names but the game does not play (the kettle, the kitchen light), a shared sound signature that links the cold open to the reveal, and room tones that survive phone speakers.

### Cited Findings
- [C] Firewatch's ambience is layered and changes with location, time of day and wind, but rarely calls attention to itself; some areas are dense, some sparse; the first pass felt too "video gamey" and was reduced; certain foley (combination locks) was deliberately "chunky"; player foley was limited to footsteps, kept low. [Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2017/07/19/firewatch-solitude-and-companionship-through-minimalism-an-interview-with-chris-remo/)
- [C] Santaolalla treats silence as part of the music and keeps small physical noises of playing because imperfection feels human. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/last-us-composer-gustavo-santaolalla-silence-key-drawing-audience-rcna102364); [NME](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/the-last-of-us-soundtrack-composer-gustavo-santaolalla-interview-3756841)
- [E] Music placed with a scene's outcome helps memory when mood-congruent, and a cue can trigger recognition of a scene that cannot be recalled (Boltz et al. 1991). [Haverford](https://scholarship.haverford.edu/psychology_facpubs/260)
- [D] Ducking parameters from tool documentation (not a game-audio standard): a 6 to 12 dB dip is gentle, about 20 dB is a strong typical setting, 30 dB or more nearly removes the music; short attack (1 to 10 ms), slow release (about 0.5 to 2 s) to avoid pumping. [Vindral sidechain ducking docs](https://composer-docs.vindral.com/operators/operators-sidechain-ducking.html)
- [D] Haptics on iOS web: the Vibration API (`navigator.vibrate`) is not supported in Safari. A hack (a hidden `<input type="checkbox" switch>` whose label is clicked from script) produced a haptic tick from iOS Safari 18 (some sources say 17.4); WebKit filed it as an exploitable behavior and marked the bug fixed, and one tester site claims Apple patched it in iOS 26.5 (unverified). [WebKit bug 285120](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=285120); [ios-haptics package](https://npmjs.com/package/ios-haptics)

### Inferences
#### Code observations (sfx.js)
- The script says "You put the kettle on. You don't want tea." and "You turn off the kitchen light." and neither has a sound. These are exactly the small diegetic sounds that make an empty room feel inhabited and then empty.
- `PLACES.room` is noise low-passed at 110 Hz, `car` at 160 Hz, `fire` at 200 Hz. The `buzz()` uses a 75 Hz sine under a 150 Hz square. Inference (not sourced here, verify by ear on a real phone): small phone speakers reproduce very little below a few hundred hertz, so on the phone-first target these beds and the buzz's body are mostly inaudible, and the score's bass (MIDI 35 to 45, about 62 to 110 Hz) is carried only by the quiet octave-up triangle (`gain 0.04`).
- The cold open (`kitchenWait`, ambience `night`) and the reveal (`kitchenWaitMom`) replay the same phone steps; the sound is not designed to rhyme between them.
- The porch clock (`clock()`, 1 s, 1900/1500 Hz triangle) is the present-time signifier and works.

#### Recommendations
1. **Mom's kitchen signature (cold open and reveal).** Give `kitchenWait` and `kitchenWaitMom` an identical, distinctive bed: a slower wall clock (a lower "tock", for example a 700 to 900 Hz triangle blip plus a short band-passed noise click, alternating tick and tock every second), a fridge hum (a 100 or 120 Hz sine plus 200, 300, 400 Hz harmonics at falling levels, so it survives a phone speaker), and nothing else. Play it exactly the same both times. The player may not consciously remember the cold open, but the sound cue supports recognition (Boltz et al. 1991), so the reveal lands as "I have been here before" before the text says so.
2. **The kettle.** On "You put the kettle on.", start a kettle: a switch click, then a low rumble (low-passed noise, 150 to 400 Hz) that slowly turns into a rising hiss (band-pass sweeping from about 500 Hz to 3 kHz over 30 to 60 s). Let it heat under the final wait (Delivered, Read) and have it click off on its own at "Read" or at the typing that stops. Never let it whistle. A parent waiting is a kettle nobody wants.
3. **The kitchen light.** On "You turn off the kitchen light.", a wall-switch click (2 to 3 ms noise burst, high-passed at about 1.5 kHz, plus a 10 ms low thump) and, one beat later, the fridge hum stops. The absence of the hum is the sound of the room going to sleep; porchDark's crickets then come in, then porchDawn's birds.
4. **Room tones that survive the phone.** Add a quiet mid-band layer to `room`, `car` and `fire` (band-pass 250 to 700 Hz at a very low level) and give the buzz a body at 150 to 250 Hz plus a table rattle (short band-passed noise at 2 to 4 kHz, amplitude-modulated at the motor rate) so it reads as a phone on wood through a phone speaker.
5. **UI sounds for the two key messages.** For the kid's "home.", do not play the generic `pop()`; let the theme's first piano note (D5, 587 Hz) be the arrival sound, so message and music are one event. For the final send to Mom, replace `whoosh()` with the music box's first note. Everywhere else, keep the generic sounds quiet and short.
6. **Typing dots.** The other side's typing dots are silent in real life; keep them silent. The silence while the dots show and then stop (twice in the final wait, once in the cold open, once in the reveal) is the most "absent" sound in the story; let only the room tone and the clock fill it.
7. **Ducking.** This is a text game, so classic dialogue ducking is less needed. Two uses are worth it: duck the score by about 4 to 6 dB for about 1 s under the arrival of key messages (attack about 10 ms, release about 1 s), and duck the ambience, not the music, when the theme enters at the finale so the theme has the space.
8. **Haptics.** Use `navigator.vibrate([180, 120, 180])` with the buzz on platforms that support it (feature-detect). Do not rely on the iOS switch hack; on iPhone the sound of the buzz is the haptic, which is another reason it must be audible on the phone speaker.
9. **Recorded sound that would matter (optional).** The single most reliable chill trigger in the literature is a voice entering (Grewe 2007). If the author ever adds one recorded asset, the strongest candidate is a human humming the theme (a lullaby in a childhood scene, returning very quietly under the final send to Mom). The author could record it themselves, so no licence is needed. Everything else in this list can stay synthesized.

### Gaps

- No game-specific ducking standard (Wwise, FMOD, GDC) was found; the dB figures are from general sidechain documentation.
- No sourced research found specifically on the emotional effect of UI notification sounds in narrative games.

## 4. Adaptive and interactive music: resequencing, layering, and making the final reply land

### Takeaway
State-triggered, never-repeating music (Firewatch) and one theme underlying everything (Journey) are the proven patterns; the technical rule for an emotional sync point is to hold the music in a stable tension state for as long as the player or the story needs and to resolve exactly on the event. The current engine waits for chord boundaries (up to two chords, up to about 18 s in slow acts) and lets old chords ring for up to 13 s after a change, so its most important moments are not yet synced.

### Cited Findings
- [C] Firewatch: a music system checks game state as the player progresses; one piece can have many possible start points, each with its own conditions; some tracks are scored directly to specific conversations. [Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2017/07/19/firewatch-solitude-and-companionship-through-minimalism-an-interview-with-chris-remo/)
- [C] Journey: thatgamecompany gave Wintory detailed implementation notes for every piece, and the music was refined once mocked up in the game. [TheSixthAxis interview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2012/03/15/interview-journey-composer-austin-wintory/)
- [E] Silence after closure feels less tense than silence after non-closure (Margulis 2007). [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/silences-in-music-are-musical-not-silent-an-exploratory-study-of-/)
- [E] Expectation is one of three core chill mechanisms (de Fleurian and Pearce 2021); the cognitive side of openness (predicting how music unfolds) predicts frisson (Colver and El-Alayli 2016). [QMUL](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/75159); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/2016/06/getting-chills-great-music-connected-new-cognitive-dimension-personality-43577)
- [E] Huron's ITPRA: tension builds before an expected outcome; prediction, reaction and appraisal follow it. [Music and Letters review](https://academic.oup.com/ml/article/89/3/396/1135734)

### Inferences
#### Code observations (score.js)
- `schedule()` switches to a pending act only when `index % 2 === 0`, so a new act can start up to two chords after the cue: with `frame` and `grief` at 9 s per chord that is up to 18 s (plus the 1.5 s lookahead). A death line can be read and tapped past before the grief music arrives.
- Each `chord()` sounds for `sec` plus a 4.2 s pad release (bass 3.4 s). Changing act, calling `silence`, or calling `home()` does not stop chords already scheduled; only `master` gain moves. So when "home." arrives, the frame chord already playing (D, G, Bm or A) keeps sounding for up to about 13 s under the new D chord, which blurs the resolution.
- `home()` sets `finale = true`, ramps `master` to 0 after about 25 s, and ignores every later cue. The reveal and the final send to Mom therefore happen with no score at all; there is currently no way to cue music for the true last beat.
- `silence` ramps `master` to 0 over 1.6 s. Because the reverb return goes into `master`, the room's tail is cut with it, so it sounds like a volume knob rather than musicians stopping.
- Phone steps can already carry `music` (phone.js: "Any step may carry `music`"), so cues can be synced to "Delivered", "Read", a typed word, or an erase without new plumbing.

#### Recommendations
1. **Per-act buses and urgent cues.** Route each act's chords and notes into their own gain node (created in `play()`), so a cue can fade the old act in 0.3 to 2 s instead of waiting for its tails. Add an `urgent` flag (or a separate cue name) that switches on the next beat instead of the next even chord, for deaths, Dad's cut, and the finale. Keep the chord-boundary switch for ordinary scene changes, where it sounds musical.
2. **A hold state before the reply.** Add a `wait` act: one chord, held indefinitely and never advancing, on an A suspended chord (bass A, pad D E A B, so the theme's last note D is "in the air"), with the pad filter breathing slowly (an LFO of 0.05 to 0.1 Hz on cutoff) and no sprinkles. Cue it on `{ status: 'Read' }` in the final wait. At the first typing dots, thin it to a single high D and the bass A. When the typing stops, let it hang. On `{ them: 'home.' }`, the resolution lands on the event: bass to D, pad to D major, and the theme's first note on the same audio-clock time as the message bubble. Because the hold has no bar grid, any wait length resolves on the beat that matters. This is the "suspended chord resolving exactly on the reply" the brief asks for.
3. **Schedule the resolution from the same timestamp as the bubble.** In `home()`, `t0 = ctx.currentTime + 0.3` delays the music a third of a second after the bubble. Better: have the phone step call the score first, get back the exact audio time, and render the bubble at that time (or use a smaller offset, 0.05 to 0.1 s, matched to the bubble's animation peak).
4. **Dad's unanswered "home": cut on "Delivered", mid-phrase.** Instead of `music: 'silence'` when the phone opens (`at: 58`), let the afternoon act play the theme's first bar as the player sends "home" by hand, and put a new `cut` cue on `{ status: 'Delivered' }`: stop scheduling, fade the dry buses in about 0.15 to 0.3 s, and let the reverb return ring out on its own (send the reverb return to the output, not through `master`, or leave `reverbIn` open). By Margulis, a silence after a non-closed phrase is a tense one; the music itself goes unanswered. Then the 4.6 s wait in silence plus room tone.
5. **Lonely night, typed and erased (cityNight, 01:06).** Sync the `lonely` act's unfinished first bar to the player's own typing: bar one starts when the player begins typing "home" (`byHand`), and the `{ erase: true }` step fades the bar out mid-note. The phrase left unfinished is then literally the text left unsent.
6. **The last send to Mom (new `mom` cue, after `finale`).** Allow one cue after the finale. On the player's tap of "home" in Mom's chat: the music box alone plays bar one exactly as on the lonely night, then, for the first time, bars two to four, slower than any earlier statement (about a 4 to 5 s bar), ending with an appoggiatura (E over D, falling to D). No chords, or one very soft D pad entering only on the last note. Then "Delivered", and silence before "You turn off the kitchen light." Completion of the lonely night's phrase on Mom's instrument is the musical version of the story's last move.
7. **Two closures, two mechanisms.** Proposed split: the kid's "home." gets the warm, chill-type closure (piano, strings entering, a modest swell), and Mom's "home" gets the quiet, tear-type closure (music box solo, slow, falling appoggiatura). An alternative, if testing shows two closures within about 90 s feel repetitive: let the kid's reply stop at bar three over the A suspended chord and complete the phrase only at Mom. Test both with players.
8. **Reveal in silence.** After the kid's theme ends on a closed cadence (a calm silence, Margulis), play the reveal with no music and the Mom's-kitchen signature from section 3. The silence here is part of the score.
9. **Vertical layering for the end lines.** In `end` (old age, music box, no theme), drop one layer per decade of the life lines (keys, then bass), so by the porch the texture is nearly bare and the finale's entries (piano, then strings) are heard as new.

#### Priority order across all questions (proposal)
1. Fix sync: per-act buses, urgent cues, and a held `wait` state that resolves exactly on "home." (section 4, items 1 to 3).
2. Add the `mom` cue after the finale: music box completing the lonely night's first bar on the final send (section 4, item 6).
3. Reserve the theme's full closure for the ending (reorder `dawn`) (section 1).
4. Dad's cut on "Delivered", mid-phrase, reverb ringing (section 4, item 4).
5. Kettle, light switch, fridge hum, and the shared cold-open/reveal signature (section 3).
6. Limiter and gain staging so the finale has headroom (section 5).
7. Phone-speaker-safe room tones and buzz (section 3).
8. Up-style thinning at deaths, Dad's string voice (section 2).
9. Synthesis upgrades for piano and music box warmth (section 5).

### Gaps
- No public postmortem was found that measures player emotion against adaptive versus linear music in narrative games.
- No sourced timing data on how precisely a musical resolution must align with a visual event to feel synchronous; the 0.05 to 0.1 s figure above is an engineering guess.

## 5. Practical Web Audio: warm synthesis, autoplay, the iOS mute switch, loudness and limiting

### Takeaway
Audio must start from a user gesture; on iPhone, Web Audio is silenced by the ring switch unless `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` is set, which `main.js` already does. The bigger audible issue is the master chain: a compressor at -12 dB threshold, ratio 4, default 30 dB soft knee squeezes exactly the loudness rises that predict chills; a ceiling-only limiter and deliberate gain staging around a mobile-style loudness target would give the finale room.

### Cited Findings
- [D] Web Audio contexts are not allowed to start playing automatically; create or resume the context from inside a user gesture (normally a click); a context created outside a gesture starts `suspended`; provide user control over sound (play/stop, volume, mute). [MDN Web Audio best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices)
- [D] Starting a source outside a user input handler is subject to autoplay rules; Firefox's default blocks Web Audio until sticky activation; `navigator.getAutoplayPolicy('audiocontext')` reports `allowed`, `allowed-muted` or `disallowed`, and the policy can change after interaction without an event. [MDN Autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay)
- [D] On iOS, Web Audio output is silenced by the hardware mute switch while audio and video elements are not; the default audio session type is "ambient"; setting `navigator.audioSession.type` to `playback` (or `play-and-record`) lets Web Audio play with the switch on mute; the Audio Session API is not a W3C Recommendation; on iOS 18 the `transient` types played with no sound and were marked invalid. [Web audio iOS unmute write-up](https://portfolio-2024-b6r.pages.dev/blog/web-audio-ios-unmute); [adactio on audioSession](https://adactio.com/links/tags/audiosession); [WebKit bug 237322](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322); [WebKit bug 264473](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=264473)
- [D] Loudness: Sony's ASWG-R001 recommended -18 LUFS integrated for PS Vita (handheld), max true peak -1 dBTP, max loudness range 20 LU, against -23 LUFS for PS3/PS4; an ITU-R report cites typical portable game loudness of about -18 LKFS (plus or minus 2) against -24 for consoles; ASWG measures at least 30 minutes of representative play, full mix. These are 2012 to 2018 figures; no current official mobile web figure was found. [Garry Taylor, Sony, slides](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/loudness-and-how-to-measure-it-ppt-presentation-886969); [ITU-R BS.2434](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BS.2434-2018-PDF-E.pdf); [Designing Sound: loudness in game audio](https://designingsound.org/2013/02/loudness-in-game-audio/)
- [C] One practitioner proposes mixing quiet content around -24 LUFS and using runtime compression to lift output toward -18 LUFS (or -16 in noisy places) on mobile. [Audio Media International](https://audiomediainternational.com/mobile-loudness-an-adaptive-approach/)
- [E] Chill passages show loudness rises concentrated in 920 to 4400 Hz (Nagel et al. 2008) and widening frequency range (Guhn et al. 2007). [Sonicfield](https://sonicfield.org/library/psychoacoustical-correlates-of-musically-induced-chills); [Sonicfield](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/physiological-and-musico-acoustic-correlates-of-the-chill-response)

### Inferences
#### Code observations (main.js, score.js)
- `main.js` sets `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` before creating the context and creates it with `latencyHint: 'playback'`: correct for the mute switch. Inference to verify on device: a `playback` session will likely interrupt the player's own music app, which is acceptable for a scored story but worth a line in the sound toggle.
- The "limiter" is a `DynamicsCompressorNode` with threshold -12 dB and ratio 4; knee, attack and release are left at the defaults (knee 30 dB, attack 0.003 s, release 0.25 s, from the Web Audio spec, verify). A ratio of 4 with a 30 dB soft knee is a bus compressor, not a limiter: it reduces the difference between quiet and loud passages, exactly the contrast that chill triggers rely on. The spec's compressor algorithm also applies automatic makeup gain (to verify in the W3C spec), which raises quiet passages further.
- Recommendation: make the node a ceiling only (threshold about -3 dB, knee 0, ratio 20, attack 0.002 s, release 0.1 to 0.2 s), and shape dynamics by design: keep everyday acts around 6 to 10 dB below the finale peak, so the strings' entry at "home." is a real swell. Measure the full mix on a 30-minute playthrough recording with any LUFS meter and aim for an integrated level in the -18 to -16 LUFS range for phones, with the quietest scenes (reveal, lonely night) allowed to sit well below.
- On iOS a phone call or Siri can move the context to an `interrupted` state (verify); listen for `statechange` and resume on the next tap.

#### Warmer synthesis, implementable without audio files (engineering judgement, not sourced)
- **Piano (`VOICES.piano`).** Today: four partials with fixed decays, one fixed 2.6 kHz low-pass. Warmer: (a) velocity-dependent brightness (cutoff from about 1.2 kHz at soft to 4 kHz at loud, and the cutoff falling over the note's life), (b) two-stage decay per partial (a fast drop in the first 0.2 to 0.4 s, then a long tail), (c) slight inharmonic stretch of upper partials (multiply partial n by sqrt(1 + B n squared) with B around 0.0002 to 0.0005), (d) two or three unison oscillators per partial detuned by 1 to 3 cents (piano strings in a unison beat slowly), (e) a 3 to 8 ms band-passed noise "hammer" at the attack, (f) longer decay for lower notes. A tiny sustain pedal resonance (send to a short, bright reverb) helps the slow finale.
- **Music box (`VOICES.box`).** Keep it high, short and slightly inharmonic. Add a very soft mechanical click before each note (1 to 2 ms high-passed noise) and, for the final `mom` cue, a slight slowing between notes (as if the spring is winding down: stretch each successive beat by 2 to 4 percent) so the last phrase sounds like the box running out, then resolving. The spring winding down is a tear-type gesture (slow, calming) and needs no words.
- **Plucks.** For Karplus-Strong style plucks, precompute the string into an `AudioBuffer` in JavaScript rather than using a `DelayNode` feedback loop: Web Audio requires at least one 128-frame render quantum of delay in any cycle (verify in the spec), which caps pitch for loop-based strings at a few hundred hertz.
- **Strings for the finale.** The current strings are the pad doubled an octave up. Warmer: per-voice slow vibrato (4.5 to 5.5 Hz, 5 to 10 cents) that fades in over the first second, slight independent detune drift per voice (a very slow LFO, 0.05 to 0.2 Hz), a band-pass "body" resonance around 400 to 800 Hz, and a slower attack (0.8 to 1.5 s) so they swell in. Bring them in at bar two or three of the theme, not at bar one, to use the "new voice entering" effect.
- **Memory colour for the reveal ("years ago").** A tape-like tint on the reveal scene only: wow and flutter as a 0.3 to 0.6 Hz LFO of 3 to 6 cents on detune of anything playing, a low-level hiss (high-passed noise far under the room tone), a low-pass at 5 to 7 kHz, and a gentle `WaveShaperNode` tanh curve. Apply the same tint to the cold open so they match.
- **Reverb (`impulse()`).** The generated impulse uses a polynomial decay on filtered noise. More natural: an exponential decay (amplitude proportional to exp(-6.9 t / RT60)), 15 to 30 ms of pre-delay, and a high-frequency roll-off that increases over the tail (darker as it decays). Use two impulses: a small room (RT60 0.4 to 0.7 s) for diegetic sounds in kitchens (kettle, clock, switch, fridge) and the existing larger space for the score. Keeping the room sound "in the room" and the music "around the room" separates the world from the memory.
- **Mobile performance.** Each chord is 8 to 16 detuned sawtooths plus two bass oscillators, overlapping the next chord for about 4 s; with sprinkles and the theme this is a few dozen oscillators, which modern phones handle, but test on an older iPhone and Android with the screen dimmed. If needed, render the pad of the `wait` hold state once into a looping buffer.
