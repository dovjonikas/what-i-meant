# Emotional design in story-driven games: lessons from the best narrative games

Scope note: researched 2026-10-29 with WebSearch and WebFetch only. Some pages could not be fetched (PC Gamer making-of returned an empty shell, Giant Bomb returned 403). Where a quote reached me only through a search-result summary and not the full page, it is marked "(snippet)". Quotes are kept short; most material is paraphrased. Lens throughout: a solo creator making 5 to 30 minute personal stories for the phone web.

## Q1. How do games make the controls and the way of playing carry the emotion (the mechanic is the metaphor)?

### Takeaway
The strongest examples choose one small, physical verb that enacts the feeling (a puzzle that needs fewer pieces as two people get comfortable, a blink you cannot hold back, a button that belonged to a dead brother, a juice box that does nothing), teach it through repetition, then change, revisit or break it at the emotional peak. Designers say outright that they chose mechanics for feeling rather than depth or challenge, and several built the mechanic first and only then asked whose story it was.

### Cited Findings

**Florence (Mountains, Ken Wong, 2018, mobile)**
- Wong's stated goal was emotional impact; traditional values such as challenge, agency and long sessions did not guide the design. His rule: "Use whatever mechanics feel right for the story." He admits none of the mechanics are "deep". ([Game Developer, GDC 2019 write-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-))
- Many small mechanics act as metaphors for the player's emotional state; Wong says touch screens were especially useful for this, and that revisiting a mechanic later is a way to show characters changing. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-))
- The conversation mechanic: speech bubbles are jigsaws. Wong's seed question was whether her speaking could be "a puzzle, of sorts, and it gets easier over time". On early dates the bubbles are broken into many pieces; as the couple grows close the pieces get fewer; in arguments you only assemble Florence's bubbles, the shapes turn jagged, and it becomes a race against Krish. ([GameIndustry.com interview](https://www.gameindustry.com/interviews/ken-wong-florence-love-story-mobile/); [Milanote breakdown](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence): "the puzzles become easier and easier")
- An earlier argument concept (one puzzle where a character is hidden until pieces are rearranged) was dropped because it would not capture what an argument feels like; a visual-only idea failed because "it was only visual and not interactive". ([Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence))
- Each chapter became "a container that could contain many mechanics" rather than one mechanic per chapter. The game is 20 short chapters, mostly everyday routines. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-); [Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence))

**What Remains of Edith Finch (Giant Sparrow, Ian Dallas, 2017)**
- The Lewis (cannery) chapter: one stick chops fish heads on the line while the other steers Lewis's imagined self through a fantasy world; the two tasks run at once. ([Game Developer, "30 different control schemes"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-))
- Design order at Giant Sparrow: find a mechanic players can explore first, then decide "who this event would occur to". The hard part was a mechanic that was explorable and also had a story in it. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-))
- No reward feedback for doing it well: "There's no meter or explosions when you do it perfectly." ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-))
- Dallas called the Lewis story the game's "capstone"; it nearly got cut because of the ship date and its complexity, and the chopping was "fiddly and annoying" until a new programmer rewrote it. ([PC Gamer making-of](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-making-of-what-remains-of-edith-finch/), snippet)
- The target tone was the sublime: "something very beautiful but also a little unsettling"; Dallas says story there "is more about setting the mood than it is the main event". ([Digital Trends interview](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/what-remains-of-edith-finch-interview/))

**Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons (Starbreeze, Josef Fares, 2013)**
- One controller, one stick and trigger per brother. After the older brother Naia dies, the younger Naiee, who is afraid of water, must cross a river alone; his own button does not make him swim. The player has to press the dead brother's button; the camera pulls in and the controller vibrates. ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-emotional-story-bond-success/); [Medium, Bootcamp](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/understanding-emotion-design-in-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-babb3029f39d))
- Fares says he knew from day one both the control scheme and how the game would end ([Giant Bomb, "A Tale of One Game"](https://www.giantbomb.com/articles/a-tale-of-one-game/1100-4736/), snippet; page returned 403); the ending was "the first thing I came up with" and he calls people crying at it a huge accomplishment ([Game Informer, Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons.aspx), snippet).
- Player split: one Steam user says the vibration guides you into the move, another complains the controls change without notice. ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/225080/discussions/0/846964364045398435/))

**Before Your Eyes (GoodbyeWorld, 2021)**
- Blinking (read by webcam) moves the story forward. Designers: you try to hold on to moments "but inevitably you're going to have to blink". The eye strain is intended; the pain pushes you to leave the scene while your body fights it. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes))
- Founder Will Hellwarth: lead designer Bela Messex realised early that blink tracking is less a mechanic than "a new form of controller". ([GamesPew](https://www.gamespew.com/2021/04/before-your-eyes-interview/)) Messex set up a visual language separating blinks that cut a scene from blinks that do not. ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/when-we-made-before-your-eyes/))
- Even the early prototype made people cry. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes))

**That Dragon, Cancer (Numinous Games, Ryan and Amy Green, 2016)**
- In the hospital night scene you try to soothe the crying baby Joel (bouncing him, offering juice) and nothing works, as in life. ([NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/463224149); [Toronto Star via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/toronto-star/20160116/282793535409081))
- Ryan Green's stated aim: to show "what it feels like to be helpless, but to have received grace" (snippet; surfaced with the NPR and Toronto Star coverage, not verified in the full text). A player writes that the frustration was something they were meant to feel. ([Epilogue Gaming](https://epiloguegaming.com/why-i-almost-stopped-playing-that-dragon-cancer/))

**Journey (thatgamecompany, Jenova Chen, 2012)**
- Chen removed competition, power and identity so online strangers would bond: no weapons, only jump and chirp; no HUD, no lobby, no names until the credits. He wanted interaction to be an "exchange of emotions or feelings, rather than exchange of blows". Items that worked for only one player created competition, so they were made to respawn for the second player. Chen accepted this may have cost virality. ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/))
- Chen: "If you put two people in an emotional struggle together, a bond will be formed." (snippet, attributed to [CGMagazine interview](https://www.cgmagonline.com/interviews/journeys-interview-with-jenova-chen/))
- Origin: loneliness in World of Warcraft despite meeting many players. ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/journey-inspired-by-world-of-warcraft-loneliness/))

**Gris (Nomada Studio, 2018)**
- No deaths, no game-over screens, no dialogue or text; the aim was that non-players could follow a girl through grief. ([indiegame.com](https://indiegame.com/en/archives/22258); [Red Bull preview](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/gris-preview-interview))
- The world starts monochrome and regains colour as she moves through it, structured on the five stages of grief. ([Medium critique](https://medium.com/@shanemarkmoosa/a-measured-critique-of-gris-d5eec19ef527); [indiegame.com](https://indiegame.com/en/archives/22258)) Caveat: the claims that about 70% of the team came from outside games and that a psychologist advised on the colour stages appear in only one source (indiegame.com).

**Celeste (Maddy Thorson, 2018)**
- The climb externalises anxiety and depression; Madeline's self-doubt becomes a shadow double (Badeline) who is the main antagonist; in a stalled gondola Theo talks her through a panic attack by asking her to keep a feather floating, a breathing minigame. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)); [The Clarion](https://www.theonlineclarion.com/arts-culture/2023/04/19/celeste-acknowledges-anxiety-introduces-ways-of-approaching-it/))
- Assist Mode (slower speed, infinite air dashes, invincibility) is framed by the game as not cheating. ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/my-biggest-revelations-of-2018-came-from-an-indie-video-game/); [The Clarion](https://www.theonlineclarion.com/arts-culture/2023/04/19/celeste-acknowledges-anxiety-introduces-ways-of-approaching-it/))

**Unpacking (Witch Beam, Wren Brier and Tim Dawson, 2021)**
- Handling each object forces the player to consider it; the team calls this "empathy through detective work". Familiar objects let players relate, and withholding definite answers lets them form theories. Rule: "No item? No trait!" ([Game Developer, GDC 2022 write-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items))
- In the boyfriend's apartment there is almost no room for her prized things; the diploma ends up over the toilet and later under the bed, a red flag shown without any dialogue or cutscene. Their summary: "item progression is character progression". ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items))
- Brier's GDC 2023 talk is about subtractive design: strengthening the core by removing anything not serving the core ideas. ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/19398-unpacking-dev-discusses-subtractive-design-in-gdc-talk))

**Papers, Please (Lucas Pope, 2013)**
- The job itself (checking documents fast, paid by accuracy) is set against the family's survival, so every rule-versus-mercy choice is a felt cost. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2013/11/10/244413539/papers-please-puts-your-loyalty-sympathy-to-the-test)) Pope wanted empathy for the inspector's difficult job ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)) and built from one constraint: play an immigration officer checking documents ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/lucas-pope-on-making-games-out-of-restrictions)).
- Pope notes that if you do not think of the officer's family as real, it is easy to let them waste away; the cost lives in the player's imagination. ([GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/features/lucas-pope-papers-please-return-of-the-obra-dinn-and-no-sequels-20220523))

**Venba (Visai Games, Abhi Swaminathan, 2023)**
- The mother's recipe book is damaged; ink splashes hide steps and the player deduces what is missing, so cultural loss between generations is enacted as a puzzle. Full South Asian recipes made poor puzzles; some recipes turned out to have puzzles already built in and only needed context. No failure state. ([MobileSyrup 2021](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/03/08/abhi-developer-venba-indian-cooking-game-interview/); [AnimationXpress, IGDC 2024 postmortem](https://animationxpress.com/games/visai-games-creative-director-breaks-down-the-success-of-their-indian-cooking-game-venba/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-venba-uses-food-to-explore-connections-and-disconnections-with-culture-for-immigrants))

**Spiritfarer (Thunder Lotus, Nicolas Guérin, 2020)**
- The hug became central to development and reinforced "mechanical ways to take care of others". The Everdoor, where spirits leave, was meant to be "neither fear-inducing nor too whimsical". ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-))
- The player decides when to take a spirit to the Everdoor; they cannot go without you (secondary analysis: [Cultured Vultures](https://culturedvultures.com/spiritfarer-death-everdoor/)).

**Firewatch (Campo Santo, Sean Vanaman, 2016)**
- The opening is a short text adventure (Twine) about Henry's marriage. Vanaman needed to put Henry's backstory into every player in the first five to ten minutes, and the text format meant "I never had to tell you who he was". ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2016/02/the-video-game-firewatch-and-its-origins-in-1980s-text-adventures.html)) It began as an internal stand-in for character documents. ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/features/firewatchs-henry-and-tutorialising-narrative/))

**Kentucky Route Zero, Act III (Cardboard Computer, 2014)**
- During Junebug's song "Too Late to Love You" the player picks one of three lines for each verse; every combination stays a song about a lover who left. Composer Ben Babbitt: the point was making it participatory "rather than making you just sit and watch". ([KRZ wiki](https://kentucky-route-zero.fandom.com/wiki/Too_Late_to_Love_You); [FACT](https://www.factmag.com/2016/08/23/kentucky-route-zero-ben-babbitt/))

**Outer Wilds (Mobius, Alex Beachum, 2019)**
- Progress is gated only by knowledge (a player who knows everything could finish quickly); each mystery's clues are split across planets. The ending shifts the goal from stopping the universe's end to understanding it. ([Medium interview](https://medium.com/@cordialkobold/interview-with-alex-beachum-creative-director-of-outer-wilds-a01bb9631e20); [USC thesis](https://digitallibrary.usc.edu/archive/Outer-Wilds--a-game-of-curiosity-driven-space-exploration-2A3BF163YX5A.html))

**Undertale (Toby Fox, 2015)**
- The non-violent option was hard because violence has many established ways to feel fun, while making mercy a real option is "way more complex". ([The Mary Sue interview](https://www.themarysue.com/interview-undertale-game-creator-toby-fox/))

**Theory**
- Katherine Isbister (How Games Move Us, MIT Press 2016) names choice and flow as what sets games apart emotionally, and covers avatars, NPCs and physical movement as tools for social emotion; Journey is one of her examples. ([MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/3115/How-Games-Move-UsEmotion-by-Design); [York University summary](http://www.yorku.ca/caitlin/futurecinemas/?p=3916))

### Inferences
- Recurring recipe: (1) one verb that physically resembles the feeling; (2) repeat it until it is automatic; (3) at the peak, change what the verb means or take it away (Florence's shrinking puzzles, Naiee using Naia's button, the blink you cannot stop). The peak only works because the verb was taught earlier.
- Deliberate futility is one of the strongest tools: an input that cannot fix things (juice box, Naiee's own button, Papers' rules vs family) produces helplessness more directly than any cutscene.
- Restraint about reward matters: Edith Finch gives no meter, Gris no death, Venba no failure, Unpacking no score. Scoring tells the player the moment is a test, not a feeling.
- Phone-first, solo-sized translations (my inference): drag-to-assemble (Florence), hold-to-keep and release-to-lose (a touch analogue of the blink), objects placed in a single room (Unpacking at 15 to 30 objects), a short text prologue the reader co-authors (Firewatch), picking a lyric line per verse (KRZ), filling in a damaged recipe or letter (Venba). All need only touch, text, simple 2D and one music track.
- Needs a big team or long tuning: Journey's anonymous online pairing (three years for two hours), Edith Finch's many bespoke control schemes, Spiritfarer's hand-animated hugs, Before Your Eyes' webcam blink detection (years of tuning across cameras and lighting), Outer Wilds' simulated solar system, Brothers' dual-character 3D.

### Gaps
- Journey's specific failed multiplayer experiments (I recall reports that testers pushed each other around and that collision was removed) were not confirmed in any fetched source; the GDC 2013 talk itself is on the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/GDC2013Chen) but was not reviewed.
- No primary Maddy Thorson quote on Celeste's design was found. The 2018 [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692) piece uses her earlier name (it predates her public name change; background knowledge, not verified here).
- No direct Jake Elliott statement on the KRZ song, and no Arnt Jensen explanation of Inside's ending (he prefers players to interpret: [Looper](https://www.looper.com/313060/the-secret-ending-in-inside-that-you-probably-didnt-see/)).
- A blog claims Ian Dallas used the cannery scene in a 2018 GDC talk ([Mothership](https://www.mothership.blog/5-games-that-will-change-your-perspective-on-life/)); not confirmed.

## Q2. What do music, sound, silence, pacing, restraint, small details and endings do for the emotional peak? What makes an ending land?

### Takeaway
Music is often built first and carries most of the mood when visuals are simple; near-silence makes small sounds loud; restraint means cutting whatever does not serve the core feeling and leaving gaps the player fills. Endings that land usually close the loop opened at the start, refuse a tidy reunion, and hand the final action to the player instead of showing it.

### Cited Findings
- Jenova Chen starts prototyping with music because it is "the most effective and powerful medium that can create emotion". ([Game Developer, GDC 2013 video page](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-))
- Kan Gao (To the Moon) says the atmosphere relies almost entirely on the music because the visuals are simple, and composing during writing breaks made the dialogue easier to write. ([YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)) The score re-voices one theme, "For River", in several arrangements tied to characters and the ending. ([Albumpedia track list](https://albumpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Kan_Gao:To_the_Moon))
- Spiritfarer's score underlines the relaxing loop subtly most of the time and turns "sweeping and epic" at other moments. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-))
- Before Your Eyes uses layered music that responds beat by beat to player action (Wwise), so a blink changes the soundtrack too. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes))
- Martin Stig Andersen (Limbo, Inside): Limbo's frequent near-silence and loose sync between sound and image were borrowed from old film soundtracks; to make the world feel silent, the boy's own sounds were kept relatively loud. ([Audiokinetic interview](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/interview-with-martin-stig-andersen-composer-and-sound-designer-on-playdeads-limbo/)) His GDC 2016 Inside talk argues that without feedback from sound back into the game, the game is like a dancer who cannot hear the music. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-the-audio-for-i-inside-i-a-game-that-listens)) Inside's score was played through a human skull as a filter. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2016-10-10-inside-game-soundtrack-human-skull.html))
- Florence has no dialogue; composition, colour and music carry it. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-)) Ending: an early version with a chance reunion decades later was dropped; "This part of Florence's life needed to end as it began", by herself. Wong: no comebacks, no second chances, "You don't always have to have that traditional happy ending." ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-); [Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence))
- Edith Finch pacing: Dallas eases the player into each bedroom story and out again with relative calm, and describes the tone as a "joyful inevitability" carried largely by music and art (snippet, attributed to the [GodIsAGeek interview](https://godisageek.com/2017/04/interview-with-ian-dallas-creative-director-on-what-remains-of-edith-finch/)). One story is told only on the last page of a divorce contract; "There wasn't any benefit from going into the details." ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/what-remains-of-edith-finch-interview/))
- Journey's climax was built by contrasting struggle with a sudden sense of freedom (snippet, podcast summary: [The Fourth Curtain](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/journeys-jenova-chen-and-the-art-of-the-positive-game/id1671350556?i=1000700059004)).
- Brothers' ending reuses the control scheme taught over the whole game (see Q1); the press itself is the climax. ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-emotional-story-bond-success/))
- Outer Wilds' finale is a discovery, not a victory: understanding what the ancient device does and why the universe ends; Beachum's answer to mortality is that exploring and connecting is still worth it. ([USC thesis](https://digitallibrary.usc.edu/archive/Outer-Wilds--a-game-of-curiosity-driven-space-exploration-2A3BF163YX5A.html); [Medium](https://medium.com/@claire.boeck.172/interview-with-alex-beachum-ba7e21ee5d17))
- Small reactive details: in Firewatch, if teenagers call Henry a creep, he later uses the same word about the stranger when talking to Delilah. Vanaman says the system picks "the truest and most specific thing" that can happen next, based on what you said and did. ([GDC Game Narrative Review PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/GameNarrativeReview/Aimee%20Zhang_Firewatch%20GDC%20Game%20Narrative%20Review.pdf); [Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2016/02/the-video-game-firewatch-and-its-origins-in-1980s-text-adventures.html))
- Unpacking: empty rooms read as "fresh start, renewal, independence"; events between moves are left unshown and players must accept ambiguity. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items))
- Journey's restraint went as far as hiding partner names until the credits, which turns the end credits into a small reveal. ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/))

### Inferences
- A single recurring melody, re-arranged for the peak and the ending (To the Moon), is the cheapest high-impact tool for a solo creator; Web Audio layering keyed to taps can imitate Before Your Eyes' reactive score at small scale.
- Silence is a budget item: quiet stretches make one sound (a door, a breath, a phone buzz) land. Keep the player's own sounds a little louder than the world (Andersen's Limbo rule).
- Endings that land, as a checklist drawn from these cases: return to the opening image or verb; give the last action to the player; refuse the reunion or the explanation if it would be dishonest (Florence, Inside); stop soon after the peak.
- Phone note (my technical inference, not from the sources): Brothers-style rumble can be approximated with the Vibration API on Android browsers, but iOS Safari does not support it, so the peak must not depend on haptics.

### Gaps
- The silence techniques in Andersen's Inside GDC talk were not available in text; only the Limbo-era interview speaks to silence directly.
- No designer-sourced material on Inside's or Celeste's endings was found.

## Q3. How short can a moving story be, and what do the best short pieces (5 to 30 minutes) do differently?

### Takeaway
Five minutes is enough: Passage (2007) holds a whole life in five minutes and is in MoMA's collection. The short pieces that work commit to one metaphor, use rules as the poem, avoid failure states and skill gates, and end before the idea wears thin. Short is not the same as cheap: Journey took three years for two hours.

### Cited Findings
- Passage (Jason Rohrer, 2007): an entire life in five minutes; you start young at the left edge, age as you move and die. Rohrer frames it as memento mori with rules as poetic metaphors; there is one life only; travelling with a spouse earns more points but limits where you can go, and you are slowed by grief when they die. Inspired by an elderly neighbour's death and starting a family. Rohrer: "I guess it made a lot of people cry." Acquired by MoMA in 2012. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passage_(video_game)); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/145533); [NEORT](https://x.com/neort_io/status/2092830783954420101); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/lifestyle/names/2016/02/10/jason-rohrer-video-games-might-make-you-cry/4zaZOHTrgKgfDsQIbyEvBN/story.html))
- Dys4ia (Anna Anthropy, 2012): a solo browser (Flash) game covering about six months of hormone therapy as a string of WarioWare-style microgames, which she called a collage of experiences and frustrations; republished on itch.io in 2023. Anthropy later moved away from hard games because skill requirements limit who can reach the story. ([Wikipedia: Dys4ia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dys4ia); [Wikipedia: Anna Anthropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Anthropy))
- Florence: 20 short chapters; Wong favours simple games anyone can play and finish quickly. ([Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence))
- Before Your Eyes' designers drew the explicit lesson "keep the game short" because the mechanic is physically uncomfortable; they put each scene's key beat at its start because players may blink early, and asked "how to tell a coherent story even if you skip most of it". ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes))
- Firewatch's text prologue delivers a marriage's arc in five to ten minutes. ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2016/02/the-video-game-firewatch-and-its-origins-in-1980s-text-adventures.html))
- A Short Hike (Adam Robinson-Yu, solo): first release built within a four-month deadline using a flexible scope with stretch goals that scaled to the real pace of work; the talk praises working within limitations, which led to its pixelated 3D look. ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A); [Class Central talk summary](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-short-hike-postmortem-158044); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-a-postmortem-look-at-the-making-of-i-a-short-hike-i-))
- Journey: about three years for a roughly two-hour game. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-))
- Gris and Venba both removed fail states so non-players could reach the end. ([indiegame.com](https://indiegame.com/en/archives/22258); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/03/08/abhi-developer-venba-indian-cooking-game-interview/))
- Business note: Florence recouped its costs and a little more, and Wong warns that a premium-mobile business plan is "going to be tough". ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-))

### Inferences
- What short pieces do differently: one premise, one verb, one emotional turn; the rules are the metaphor (Passage's walking right equals ageing); no tutorial text because the verb is obvious; no fail state; front-load each beat so skimming players still get it (Before Your Eyes); end soon after the turn.
- A 5 to 30 minute phone story is within solo reach in the Dys4ia and Passage tradition; a free web link also sidesteps the premium-mobile problem Wong describes.
- Use A Short Hike's scope tactic: define the smallest complete story first, then stretch goals (extra scenes, music variants) that can be dropped without breaking the ending.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave Florence's exact playtime (commonly cited at well under an hour; not verified here).
- No data was found on completion or emotional-response rates for short web stories versus longer games.

## Q4. What do designers say goes wrong: melodrama, telling instead of showing, too much text, too much control taken away?

### Takeaway
The failures designers describe are mostly about focus: a mechanic too complex or too powerful steals attention from the feeling, a prototype looks right but the emotion does not come across, systems quietly reintroduce competition or reward that contradict the story, and control changes or cutscenes leave the player confused or watching. Direct designer quotes about "melodrama" and "too much text" were not found; the evidence is indirect.

### Cited Findings
- Florence: the team spent two or three weeks on a chapter prototype "only to find out the emotion of the story wouldn't come across", dropped 3D because it "wasn't adding anything to the storytelling", threw out code and art, and cut an ending that felt too personal to Wong. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-))
- Edith Finch: the cannery mechanic, tuned in isolation, overloaded players, so complexity was cut, while the team worried less to do would bore them. A rejected prototype (a sister breathing through anxiety on the grass, with a flipped screen) created "more of a disconnect" than the real experience. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-))
- Before Your Eyes: the blink mechanic was "in danger of taking away from the story" because it was too powerful; glasses, lighting and webcam variety broke detection. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes))
- Journey: single-player item pickups bred competition until changed; Chen says the simple final design took a long time to reach. ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/))
- Ludonarrative dissonance (Clint Hocking, 2007): BioShock's story asks for selflessness while its mechanics reward self-interest (empathy for the Little Sisters versus the power gained by harvesting them). Later scholarship pushes back on treating such dissonance as always a flaw. ([Looper](https://www.looper.com/295787/what-is-ludonarrative-dissonance/); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/ludonarrative-dissonance-meaning-video-games-origins-definition))
- Papers, Please shows the dependency risk: if the player does not imagine the family as real, the moral weight vanishes. ([GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/features/lucas-pope-papers-please-return-of-the-obra-dinn-and-no-sequels-20220523))
- Brothers: some players felt the control change at the climax came without notice. ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/225080/discussions/0/846964364045398435/))
- Firewatch: a GDC narrative review notes that whatever you choose in the prologue and conversations, the overall outcome is the same, a known trade-off of choice that colours rather than branches. ([GDC Game Narrative Review PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/GameNarrativeReview/Laks_GNR-James-Laks-Firewatch.pdf))
- Unpacking rejected branching narrative to avoid fear of missing out. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items))
- Cutscene critique (blog-level sources, lower rigor): cutting away from play reminds players they are watching; Half-Life 2's scripted scenes keep camera control even when movement is restricted; long cutscenes and quick-time events are common complaints. ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/death-to-cutscenes-reclaiming-player-agency-in-game-storytelling); [Algoryte](https://www.algoryte.com/blogs/when-cutscenes-enhance-or-disrupt-the-gaming-flow/))
- Night in the Woods' team aimed for a game about people who live with mental health issues, not about the issues, and kept the characters' conditions deliberately ambiguous (snippet; surfaced with the [Scott Benson interview on Medium](https://medium.com/@wsong/interview-scott-benson-on-night-in-the-woods-c6a7ed09367c) and [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/night-in-the-woods-treats-depression-like-a-part-of-lif-1797400607)).

### Inferences
- Anti-melodrama tactics visible across these games: depict ordinary routines rather than big speeches (Florence, Unpacking); let objects and documents imply the drama (Edith Finch's divorce contract, Unpacking's diploma); leave the cause unstated (Gris, Inside); centre people rather than the issue (Night in the Woods).
- "Too much control taken away" has a cheap fix at phone scale: keep one small input alive during a heavy moment (look, hold, breathe, place) instead of a non-interactive cut.
- Test early with real players for whether the emotion arrives, not whether the mechanic is fun (Florence's thrown-away prototype is the cautionary tale).

### Gaps
- No primary designer statement specifically on "melodrama", "too much text" or "telling instead of showing" was located in this pass; those lessons are inferred from the cases above.

## Q5. How do humour and lightness sit beside sadness in the best of them?

### Takeaway
The sad moments are earned by warmth first: designers make the player like the characters through jokes, cosiness and mundane routine, then spend that attachment at the peak. Lightness is the setup, not a distraction, and the best of them refuse to moralise about the grief.

### Cited Findings
- Toby Fox on his model, EarthBound: compelling because it is "funny and strange, but also sincere and emotional". Kill Screen describes his plan as befriending players before eviscerating them. ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/behind-humor-toby-foxs-undertale/)) His own rule: "I just write what's funny." ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/interview-undertale-game-creator-toby-fox/)) The intimidating king Asgore was made goofy after Fox thought of a friend; Toriel's fight was originally meant to end with a hug followed by a suplex. ([Wikipedia: Asgore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgore); [Wikipedia: Toriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toriel))
- Spiritfarer's brief was to treat death "in a cozy and wholesome way"; Guérin names the challenge as "balancing drama and lightheartedness". Thunder Lotus calls it a cozy management sim about death. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritfarer))
- Night in the Woods: Scott Benson describes the opening as setting up a colourful, funny surface before the game shows it has other things on its mind ([Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-infinite-fall-s-i-night-in-the-woods-i-), snippet); everyone on the team had experienced depression ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/night-in-the-woods-treats-depression-like-a-part-of-lif-1797400607)); co-writer Bethany Hockenberry pushed back on the assumption that her hometown was simply ruined ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/night-in-the-woods-is-an-affecting-portrait-of-a-rust-belt-community/)).
- Edith Finch: the tone is beautiful and unsettling at once, a joyful inevitability (see Q2), and Dallas declines to moralise: on whether a longer, less interesting life is better, "I don't think there's an answer to that". ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/what-remains-of-edith-finch-interview/))
- Florence builds its love story from everyday routines and small social moments rather than set pieces. ([Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence))
- Celeste pairs its anxiety story with a forgiving tone (Assist Mode framed as not cheating). ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/my-biggest-revelations-of-2018-came-from-an-indie-video-game/))
- Rohrer's Passage hides grief inside a cheerful scoring rule (more points together), so the loss is felt as a change in play rather than told. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passage_(video_game)))

### Inferences
- A practical ratio for short stories: spend most of the runtime on warmth, play and small jokes the player would remember, and a small fraction on the turn; the turn costs more the more the player enjoyed what came before.
- Humour drawn from real, specific habits (a friend's goofiness turned into Asgore, routines in Florence) feels sincere; generic jokes do not carry over into the sad beat.
- Do not resolve the grief with a lesson; the cited designers leave the question open.

### Gaps
- No primary Scott Benson or Bethany Hockenberry statement on humour as a writing technique was found; nor any Kan Gao statement on To the Moon's comic banter between the two doctors.
