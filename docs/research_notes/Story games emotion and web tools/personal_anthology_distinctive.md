# Personal and autobiographical games, anthology structures, and what makes a small web story game distinctive and found

Scope note: researched 2026-10-29 with WebSearch and WebFetch only. Context for the inferences: one creator, writing in English, stories from his own thoughts, played in the browser on phones first, hosted free on GitHub Pages from a public repo. Several primary sources (GDC Vault talks, some magazine pages) were gated or truncated; where a claim comes from a search summary rather than a page I read in full, that is said. Dashes are written as plain hyphens on purpose.

## 1. Autobiographical and personal games: what creators say about voice, specificity, honesty and what to leave out

### Takeaway
The makers who did this well describe the same moves: wait until you can be honest (including about your own unflattering side), pick one narrow slice instead of the whole life, filter details by "what communicates the experience" rather than "what mattered to me", let a single mechanic or metaphor carry the feeling, and allow some fictionalising. The main failure mode named by the creators themselves is not self-indulgence but over-claiming: treating one person's story as a lesson about a whole group.

### Cited Findings

**Nina Freeman (Cibele, We Met in May, How Do You Do It): the clearest statement of the "storyteller filter"**
- On honesty over self-flattery: "It would be easy to make myself look cool in a game about myself, but that's not my goal." and "I want to be honest, which doesn't always make me look cool in the game story." - [Game Developer, Road to the IGF: Cibele](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- On distance: she waited years before making Cibele: "I needed to wait until I felt that I could write honestly about it." - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- On narrowing: she focused "on the specifics of how and why I met up with this guy" rather than the whole relationship; "it would be pretty boring to try and explain every single thing that happened between us during that time." - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- Her filter: "I ask myself what the most important details are that will communicate this experience as clearly as possible", and she had "to filter out all of that extra stuff that is important to me as a person." - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- She does not call Cibele strictly autobiographical: her approach "matches up less with autobiographical writing, and more with fiction writing." She is "interested in making games about ordinary lives" and works in "vignette" portraits, with sparse mechanics so nothing distracts from the narrative. - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- Cibele uses real photos and writing from that period of her life; Freeman studied poetry before making games and says it shaped her design; she hopes her games inspire others to draw on their own lives "no matter how ordinary or mundane" (search summary of interviews) - [GamesRadar interview](https://gamesradar.com/sex-poetry-and-autobiographical-games-cibele-and-we-met-in-may-creator-nina-freeman-on-making-the-personal-playable); [Designer Notes #10](https://www.designer-notes.com/designer-notes-10-nina-freeman) (both pages only partly retrievable)

**Anna Anthropy (Dys4ia, 2012): frustration as material, and the limit of what a personal game can claim**
- Dys4ia is about her hormone replacement therapy; she described it as "a series of short fast scenes, kind of like Wario Ware, that represents the different facets of what happened to me in this part of my life"; the follow-up Dy5phoria was framed as a fifth chapter. - [SF Bay Guardian interview (2013)](https://sfbgarchive.48hills.org/?p=28855)
- Her stated aim was to "catalog all of the frustrations of the experience and maybe create an 'it gets better' for other trans women"; "I figured if I was frustrated, other trans* folk might find it comforting to know that they didn't have to rage alone." - [Autostraddle interview](https://www.autostraddle.com/anna-anthropy-the-autostraddle-interview-143395)
- On frustration as craft: "Frustration is a powerful tool because STRUGGLE is at the heart of any story worth telling." On the avatar's shape changing scene to scene: "If I had to change every day then, so, obviously had the game." - [Autostraddle](https://www.autostraddle.com/anna-anthropy-the-autostraddle-interview-143395)
- On the zine idea (from her book Rise of the Videogame Zinesters): a growing number of people "create free games simply to make their voices heard." - [Autostraddle](https://www.autostraddle.com/anna-anthropy-the-autostraddle-interview-143395)
- Anthropy objected when a New York teacher used Dys4ia to teach students "what it's like to be trans"; her position is that playing it does not mean someone has learned empathy for trans women (the "empathy games don't exist" argument). - [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/empathy-games-dont-exist/)
- A reader in the Autostraddle comments argued Dys4ia read more as an explanation for non-trans people than a game for trans players: the audience question is live even for celebrated work. - [Autostraddle comments](https://www.autostraddle.com/anna-anthropy-the-autostraddle-interview-143395)

**Celeste (Maddy Thorson, 2018): the theme was discovered during development, not planned**
- (The 2018 Kotaku article uses her former name; Thorson confirmed she is a trans woman in 2020 - [Wikipedia: Madeline (Celeste)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeline_(Celeste)).) Her depression and anxiety became severe enough that she "had no choice but to face them head-on"; "It took me a while to realize that crunch can be a form of self-harm." - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)
- "I didn't know how it would end because I was still dealing with this stuff while we were making it." She knew early that Madeline would have to fall down the mountain, but the details "came as I understood my own anxiety better." No mental health professionals were consulted; "Our intention going in wasn't to represent mental illness in general." - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)
- The ending is acceptance, not victory: Madeline is "not just magically better. There's still conflict within her." The forgiving design (assist mode, generous checkpoints) came from wanting to "show the same kindness to the player that we want to show ourselves." - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)

**Papo & Yo (Vander Caballero, 2012): the father as a monster with two sides**
- The game's Monster is modelled on Caballero's father, who had alcohol and drug dependency; Monster is gentle until it eats frogs, which turn it into a rage-filled beast (the metaphor for drinking). - [Wikipedia: Papo & Yo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papo_%26_Yo); [The World (2013)](https://theworld.org/stories/2013/12/09/video-game)
- Caballero says a mentor told him his story had been told many times; the task was to "figure out how to use the skills you have with this new medium and tell it." - [The World](https://theworld.org/stories/2013/12/09/video-game) (via search summary)
- The team's postmortem says the deeply personal story guided direction but made the game's scope harder to define. - [Game Developer: Papo & Yo behind the scenes](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-papo-yo-i-behind-the-scenes-making-a-game-that-matters)

**That Dragon, Cancer (Ryan and Amy Green, 2016): a true story with a fixed ending**
- Green: "I was scared I would forget Joel." The game's choices do not change the outcome "because we are telling a true story. Joel doesn't survive." - [Today](https://www.today.com/today/amp/tdna17506)
- The family's own voices, including their other children, are used in home, playground and hospital scenes; making it helped Green get distance from and then process his grief. - [MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/10/30/an-unusual-approach-to-grief-make-a-video-game); [LAist](https://laist.com/shows/take-two/how-making-a-video-game-about-a-childs-cancer-diagnosis-helped-a-father-grieve); [Johns Hopkins Hub](https://hub.jhu.edu/2021/05/19/video-game-grief-ryan-green)
- Critics locate the game's force in "the honesty with which the Greens let us into their hearts and minds." - [Feminist Frequency review](https://feministfrequency.com/?p=35732)
- Sources disagree on Joel's age at death (5 per Today, 4 per Johns Hopkins). - [Today](https://www.today.com/today/amp/tdna17506); [JHU](https://hub.jhu.edu/2021/05/19/video-game-grief-ryan-green)

**Venba (Abhi / Visai Games, 2023): specificity through food, and a shifted point of view**
- "I immigrated from India with my entire family and came to Toronto when I was twelve." The game is "not 100% autobiographical" and his relationship with his own parents does not mirror Venba and Kavin's. - [Xbox Wire interview](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/07/19/venba-interview/)
- He centred the mother because most immigrant stories are told from the children's side, and first-generation parents who arrive in their 30s or 40s often live isolated lives. The mother communicates through recipes; the recipes come from a smudged, torn, handwritten cookbook the player restores; a scene has the mother selling puttu over pizza by comparing cooking to a rocket launch (summarised across interviews) - [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/07/19/venba-interview/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/venba-is-an-upcoming-cooking-game-about-an-indian-mother-restoring-her-lost-recipes); [Game Developer](https://GamaSutra.com/design/how-venba-uses-food-to-explore-connections-and-disconnections-with-culture-for-immigrants)

**Unpacking (Wren Brier and Tim Dawson / Witch Beam, 2021): a life told only through objects**
- The idea came from "a real-life unpacking session" when her partner moved in with her; it "came together when we decided to use a series of moves to tell a character's life story." Seven of the eight story beats were found about a week after the first idea. - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)
- "Personality items" carry interests, job and health; item order changes meaning (a bottle read as wine turns out to be balsamic vinegar when it comes out after the olive oil); the diploma, hung proudly in one level and with no room on the wall in a later one, carries a whole relationship. - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)
- What was cut: a parents' divorce subplot, because it could not be expressed through items; "nix a story thread because we simply couldn't convey it without any words." Brier calls the method "storytelling with duplo blocks", "very blunt and imprecise", so the major beats were kept simple and the smaller threads optional: "The game can be enjoyed without picking up on these." - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)
- A profile quotes Brier: "This is a very personal game", reflecting her own life experience (single, less prominent source). - [bi.org](https://bi.org/famous/wren-brier)

**Florence, Lost Words, A Space for the Unbound, Gris (lighter evidence)**
- Florence (Ken Wong, Mountains, 2018): little written dialogue, brief chapters; each mini-game "had a purpose and represented something", down to tooth-brushing; Wong kept the whole game on a wall and swapped chapters as they iterated; blind playtests with friends who were not told what the game was; "making short games is not a bad thing." - [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/077973/r/); [80.lv GDC recap](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-florence-postmortem); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game)); the GDC 2019 talk "Letting Go: A Florence Postmortem" is gated - [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025694/Letting-Go-A-Florence)
- Lost Words: Beyond the Page (story by Rhianna Pratchett): a girl whose grandmother has a stroke; the player walks across her diary pages and into the fantasy story she writes to cope; the developer says Pratchett drew on her own experiences (which ones is not stated). - [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/interview/lost-words-beyond-page-grief)
- A Space for the Unbound (Mojiken, Surabaya): about anxiety and depression, set in rural 1990s Indonesia; made on and off from a 2015 studio camp; the studio links its long development to anxiety and loneliness. - [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/01/26/diving-into-the-world-of-a-space-of-the-unbound/); [GamePilgrim](https://gamepilgrim.com/2020/02/22/poignant-prologue-chapter-available-for-a-space-for-the-unbound/)
- Gris (Nomada): reviewers read it through the five stages of grief, each level gaining a colour; I found no creator interview confirming a personal loss behind it. - [A Garden of Stories](https://agardenofstories.substack.com/p/gris)

**The small-tool scene (Bitsy, Twine, itch.io)**
- itch.io has living tag clusters for this work: Bitsy plus "diarygames", "introspective", "vignette", "semi-autobiographical", and a Bitsy essay jam; examples include Personal Game 1 (tagged non-fiction, autobiographical), Man or Muppet (hotelbones, a personal essay about being trans built in Bitsy with video clips), Dear Diary (intrusive thoughts). - [itch.io bitsy+diarygames](https://itch.io/games/top-rated/free/tag-bitsy/tag-diarygames); [bitsy+introspective](https://itch.io/games/tag-bitsy/tag-introspective); [bitsy+vignette](https://itch.io/games/tag-bitsy/tag-vignette); [Nathalie Lawhead on itch creators](https://www.nathalielawhead.com/candybox/itch-io-creators-you-need-to-follow-some-of-their-beautiful-work)
- Porpentine's Twiny Jam (2015) drew 239 entrants with a 300-word target, an example of how low constraints open the form. - [No Time To Play, "Easy as blogging"](https://notimetoplay.org/engines/easy-as.html)

**Academic framing**
- Gallagher (2022, Convergence) proposes a "ludobiographical mode" and studies That Dragon, Cancer, arguing life-writing has shaped ideas about agency and the human in games. - [MMU repository](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/629608/)
- Tenhaef (2025, European Journal of Life Writing) reads Dys4ia through "automediality", comparing it with weblogs, and discusses its disputed status between game and autobiography. - [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c2a61b9a1a1f4f40bf6498d4e8b0ae1b)
- Werning (2017, Persona Studies) frames autobiographical game-making as a playful performance of a persona, not a transparent self, and links the scene to accessible tools like Twine and Ren'Py (search summary; full text not opened) - [Utrecht repository listing](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/358200)

### Inferences
- The recurring recipe across Freeman, Brier, Abhi and Thorson: (1) one narrow slice, (2) concrete objects or actions instead of statements of feeling (recipes, boxes, a swing, a mountain), (3) a filter that keeps what communicates and drops what is merely precious, (4) permission to fictionalise. "Honest" in these accounts means not flattering yourself, not "include everything".
- Self-indulgence, as these creators frame it, is the uncut version: every detail that mattered to the maker. The cure they describe is editing for the player, plus time and distance before writing.
- Anthropy's objection suggests a framing rule: present each story as one person's version, never as "what it is like to be X". Edith Finch's "someone's version of what happened" (section 2) is the same idea at the structural level.
- Celeste shows the theme can surface while making; it does not need to be fully known at the start.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes from Ryan Green on honesty in his own words beyond the Today lines; the Seminary Dropout podcast and the documentary Thank You for Playing would be primary sources.
- Ken Wong's Florence talk (how the conversation puzzle mechanic maps the relationship) is gated; the specific mechanic details are not confirmed here.
- Maddy Thorson's 2020 Medium essay could not be retrieved.
- No creator interview found on Gris's personal origins or on Pratchett's specific experiences in Lost Words.
- The Werning and Cibele academic papers were not opened in full (403 on core.ac.uk).

## 2. Anthologies with a different style per story: how they hold together

### Takeaway
The anthologies that feel like one work keep a few things constant underneath the changing surface: one input grammar, one motion feel, one frame (a house, an album, a title), and one stance toward truth. Love, Death and Robots is the counterexample: its creators say there is "no connective tissue" beyond the title and a shared tone, and reviewers still judged that enough.

### Cited Findings

**What Remains of Edith Finch (Giant Sparrow, 2017)**
- 13 sub-stories, each with its own art style and mechanic; the team prototyped more than 13 games to get there; the GDC 2018 talk was "Weaving 13 Prototypes into 1 Game". The goal was mechanics players had never seen yet that stayed intuitive without tutorials. - [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/gdc-2018-see-edith-finchs-devs-weaved-13-prototypes-1-game); [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025016/contactUs)
- The constants: every story uses only two sticks and one trigger, so players learn one set of inputs. Lead designer Chris Bell: "Discovery and surprise comes from how using the same buttons gets wildly different responses." Motion everywhere uses harmonic springs for a shared organic feel: "We have springs, or springs connected to other springs, that have an inherently organic and pleasing feel to them." - [Game Developer: the 30 control schemes](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)
- Process: gameplay first, with a clear beginning, middle and end, before story or character ("Once that was working, we thought about who this event would occur to." on the swing, Calvin's story). A mechanic had to be explorable and carry a story. No reward meters: "There's no meter or explosions when you do it perfectly." Prototypes were rewritten from scratch, keeping lessons not code. - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)
- Cuts: a fish-sorting quota layer in Lewis's cannery story was too much on top of the imagination world ("we didn't appreciate that it was a piece of lots of other things"); a twin-sister story with a 180-degree flipped screen; night mountain biking; a parking garage based on Borges's "The Library"; a world made of letters. Prototypes died when "confusing or frustrating", technically too hard, or without a satisfying ending. - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-); [Gaming Trend interview](https://gamingtrend.com/interviews/interview-ian-dallas-talks-about-ambiguity-cut-features-and-magic-in-what-remains-of-edith-finch)
- The stance that unifies the stories: each is "someone's version of what happened"; "none of them feels like exactly what happened"; whether the curse is real is left to players. The aim was "what it feels like to encounter the sublime - moments that are simultaneously beautiful and overwhelming"; the finale took longest and became clear only "when we really understood our own game." "It's very easy to spoil everything and shatter the mood." - [Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/interviews/interview-ian-dallas-talks-about-ambiguity-cut-features-and-magic-in-what-remains-of-edith-finch)

**Sayonara Wild Hearts (Simogo, 2019): the album as frame**
- Each level is presented as an album track, in menus and as a step in an emotional journey from "Begin Again" to "Wild Hearts Never Die"; levels are short and switch between on-foot, rails, motorbike and more. - [Uppercut](https://uppercutcrit.com/soundscapes-as-stories/); [Nintendo Life hands-on](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/09/hands_on_sayonara_wild_hearts_is_pop_perfection_in_video_game_form)
- A reviewer called it "a flowing concept album with different peaks and valleys, but clearly retaining a single theme." Composer Daniel Olsen on the workflow: "Level changes to fit music, music changes to fit level, repeat." - [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/09/hands_on_sayonara_wild_hearts_is_pop_perfection_in_video_game_form); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayonara_Wild_Hearts)
- The game's frame story is a heartbroken young woman; NPR covered players using it as a recovery ritual during lockdown. - [NPR (2020)](https://www.npr.org/2020/09/14/908365152/sayonara-wild-hearts-pop-music-lockdown-depression-recovery)

**Kentucky Route Zero interludes (Cardboard Computer): a different medium between acts**
- Between acts the studio released free metafictional pieces: Limits and Demonstrations (an interactive art exhibition), The Entertainment (a stage play, also sold as a physical script under a fictional author with a fake ISBN), Here and There Along the Echo (a phone that calls one number, an audio guide voiced by Will Oldham), Un Pueblo de Nada (a 30-minute live-action public-access-style broadcast plus a hotline). - [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/kentucky-route-zeros-third-interlude-is-a-phone-call-you-wont-forget); [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/kentucky-route-zero-interlude-questions-our-perspective-games/); [Radiator blog](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2018/02/video-killed-video-star-on-un-pueblo-de.html?m=1)
- Jake Elliott says they err on the side of following intuition and going wherever they want to go. - [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2016-02-16-kentucky-route-zero-act-4-interview.html)

**Dys4ia as a WarioWare-style collection**
- Anthropy's own comparison: short fast scenes "kind of like Wario Ware", each a facet of one period of her life; later work added as a "fifth chapter". - [SF Bay Guardian](https://sfbgarchive.48hills.org/?p=28855)

**Love, Death and Robots as the TV model**
- Each episode is standalone and made by a different animation team (an early review counted 18 episodes from 14 studios); styles range from photoreal to 2D. - [Loyola Maroon review](https://www.LoyolaMaroon.com/10022790/life-times/review-netflixs-love-death-and-robots-is-a-burst-of-creativity/); [Wikipedia snapshot](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Love_Death_%2B_Robots)
- Tim Miller compared it to the film Heavy Metal but said "there is no connective tissue, there is no Locknar"; beyond the title there is no underlying theme. Reviewers still felt episodes "seamlessly belong with each other" through shared tone (dark humour, adult attitude) and each episode's link to one of the three title words. - [Syfy Wire](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/sxsw-love-death-robots-tim-miller-love-letter-to-nerds); [Loyola Maroon](https://www.LoyolaMaroon.com/10022790/life-times/review-netflixs-love-death-and-robots-is-a-burst-of-creativity/)

### Inferences
- Four binding layers appear across these: (1) a constant control grammar (Edith Finch's two sticks and a trigger; on a phone the equivalent would be a small fixed set like tap, drag, hold), (2) a constant motion feel (springs), (3) a frame object that is already a collection (a house of rooms, an album of tracks, a title of three words), (4) a shared stance (each story is "someone's version"). A project can vary art style per story freely if layers 1 to 3 stay fixed.
- "Gameplay first, then whose story is this" (Edith Finch) is the reverse of how a personal writer usually starts; a hybrid is plausible: start from the thought, then search for the one action that makes it felt, and drop the story if no action fits (Unpacking dropped the divorce for that reason).
- Love, Death and Robots shows a strong title plus a consistent tone can carry wildly different looks even with no narrative link.

### Gaps
- No primary WarioWare design source was gathered (how the host characters and speed-up bind microgames).
- No creator interview on why the KRZ interludes were made in other media, beyond Elliott's "intuition" remark.
- Sayonara Wild Hearts sources do not confirm that every level has a distinct musical style.

## 3. The hub or menu as part of the story

### Takeaway
The strongest examples make the navigation itself carry the theme: the Finch house is a family tree you climb, with sealed rooms as memorials; Sayonara's track list is an emotional arc; Unpacking's sequence of moves is a life; Lost Words' diary pages are the space you walk on; Cibele's desktop is the archive you snoop in. Generic diegetic-UI writing is about HUDs (Dead Space, Pip-Boy), not story hubs.

### Cited Findings
- Edith Finch's house: when a Finch dies their bedroom is sealed and left as it was, a shrine; grandmother Edie drilled peepholes into the sealed doors and added plates with each owner's name and dates of birth and death; secret passages lead in through back entrances. - [Hypercritic review](https://hypercritic.org/collection/what-remains-of-edith-finch-2021-review)
- The house was "cobbled together over generations" with additive modules (school, observatory, loft) stacked on the roof; the route is a forward-only spiral from a bunker under the basement to the loft, which the author likens to a labyrinth (calming) rather than a maze (confusing); lights switch off behind you; opening sealed rooms is "an act of archaeology"; the structure makes time visible in the level design. - [Game Developer, "The 4D Finch House" (Justin Reeve, 2018)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-4d-finch-house)
- Sayonara Wild Hearts' level menu is presented as an album track list. - [Uppercut](https://uppercutcrit.com/soundscapes-as-stories/)
- Unpacking: each level is a move, so the level select is a life timeline (the article refers to a "2010 level"). - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)
- Lost Words: the diary is a playable space between the fantasy chapters. - [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/interview/lost-words-beyond-page-grief)
- Cibele is framed as a computer desktop of emails, journal entries and images (interviewer's description). - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)
- KRZ's Here and There Along the Echo is a phone that calls one number: the interface is the whole piece. - [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/kentucky-route-zeros-third-interlude-is-a-phone-call-you-wont-forget)
- General diegetic UI literature centres on Dead Space's suit-projected inventory and Fallout's Pip-Boy, and warns diegetic elements can fail when not legible and sometimes the fiction must break to show the player more. - [Indieklem: The Diegetic Dilemma](https://indieklem.substack.com/p/19-the-diegetic-dilemma-benefits); [Wayline: diegetic interfaces](https://www.wayline.io/blog/diegetic-interfaces-game-design)

### Inferences
- Good hubs share three traits: the hub is a collection object that exists in real life (house, album, diary, desktop, moving boxes), its order means something (up the family tree, track 1 to track N, year by year), and finished or unfinished states are visible (sealed doors, a lit track). For a phone-first page this maps naturally to a vertical scroll (Finch's vertical climb) or a list (track list, contents page, diary index).
- The Finch plate detail (name plus dates on a door) shows how a hub can tell part of each story before it is opened.
- Diegetic menus must stay legible; on a small screen a clear list styled as the frame object is safer than a spatial scene the player must explore to find stories.

### Gaps
- No developer-side source on Edith Finch's house design specifically (the Reeve piece is critical analysis, Hypercritic a review).
- Did not find design writing on bookshelf or memory-palace menus in shipped narrative games.

## 4. Distinctiveness: what makes a small game "unforgettable" and get shared

### Takeaway
Marketing writers converge on a hook you can say in one line and see in one image, paired with something familiar; small web hits add a shareable artifact that does not spoil the experience (Wordle's grid). Design and marketing are one decision for a small maker: the idea that stops a scroll has to be inside the game, on its first screen and in its title.

### Cited Findings
- A hook "distinguishes your game from the rest"; example: Untitled Goose Game's "you are a goose." Players rarely cite the hook as the reason they bought; they cite familiarity (genre, a friend), so a hook needs an anchor: the hook gets people to the page, the anchor converts. - [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=448) (via search summary)
- Ryan Clark (Brace Yourself Games) separates "pre-play hooks" (what sells a trailer or GIF) from "post-play hooks" (what keeps players once they start), arguing for indies design and marketing are the same thing (search summary; article body not retrievable). - [PC Gamer opinion](https://pcgamer.com/opinion-for-indie-devs-game-design-and-marketing-are-the-same-thing)
- Joe Henson (Indie Game Joe): the best posts have "a strong visual identity" or "a mechanic that really stands out" so people stop scrolling; a good one-sentence pitch says what you do, why to care and what makes it stand out; a weak trailer can sink a good hook. - [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-secrets-to-viral-success-in-game-marketing-according-to-indie-game-joe)
- A hook makes press easier "but it's not a magic potion either"; "people buy people first" applies to narrative games where the maker's story is part of the pitch. - [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/marketing-on-a-shoestring-how-indies-are-punching-above-their-weight-in-the-promo-wars/)
- Wordle: Josh Wardle saw New Zealand players typing coloured squares to share results without spoilers and automated it; the grid let people brag without ruining the puzzle; growth went from under 1,000 to 2 million players in weeks; philosopher Thi Nguyen praised the grid as a compact record of an "arc of decisions, attempts and failures"; some found the flood of squares annoying. - [Know Your Meme](https://amp.knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/wordle); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2256733); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/); [The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/media/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault)
- Neal Agarwal (neal.fun: The Password Game, Draw a Perfect Circle, Infinite Craft) launched in 2017 and earned a full-time living from ad revenue by graduation; he cites Flash-era "Weird Web 1.0" as his influence. - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Agarwal) (his own interview on craft at Working Theorys is paywalled)
- itch.io-specific: the cover image appears in browse, search, collections and social embeds, so it should be designed for thumbnail size with a readable title, bold colour and one focal point (third-party 2026 guide). - [Cinevva itch.io launch guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide)
- A Twine developer suspected missing cover art was costing views: "Imagine how many views I'd get if I had (a) proper logo/cover art for the game." - [Tales from the Road devlog](https://iforgotmybrain.itch.io/tales-from-the-road/devlog/148788/3000-views-and-1000-plays-downloads)
- Edith Finch's lesson applies to small work too: the design target was a feeling (the sublime), and details like controls and room layout were guarded because "it's very easy to spoil everything and shatter the mood." - [Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/interviews/interview-ian-dallas-talks-about-ambiguity-cut-features-and-magic-in-what-remains-of-edith-finch)

### Inferences
- For a personal story collection the "one line" could be the frame itself (a house of sealed rooms, an album, a diary of things he meant to say) plus the anchor of a familiar format (short stories, playable in two minutes on a phone).
- A Wordle-like share object fits a story site if it reveals the feeling, not the content: e.g. a per-story card or a small visual trace of the player's choices that shows up in a link preview without spoiling.
- The cover image, the og:image link preview and the first screen are the same asset problem; they should be designed together, readable at thumbnail size.

### Gaps
- No reliable source quantifies why reviewers call a small game "unforgettable"; this section relies on marketing practitioners and one viral case. A review-language study was not found.
- Ryan Clark's article body and Neal Agarwal's interview could not be read in full.

## 5. Getting found on the web in 2026: platforms, devlogs, link previews and real numbers

### Takeaway
Realistic baselines for a small free narrative browser game are tens to low thousands of plays, mostly arriving through itch.io's own tags and browse pages; making a game browser-playable and giving it proper cover art are the cheapest levers reported. TikTok and Shorts success stories with numbers are mostly from 2022 to 2023 and for Steam games; I found no solid 2025 to 2026 data for solo narrative web games.

### Cited Findings

**Small, honest numbers from itch.io postmortems**
- Tales from the Road (Twine interactive fiction, name your price): lifetime 3,010 views, 317 downloads, 689 in-browser plays; 98% of traffic came directly from itch.io, about 2% from one FurAffinity post; no marketing spend except $30 on art. Porting from a Python download to a Twine browser build raised weekly numbers from about 30 views and 5 downloads to 60 to 80 views and 30 to 50 plays; about 55% of viewers played. The developer never posted to Reddit, unsure the game was "good enough". - [itch.io devlog](https://iforgotmybrain.itch.io/tales-from-the-road/devlog/148788/3000-views-and-1000-plays-downloads)
- Toilet Skeleton (solo journaling zine, 2024): itch.io tags were "my number one source of page views and downloads"; Cohost and a feature on a community site tied second; BlueSky "didn't produce much interest"; one LinkedIn share produced one sale; gross $195 on a 15-copy print run; a press kit and social sharing "works wonders". - [bearblog postmortem](https://slasherepoch.bearblog.dev/toilet-skeleton-postmortem/)
- Other baselines: Mystery Miniaturist (free visual novel) 169 downloads in its first month - [devlog](https://katy133.itch.io/mystery-miniaturist/devlog/283362/mystery-miniaturist-postmortem); a solo dev with one browser game reported 250 views and 55 browser plays after a couple of months - [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/11304564); a jam-born game had 178 views and 52 downloads in five days, mostly via tags, the jam page and YouTube - [dev.to](https://dev.to/digi_talino/first-game-to-gain-traction-after-several-published-games-in-itchio-57dm); Flufftopia about 60 downloads on day one - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/flufftopia-postmortem-an-itch-io-game-by-the-numbers)

**How itch.io surfaces games**
- An itch.io staff comment says the Popular browse section drives more traffic than Recent uploads, and "Recent games" is not really where games get discovered (search summary). - [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/13235936)
- Third-party observation: Popular and New and Popular are driven by ratings, written reviews and video reviews in a recent window; tags decide where a game appears in browse, so specific tags beat broad ones. - [Cinevva 2026 guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide); [Minoh Workshop SEO devlog](https://minoh.itch.io/minotaur-hotel/devlog/125081/how-to-get-your-game-noticed-search-engine-optimization-for-itch) (self-reported claims)
- Platform policy risk: on 2025-07-24 itch.io deindexed adult NSFW content from browse and search after payment processor scrutiny, later publishing a narrower list of disallowed content. Not relevant to non-adult work, but shows browse visibility depends on itch's processors. - [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2025/07/game-store-itch-io-has-deindexed-adult-content-due-to-payment-processor-scrutiny); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/itch-io-releases-list-of-the-adult-content-you-cant-put-on-its-store-anymore-after-anti-nsfw-crackdown-including-stuff-thats-not-illegal-but-just-icky/)

**Newgrounds and other portals**
- Newgrounds says a majority of its games are now HTML5; upload is a zip with index.html at the root; Ruffle runs old Flash. - [Newgrounds wiki: game dev resources](https://www.newgrounds.com/wiki/creator-resources/game-dev-resources); [Wikiwand: Newgrounds](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Newgrounds)
- I found no 2025 to 2026 traffic data for Newgrounds or Game Jolt front pages.

**Short video and devlogs**
- Case studies with numbers are older and Steam-focused: "From 400 to 20,000 wishlists with 1 video" (TikTok) - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/blogs/from-400-to-20-000-wishlists-with-1-video-how-tiktok-can-make-your-game-go-viral); Mortal Rite 120m views and 60k wishlists from TikTok (2022) - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/this-game-used-tiktok-to-get-120m-views-60k-steam-wishlists-); The Hive 10,000+ wishlists in about 10 days by cross-posting clips everywhere and letting platforms pick - [itch.io devlog](https://itch.io/devlog/1164902/10000-wishlists-in-10-days-what-changed-for-the-hive.amp)
- One 2025 opinion piece claims TikTok's organic reach collapsed in early 2025; it gives no data. - [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-originals/the-indie-game-discovery-problem-is-getting-worse)
- Steam crowding context (secondary sources, conflicting counts): roughly 9,370 of 20K+ 2025 Steam releases got fewer than ten reviews; 66% of recent releases had under 10K wishlists - [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-originals/the-indie-game-discovery-problem-is-getting-worse); another source counts 17,889 Steam releases in 2025 - [Metricus](https://metricusapp.com/blog/indie-game-distribution-user-acquisition-painpoints-2025-2026/)

**Link previews (relevant to a GitHub Pages site)**
- Guides agree on a 1200x630 og:image (about 1.91:1); off-ratio images get centre-cropped; keep text away from edges and not smaller than about 20px; JPEG at 80 to 85% quality; a secondary guide says Slack, Discord and iMessage read the same standard og:image. No source covered WhatsApp; only Facebook offers a public debugger. - [DEV: Open Graph image sizes 2026](https://dev.to/grabbit/open-graph-image-sizes-and-dimensions-the-complete-2026-guide-1k16); [Front-End Checklist](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/seo/og-image-size)

### Inferences
- For a GitHub Pages project, itch.io is best treated as a discovery mirror (an HTML5 upload or a page linking to the site) because the data above says itch's own tags and browse pages, not social posts, bring most traffic to tiny games. The Bitsy/diary/introspective/vignette tag clusters are where a personal-story audience already browses.
- The most repeatable gains in the postmortems are cheap: playable in the browser (Tales from the Road's jump), proper cover art, specific tags, and jams (jam pages bring a guaranteed first audience).
- Since each story is short, each could be its own small shareable unit with its own og:image and URL, so one story can travel in a chat without the whole site; this is inference, not tested by any source here.
- Expect tens to a few thousand plays in the first months; anything more depends on one share-worthy hook landing with a curator, a community or a video, which none of the sources show as predictable.

### Gaps
- No 2025 to 2026 dataset on TikTok or YouTube Shorts devlogs for free web narrative games; all numbers found are older or for Steam wishlists.
- No current traffic numbers for Newgrounds, Game Jolt or itch.io overall.
- No postmortem found for a personal, phone-first, GitHub-Pages-hosted story project specifically; no Reddit-driven Twine breakout with numbers.
- itch.io's official documentation on browse ranking was not found; ranking details are from staff comments and third parties.
