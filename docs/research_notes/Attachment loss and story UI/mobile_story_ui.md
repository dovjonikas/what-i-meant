# Mobile story UI: reading, tapping, choices, life lines, access and sessions for a phone-first web story

Scope: interface and interaction design for "Text me when you get home" (20 to 30 min, phone first, painted scenes, word-by-word captions with a tap dot, phone chat overlay with reply chips and tap-typed replies, "your call" choices, "Tap three" picker, hold-to-do with fill bar, life lines with dots and names and self-running years, lock screen between parts, chapter cards, gate screen with Start, sound toggle, save/resume, install).
Method: web search and fetch, 2026-10-29. Primary sources were fetched where possible (Netflix, Ren'Py, NN/g, WCAG, Game Accessibility Guidelines); several findings rest on search-result summaries of secondary sources, flagged where so. Everything under "Inferences" is my reasoning, not evidence. The four pain points the author raised are tagged [PAIN: unclear choices], [PAIN: names on dots], [PAIN: too fast], [PAIN: return to present].

## Reading and pacing on phones

### Takeaway
Adults read silently at about 238 to 260 words per minute, but subtitle standards that pair text with pictures budget far slower (BBC 160 to 180 wpm, Netflix up to 20 characters per second for adults, 17 for children), and VN engines default to instant text, tap-to-advance, no auto-forward, and skip that only covers seen text. Anything in the game that moves on its own should be timed to the subtitle numbers, not to reading speed, and text the player taps through should never be slower than the reader.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 190 studies (18,573 participants): average adult silent reading of English non-fiction is 238 wpm, fiction 260 wpm; most adults fall in 175 to 300 wpm (non-fiction) and 200 to 320 wpm (fiction); oral reading averages 183 wpm. The author says older, higher figures that are commonly cited were inflated - [Brysbaert 2019, J. Memory and Language (PDF)](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/linguistics/2019-brysbaert.pdf); [BPS Research Digest summary](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/most-comprehensive-review-date-finds-average-persons-reading-speed-slower)
- Netflix English (USA) timed text: max 42 characters per line; max two lines; "Text should usually be kept to one line, unless it exceeds the character limitation"; prefer a bottom-heavy pyramid when breaking, avoid one or two words alone on the top line; reading speed up to 20 characters per second for adult programmes, up to 17 cps for children's - [Netflix English (USA) Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)
- Netflix general requirements: minimum duration 5/6 of a second per subtitle event (20 frames at 24 fps), maximum 7 seconds; break lines after punctuation, before conjunctions and prepositions; never split article+noun, first+last name, verb+pronoun, verb+auxiliary or negation - [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide: General Requirements](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements)
- BBC subtitle guidance as reported by secondary sources (official BBC page could not be fetched): target 160 to 180 wpm, about 0.33 to 0.375 seconds per word; a line spans about 68% of a 16:9 frame width (roughly 38 to 42 characters depending on font); 180 wpm is about 15 cps in English - [Clevercast summary of BBC guidelines](https://clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines); [Web-Translations summary](https://web-translations.com/?p=8957). A BBC R&D post (read via a mirror) says Ofcom sets the maximum at 160 to 180 wpm and anything over 200 wpm is hard for many viewers - [BBC R&D "How fast should subtitles be?" (mirror)](https://bbcbreakingnews.pages.dev/rd/blog/2015-09-how-fast-should-subtitles-be)
- Ren'Py preference defaults: `text_cps` = 0 (text appears instantly; a positive value sets characters per second); `afm_enable` = False (auto-forward off by default); `afm_time` = 15 (auto-forward wait, scaled by line length); `skip_unseen` = False (skip only skips text already read); `skip_after_choices` = False (skipping stops at choices); `transitions` = 2 (all on, 0 = none); `mobile_rollback_side` and `desktop_rollback_side` = "disable" (tapping a screen side does not roll back unless enabled) - [Ren'Py docs: Preferences](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/preferences.html)
- Ren'Py keeps a dialogue history data model; `config.history_length` "must be set to enable history at all", and each entry can carry a `rollback_identifier` so the player can jump back to that line - [Ren'Py docs: Dialogue History](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/history.html)
- Rereading matters for comprehension: when each word was masked once the eyes passed it (no regressions possible), comprehension dropped; the authors conclude regressions support understanding and question RSVP speed-reading apps that remove eye movements - [Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014, Psychological Science 25(6)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797614531148)
- No study was found that measures typewriter or word-by-word reveal in games against whole-line reveal; the only engine-level evidence is that typewriter extensions ship with a skip-to-end action - [GDevelop auto-typing extension](https://gdevelop-wiki-git-fork-lunimoon-patch-2-gdevelop.vercel.app/gdevelop5/extensions/auto-typing)
- inkle's 80 Days delivers text in passages of "100 words or less"; Failbetter's Sunless Skies splits text so players read "two or three paragraphs at a time"; inkle's Jon Ingold: if you give players "at least half an excuse not to read something then they won't", and readers often pick a choice without reading the text before it, so choices must be clear on their own; he also warns against making "all dialogue ... five words long" - [Game Developer: How to remove the frustration of reading in games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: "Allow players to progress through text prompts at their own pace" (Basic); "Include an option to adjust the game speed" (Basic); "Allow all narrative and instructions to be replayed" (Advanced) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines, full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)
- On the web generally, users read about a quarter of the text on pages they visit - [NN/g, How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the cited numbers: 238 to 260 wpm = 4.0 to 4.3 words/s = 230 to 250 ms per word. A word-by-word reveal at that pace makes the reveal the ceiling for every reader faster than average (half of them). Recommendation: reveal at 2x average reading speed or faster (about 100 to 120 ms per word, or 35 to 50 ms per character), and make the first tap during a reveal complete the caption instantly and the second tap advance. This is the universal VN convention and the reason Ren'Py ships with instant text by default.
- Keep word-by-word for emotional beats only, or switch to a whole-caption fade (150 to 250 ms). Once a caption is fully shown, keep it on screen until the tap, so regressions stay possible (Schotter et al.). Never mask or scroll away earlier words of the current caption.
- Caption size: a phone column of 360 to 430 CSS px minus 2 x 16 px gutters leaves about 330 to 400 px; at an 18 to 20 px font with average glyph width about 0.5 em that is roughly 34 to 44 characters per line. So cap captions at two lines and about 80 characters (close to Netflix's 2 x 42), prefer one line, and break by the Netflix rules (after punctuation, never split a name). Longer thoughts become two taps.
- [PAIN: too fast] Anything that advances by itself (the years on the life lines, typing dots that resolve into a message, a caption that leaves on a timer) should use subtitle-grade timing, not reading speed: at least 0.35 s per word plus about 0.5 s to find the text, never under 0.83 s (Netflix floor), and nothing that must be read should vanish in less than about 1.5 s. Better: no text auto-advances at all; only ambience moves on its own (GAG Basic: own pace).
- Add a backlog: a swipe-down or a small "history" button that shows the last 20 to 30 captions and chat messages (Ren'Py history pattern; GAG Advanced: replay all narrative). For a 20 to 30 minute story this is cheap and directly helps anyone who tapped too fast.
- On replay, offer "skip what you have seen" that stops at choices (Ren'Py defaults `skip_unseen = False`, `skip_after_choices = False`), so a second playthrough for other choices costs minutes, not 20.
- Tap-typed replies (two letters per tap) are a time sink if a reply is long: a 60-character reply needs 30 taps. See the GAG "Avoid repeated inputs" finding under Accessibility.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of word-by-word versus whole-line reveal in games or VNs was found.
- Official BBC Subtitle Guidelines page could not be fetched (both bbc.co.uk and bbc.github.io failed); its numbers above come from secondary summaries.
- No data was found on reading speed specifically on phones versus desktop for narrative text.
- Ren'Py's default click-to-continue indicator behaviour and default history screen binding were not verified.

## Tap affordance and feedback

### Takeaway
Players learn where to tap from immediate, visible response, big targets and consistent placement, not from tutorials; platform minimums are 44 x 44 pt (Apple) and 48 x 48 dp (Material), WCAG AA is 24 x 24 CSS px. Web haptics are not available on iOS in 2026 (WebKit never shipped `navigator.vibrate`) and work on Android Chrome only after a user gesture, so the design cannot depend on vibration.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA: "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", with a spacing exception (a 24 px circle around an undersized target must not intersect another target or its circle); addresses hand tremor, spasticity, and "people with large fingers"; the Understanding doc recommends aiming for the stricter 2.5.5 for important controls - [W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- WCAG 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), AAA: 44 x 44 CSS px; Apple HIG minimum tappable area 44 x 44 pt for all controls; Material Design 48 x 48 dp (about 9 mm physical); the size is the hit area, not the drawn icon (a 16 px icon with padding to 44 px passes) - [Front-End Checklist: touch targets (secondary, quotes HIG and Material)](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/accessibility/touch-targets); [W3C Understanding 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)
- Hoober 2013: 1,333 observations of people using phones in public; of 780 touching the screen, 49% held the phone in one hand, 36% cradled it in one hand and tapped with the other, 15% used two hands; two thirds of one-handed grips were right-handed - [Hoober, UXmatters: How Do Users Really Hold Mobile Devices?](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)
- Josh Clark's synthesis: thumbs drive about 75% of phone interactions; only about a third of the screen is effortless thumb territory, at the bottom - [A List Apart: How We Hold Our Gadgets](https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-hold-our-gadgets/). Hoober's later work says the three-grip picture is not the only standard and people shift grips often - [UX Planet summary (secondary)](https://uxplanet.org/mobile-usability-made-simple-945e106e23eb)
- Vibration API status: WebKit has never shipped it on iOS or iPadOS and formally opposes it; Firefox desktop removed it in version 129 (Aug 2024); Firefox for Android has had it present but disabled since version 79 (returns true, no vibration); Chrome requires a user gesture since Chrome 60; the W3C spec is not expected to advance to Recommendation, and a 2024 W3C staff formal objection argued for obsoleting it on privacy grounds - [W3C Vibration implementation report](https://w3c.github.io/vibration/reports/implementation.html); [Chromium intent to deprecate vibrate without user gesture](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/QGcpjisxtik/m/reSqe_HgCgAJ); [Mozilla bug 1653318](https://bugzil.la/1653318); [W3C Vibration API](https://www.w3.org/TR/vibration/)
- iOS workaround: toggling `<input type="checkbox" switch>` (Safari 17.4+) fires the system haptic, which libraries exploit; reports say iOS 18.4 restricted it to real clicks with a grant of about one second, and one tool page claims Apple patched it in iOS 26.5 (single, unverified source) - [web-haptics browser support](https://www.mintlify.com/lochie/web-haptics/advanced/browser-support); [iOS Haptics Tester (26.5 claim)](https://rapidtoolset.com/en/tool/ios-haptics-tester)
- NN/g: small feedback animations should run "roughly 100 ms" so the response feels like physically manipulating the object; most UI animation 100 to 500 ms; "At 500ms, animations start to feel like a real drag" - [NN/g: Executing UX Animations: Duration and Motion Characteristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)
- Long press: Google argued the 400 to 500 ms wait of a long press hurts usability and discoverability because the lack of immediate feedback disconnects action from response (company design argument, not a study) - [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=363096). A gesture-design chapter notes that feedback during a gesture can reveal the affordance, while feedback after a tap cannot - [Springer chapter, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-53294-9_20)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: "Give a clear indication that interactive elements are interactive" (Intermediate); "Ensure interactive elements / virtual controls are large and well spaced, particularly on small or touch screens" (Basic); "Avoid / provide alternatives to requiring buttons to be held down" (Intermediate); "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by sounds alone" (Basic); "Provide separate volume controls or mutes for effects, speech and background / music" (Basic) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)
- inkle's Jon Ingold on mobile: the small screen keeps players from feeling overwhelmed by text, and touch-to-continue makes players feel they are drawing the game out of the glass (via search summaries of interviews; exact wording not fetched) - [Game Developer: 80 Days, building the perfect text adventure for mobile](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-80-days-i-building-the-perfect-text-adventure-for-mobile); [IF50: 2014, 80 Days](https://if50.substack.com/p/2014-80-days)

### Inferences
- Treat "continue" as a whole-screen target (everything except chips and buttons), so precision never matters during captions. Put every real control (reply chips, "your call" options, picker items, Start, settings) in the bottom third of the column, at least 44 CSS px tall with at least 8 px between targets. On iOS Safari at default zoom 1 CSS px renders as 1 pt, so 44 CSS px meets Apple's 44 pt.
- The tap dot: show it only after the caption has fully appeared, and add a gentle pulse if no tap comes for about 3 to 4 s. A dot that is always present stops meaning anything; a dot that appears exactly when input is accepted teaches the rhythm in two or three captions.
- Every tap needs a visible response within 100 ms (NN/g): a small ripple or the dot shrinking. Taps that are ignored (for example during a transition) should still show a response, or players read the game as frozen.
- Haptics: use `navigator.vibrate(10 to 20)` only as progressive enhancement on Android Chrome after the first tap; do nothing on iOS. Do not build on the checkbox-switch hack: undocumented, already changed twice, possibly patched. Pair every haptic with a visual cue and, when sound is on, a soft click, never sound alone (GAG Basic).
- Hold-to-do: start the fill on touchstart within 100 ms (feedback during the gesture reveals the affordance); keep the hold short (under about 1.2 s) unless the length is the point; if released early, let the bar drain visibly and keep the hint. Offer a tap alternative in settings (GAG Intermediate). Disable iOS long-press side effects on that element (`-webkit-touch-callout: none; user-select: none; touch-action: manipulation`) so a callout or text selection does not steal the hold.
- Sound: the single sound toggle is fine for the gate; a settings sheet with two mutes (music, effects) meets the GAG Basic item.
- [PAIN: unclear choices] When the game waits for something other than a plain tap, say the verb in the UI itself ("Hold", "Tap three", "Type"), on the control, not in a caption above it, because players skip text before choices (Ingold). If the player taps the background while a choice is pending, pulse the chips instead of doing nothing.

### Gaps
- No controlled study on long-press discoverability was found (only design arguments).
- Apple HIG and Material pages were not fetched directly; their 44 pt and 48 dp numbers come from a secondary checklist that quotes them.
- The iOS 26.5 patch claim for switch haptics rests on one tool page.

## Choices

### Takeaway
Acknowledging a choice right away gives players nearly as much felt agency as real branching (Fendt et al., ICIDS 2012 best paper), and choices feel meaningful when they are moral, social and consequential (Iten et al., CHI 2018). Choices should read as choices through a distinct visual state and a verb, be untimed in a reflective story, and pay off later in a way the player can recognise.

### Cited Findings
- Fendt, Harrison, Ware, Cardona-Rivera & Roberts built a non-branching text story that answered each choice with immediate textual feedback; in most cases there was no significant difference in self-reported agency between the branching story and the linear story with explicit acknowledgement of choices; Best Paper at ICIDS 2012 (of 48 papers) - [Fendt et al. 2012, "Achieving the Illusion of Agency" (PDF)](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/fendt2012achieving.pdf); [NC State news](https://www7.csc.ncsu.edu/news/1428)
- Iten, Steinemann & Opwis (CHI 2018), mixed-method study: moral, social and consequential characteristics were important for making choices meaningful, and meaningful choices raised appreciation - [Choosing to Help Monsters, CHI 2018 (record)](https://researchr.org/publication/ItenSO18); [edoc.unibas.ch](https://edoc.unibas.ch/66738). Their 2017 CHI PLAY work in progress found meaningful choices combined moral, strategic or emotional options, and almost all reported choices had a social component (via search summary) - [edoc.unibas.ch](https://edoc.unibas.ch/57939/)
- Telltale's "X will remember that": no formal study found. A 2017 thesis argues agency in The Walking Dead lives in character relations rather than plot; critical essays say the notices create a strong feeling of consequence while replays show outcomes change little - [Monchan 2017 thesis (UAB)](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/tfg/2017/181036/TFG_JosueMonchan_20170720.pdf); [First Person Scholar: The Talking Dead](https://firstpersonscholar.com/?p=419)
- Ingold: the core problem of branching narrative is players doubting their choices have any effect; the GDC 2015 talk covered "making choices that matter" and pacing - [GDC Vault: Adventures in Text](https://gdcvault.com/play/1021775/Adventures-in-Text-Innovating-in); [Emily Short: Interactive Narrative GDC Talks](https://emshort.blog/2015/04/02/interactive-narrative-gdc-talks-part-1/)
- Timed choices: no rigorous controlled study found. Timed decisions trace to Sakura Wars, where not answering still changes plot or relationships - [Giant Bomb: Timed Decisions](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Timed_Decisions); critique that short, vague prompts plus timers make players act against their intentions - [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=225595); a DiGRA paper contrasts unlimited time (Hades) with a hard limit (Twelve Minutes) as shaping agency - [DiGRA paper (PDF)](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2561/2554/2590); a VN essay calls timers a rare tool that clashes with calm tone - [Fuwanovel: Timed choices](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/blogs/entry/4067-timed-choices-%E2%80%93-an-anatomy-of-visual-novels)
- GAG: "Do not make precise timing essential to gameplay" (Advanced); "Allow players to progress through text prompts at their own pace" (Basic) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)
- Reigns: once each swipe was weighted on four kingdom stats, "simple swiping gestures took on a lot of meaning"; cards written as short direct question, quick snappy answer, dire consequences; portraits were replaced by cards because players were already moving cards - [Game Developer: Game Design Deep Dive, Reigns](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-creating-an-adaptive-narrative-in-i-reigns-i-); [Reigns and emerging narrative (blog summary)](https://lochrist.github.io/blog/2017-04-03-reigns-and-emerging-narrative)
- Emily is Away: pick one of three replies, then type it by pressing keys; the player sees the character type, delete and retype before sending (via search summaries of review coverage) - [Wikipedia: Emily Is Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away); [Emily Short, IF Comp 2015](https://emshort.blog/tag/kyle-seeley/). Seeley added an "is typing" indicator that AIM never had, for engagement and "narrative potential" - [We Got This Covered interview](https://wegotthiscovered.com/gaming/interview-kyle-seeley-emily-is-away/)

### Inferences
- [PAIN: unclear choices] Make one consistent "choice state" that differs from "caption state" in three ways at once: the tap dot disappears, the "your call" mark appears in the same place every time, and the options are chips or buttons with borders in the thumb zone. Background taps do nothing except pulse the options. If any choice uses a different input (hold, pick three, type), the verb is written on the control.
- Acknowledge every choice within the same beat: the chosen chip stays lit for 300 to 600 ms, the other options fade, and the next caption or message reacts to the specific choice (Fendt et al.). This is the cheapest agency the story can buy.
- Pay-offs later: when a past choice matters, echo it with the player's own words or the same colour (a quiet "you kept her close" moment), rather than a Telltale-style banner. A small, diegetic echo fits a reflective story; a banner fits a thriller. Keep at least one or two choices that visibly change what is on screen later, so the acknowledgement is not exposed as hollow on a replay.
- Keep choices untimed. The story's subject (attachment, loss) favours reflection; timers mainly add stress and, with vague chip text, misfires (Destructoid critique; GAG).
- Chip text: full, specific short sentences in the player's voice (Reigns "snappy answer"), not vague two-word prompts; the chip text is the content players actually read (Ingold).
- Tap-typed replies (Emily is Away pattern): the typing carries meaning only when it shows hesitation (typing, deleting, retyping). Use it for two or three charged replies, not routine ones, and let a hold auto-type the rest.
- "Who do you keep close? Tap three.": show three empty slots that fill as names are tapped; tapping a chosen name again removes it; the confirm control appears only when three are chosen (or the third tap commits after about 600 ms with an undo). The slots make "how many" visible without a counter and make the constraint feel like the meaning, not a form.

### Gaps
- No research on multi-select pickers inside narrative games was found.
- The Reigns stat-preview dots (which stats a swipe will move) were not confirmed by a fetched source.
- No primary Telltale developer source on the "will remember that" notice was retrieved.

## Life timeline data visualisation

### Takeaway
Direct labels beside the data cut eye movements and comprehension time compared with legends, especially as complexity grows; self-running animation is enjoyable but error-prone and the weakest form for understanding trends, including on phones. Name every dot directly, keep labels readable against painted backgrounds (4.5:1 text, 3:1 marks), never rely on colour alone, and let the player pause or step the years.

### Cited Findings
- Huestegge & Philipp 2011: placing graph elements spatially compatible with their legend entries reduced comprehension time, memory retrieval time and gaze transitions between data and legend - [Attention, Perception & Psychophysics 2011 (PDF)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-011-0155-1.pdf). A 2018 follow-up found the advantage holds once graph complexity passes a threshold, with costs in multi-panel layouts - [Huestegge & Philipp 2018](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13414-018-1484-0)
- Renshaw et al. 2004: the design and location of a graph's legend and its spatial relationship to the data area are "extremely important" for usability (via search summary) - [University of Huddersfield repository](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/1484/)
- Robertson, Fernandez, Fisher, Lee & Stasko 2008: for analysis, animation was the least effective form; both static views were significantly faster and small multiples more accurate; for presentation animation was fastest and rated enjoyable and exciting "but it does lead to many participant errors" - [Effectiveness of Animation in Trend Visualization, InfoVis 2008 (PDF)](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf)
- On mobile phones (96 crowdworkers, 9 trend tasks): small multiples were faster; animation users were slightly more confident; accuracy depended on how target and distractor items moved - [Brehmer et al. 2019, arXiv 1907.03919](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03919)
- NN/g motion: most UI animation 100 to 500 ms; 200 to 300 ms for large screen changes; entrances slightly longer than exits (e.g. 300 ms in, 200 to 250 ms out); ease-out for entrances, linear "looks weird and unnatural" - [NN/g: animation duration](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)
- WCAG 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum): 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold) - [W3C Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). WCAG 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast: 3:1 for graphical objects needed to understand content - [W3C Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html). WCAG 1.4.1: colour must not be the only visual means of conveying information - [W3C Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
- WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide: moving content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and is shown alongside other content needs a way to pause, stop or hide it - [W3C Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have colour vision deficiency - [Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)
- GAG: "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone" (Basic) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)

### Inferences
- [PAIN: names on dots] Every dot that matters carries its name as a direct label at all times it is on screen, not only on entry, and not in a legend. If space is short, label people who matter now and fade others' labels to 50% rather than dropping them.
- Label collisions: place labels with a simple rule set (prefer right of the dot, then left, then above; nudge vertically with a minimum gap of one line height; draw a short leader line when a label moves more than about 12 px from its dot). With a handful of lines this can be precomputed per year step rather than solved live. Recompute only when a line enters or leaves.
- Label legibility on painted scenes: 14 px minimum (16 px preferred), weight 600, on a semi-opaque pill or a 2 to 3 px text halo in the background colour, to reach 4.5:1 regardless of what the painting does underneath; dots and lines at 3:1 against their local background.
- Colour plus name: each person keeps one colour for life (consistent with the project's character rule), but identity comes from the name label; check the palette in a deuteranopia simulator, since 1 in 12 men cannot rely on red versus green.
- [PAIN: too fast] Self-running years: Robertson et al. say animation is fine for "presentation" but breeds errors. Make the run slow enough that each new label is readable (at least 1.2 to 1.5 s dwell per step that introduces a name), hold for a beat at each event (a birth, a loss), and give tap-to-pause and tap-to-resume (WCAG 2.2.2 once the run exceeds 5 s). Alternatively step a year per tap at key moments.
- Entries and exits: a new person's dot grows in over about 300 ms with ease-out and the label fades in 100 to 150 ms after the dot lands; an exit fades and shrinks over 200 to 250 ms but the label lingers a little longer than the dot, so a loss is read, not missed. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, replace growth and travel with opacity only.

### Gaps
- No study comparing direct labels and legends specifically on phones, or for animated timelines, was found.
- No peer-reviewed guidance found on label-placement algorithms for small animated timelines; the placement rules above are inference.

## Accessibility for narrative games

### Takeaway
The Game Accessibility Guidelines' Basic tier maps almost one to one onto this game: readable default text, high contrast, own pace through text, clear interactive cues, no colour-only or sound-only information, separate volume, large well-spaced controls. Text should be 18 px or larger for reading comfort, sizing in rem so browser zoom works, captions and chats exposed to screen readers as live DOM text, and motion that honours `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Cited Findings
- GAG items relevant here (tier in brackets): "Use an easily readable default font size" (Basic); "Allow the font size to be adjusted" (Advanced); "Provide high contrast between text/UI and background" (Basic); "Avoid any sudden unexpected movement or events" (Advanced); "Provide an option to turn off / hide background movement" (Intermediate); "Avoid flickering images and repetitive patterns" (Basic); "If any subtitles / captions are used, present them in a clear, easy to read way" (Basic); "Allow subtitle/caption presentation to be customised" (Intermediate); "Ensure subtitles/captions are or can be turned on before any sound is played" (Intermediate); "Provide captions or visuals for significant background sounds" (Intermediate); "Ensure screenreader support for mobile devices" (Intermediate); "Include contextual in-game help / guidance / tips" (Intermediate); "Avoid repeated inputs (button-mashing/quick time events)" (Intermediate) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines, full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)
- Xbox Accessibility Guideline 101 (text display): minimum text size for mobile and streaming scales with pixel density, 18 px at 100 DPI, 36 px at 200 DPI, 72 px at 400 DPI (a 5.5" 1080p screen is about 400 DPI); measured as body height from lowest descender to highest ascender; screen magnifiers do not count toward the minimum - [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)
- Rello, Pielot & Marcos (CHI 2016), eye tracking with 104 participants on Wikipedia: readability rose with font size; line spacing extremes (0.8, 1.8) hurt; recommendation: text-heavy sites should use 18 points or larger with default line spacing - [Make It Big! (record)](https://researchr.org/publication/RelloPM16). Rello et al. W4A 2013, 28 readers with dyslexia: font size significantly affected readability and understandability, line spacing did not; recommends 18 point - [Size Matters (Spacing Not), DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2461121.2461125)
- Apple HIG typography: iOS default body text 17 pt, minimum 11 pt - [Apple HIG: Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text: text can be resized to 200% without loss of content or function - [W3C Understanding 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html). WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA): motion triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential - [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html). WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable: users can turn off, adjust or extend time limits - [W3C Understanding 2.2.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` media query reports the OS "reduce motion" setting (iOS, Android, macOS, Windows) - [MDN: prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)
- ARIA live regions announce dynamic text changes to screen readers (`aria-live="polite"` waits for the user to be idle); `role="log"` is for chat-like logs where new entries are appended and has implicit polite live behaviour - [MDN: ARIA live regions](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/ARIA_Live_Regions); [MDN: log role](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Roles/log_role)
- Ren'Py ships self-voicing (built-in text-to-speech of on-screen text); auto-forward waits for it when `wait_voice` is True (default) - [Ren'Py docs: Preferences](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/preferences.html)

### Inferences
- Default caption and chat text 18 to 20 CSS px (Rello: 18 pt or larger; Apple body 17 pt), line height about 1.4, sized in rem so iOS and Android browser text zoom and pinch-free zoom work. An in-game "text size" with three steps (100%, 125%, 150%) covers GAG Advanced cheaply; test that two-line captions still fit at 150%. XAG 101's mobile numbers are stricter (they target streamed console games); I read them as an upper bound, not a requirement for a phone-native web story.
- Captions as real DOM text (not drawn into a canvas) inside an `aria-live="polite"` region; the chat overlay as `role="log"`; reply chips, "your call" options and picker items as real `<button>` elements with their full text; each painted scene gets a short alt description of only what the story needs. During word-by-word reveal, update the live region once with the whole caption, not word by word, or screen readers will stutter.
- Reduced motion: when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, replace parallax, camera drift, dot growth and travel with opacity fades; keep the years stepping but without sliding. Also offer the same switch in settings (GAG Intermediate: turn off background movement).
- Hold and repeated taps: provide a settings toggle "tap instead of hold" and "type replies automatically" (GAG: avoid holds and repeated inputs, Intermediate).
- Ensure all meaning carried by sound (a notification ping, a door, breathing) also has a visual cue (GAG Basic and Intermediate), and that the gate screen offers sound on or off before any audio plays (GAG Intermediate).

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on how screen reader users experience web visual novels specifically.
- Whether iOS Dynamic Type can be read by web pages (e.g. WebKit `font: -apple-system-body`) was not verified in this round.

## Retention and session design for a 20 to 30 minute web story

### Takeaway
On the web the first 10 seconds decide most departures and anyone who stays 30 seconds often stays two minutes or more, so the gate screen must already be the story. iOS 26 now makes every "Add to Home Screen" site open as a web app, but neither Safari nor Firefox offers an install prompt API (`beforeinstallprompt` is Chromium only), so install has to be a short instruction offered at a natural pause, not at the start. Save on every step; Safari's 7-day storage cap barely touches a 30-minute story.

### Cited Findings
- Liu, White & Dumais (SIGIR 2010), 205,873 pages and over 2 billion dwell times: leaving follows a Weibull distribution with "negative aging" for about 99% of pages (the longer users stay, the less likely they leave); the first 10 seconds carry the highest chance of leaving, leaving stays likely through the next 20 s, and after about 30 s the leave rate drops; users who stay 30 s often stay 2 minutes or more; NN/g's advice is to communicate the value proposition within 10 seconds - [NN/g: How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)
- iOS 26 and iPadOS 26: every website added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default, whether or not it has a manifest; the Add to Home Screen sheet has an "Open as Web App" toggle on by default; Apple aligned this with Mac Dock web apps - [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/?p=1042117); [heise](https://heise.de/-10749652); [Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=49472); [MacRumors how-to](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/save-safari-bookmark-web-app-iphone-home-screen/)
- `beforeinstallprompt` (the only programmatic install prompt) is supported in Chromium browsers (Chrome, Edge, Opera, Chrome Android since 76) and not in Safari (including 26.x) or Firefox; MDN marks it experimental and non-standard - [caniuse: BeforeInstallPromptEvent](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_beforeinstallpromptevent); [Web features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/beforeinstallprompt)
- Safari's 7-day cap (since Safari 13.1 / iOS 13.4, 2020): after 7 days of Safari use without interaction with a site, its script-writable storage (localStorage, IndexedDB, service worker registrations) is deleted; Apple clarified that Home Screen web apps "have their own counter of days of use" that resets with use. These are 2020 sources; no 2026 confirmation was found - [WebKit blog: Full Third-Party Cookie Blocking and More](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/); [Michael Tsai summary](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=28475); [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/apple-cops-flak-for-deleting-local-browser-storage-after-7-days-539833); a WebKit bug reported localStorage persisting in practice - [WebKit bug 211775](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=211775)
- Browsers block audio autoplay with sound until the user interacts with the page (tap or click) - [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)
- GAG: "Provide an autosave feature" (Intermediate); "Provide a manual save feature" (Intermediate); "If using a long overarching narrative, provide summaries of progress" (Intermediate); "Provide gameplay thumbnails with game saves" (Intermediate) - [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)
- Lifeline used real-time gaps as pacing: the first gap of one hour sets expectations for how long the character's tasks take; shorter gaps later make the fight for survival feel frantic, longer ones build tension before big decisions; delays were set to match what a real person would need (designer Mars Jokela, crediting writer Dave Justus) - [GameAnalytics: Lifeline interview](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/lifeline-3-minute-games-interview); [Game Developer: Building a narrative out of push notifications in Lifeline](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-a-narrative-out-of-push-notifications-in-i-lifeline-i-). A review argued the waiting falls apart after the first playthrough - [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/?p=170487)
- Bury Me, My Love: messages can arrive at intervals of minutes to hours as push notifications, like texts from a friend; the character may go offline overnight - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/bury-me-my-love-tells-a-moving-story-with-text-message-1820228225); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/bury-me-my-love-review/); [VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/features/bury-me-my-love-is-a-mobile-game-about-a-syrian-refugee-trying-to-get-to-europe)
- Florence's team (Ken Wong, GDC 2019) says "making short games is not a bad thing" and playtests ideas on people who are not told what the game is - [80.lv: GDC Florence postmortem](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-florence-postmortem); [GDC Vault: Letting Go, a Florence postmortem](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025694/Letting-Go-A-Florence)

### Inferences
- First 10 seconds: the gate is required anyway (audio needs a tap), so make it the first painted scene with the title and one line of the story over it, and the Start control in the thumb zone; the sound choice sits beside Start. No settings, no install, no instructions before the first caption. The first interaction after Start should be the plain caption tap, which teaches the core rhythm with zero risk.
- Within the first 30 s, give one tiny interaction beyond tapping (a single message arriving on the phone, a reply chip with one option), so the "this is a game" promise lands before the 30 s drop-off window closes.
- Save on every caption advance and every choice (localStorage, a few hundred bytes). On return, the gate shows "Continue" first and "Start again" second, and Continue opens on a one-line recap card ("Last time: ...", GAG summaries of progress) plus the last caption, not mid-reveal.
- Chapter cards and the lock screen are the natural stopping points: they should feel like a breath (no input needed for 1 to 2 s, then a tap), and they are the right place to say "your progress is saved" once, and to offer install once.
- Install: offer it at the first lock screen, never at the gate. On Chromium Android, call the deferred `beforeinstallprompt`; on iOS show a two-step visual instruction (Share, then Add to Home Screen; iOS 26 makes it open as an app automatically); on Firefox show nothing or the browser menu hint. Hide the offer when `display-mode: standalone` already matches. Installed iOS web apps also get their own storage day counter.
- Do not use Lifeline-style real-time waits: the game is designed to be finished in one 20 to 30 minute sitting, web push on iOS needs an installed web app, and the TouchArcade critique shows waiting does not survive replays. Use diegetic delays of seconds (typing dots) instead, timed to the subtitle numbers.
- [PAIN: return to present] An unannounced cut back to the present reads as a crash because the brain sees a state reset with no cause (GAG Advanced: avoid sudden unexpected events). Mark every time jump with the same three signals: a held beat (a 600 to 900 ms fade through black or the lock screen, longer than UI animation because it is a scene cut, not a control), an explicit time stamp in the scene or on the phone clock ("Now", or the date), and a sound cue when sound is on. Keep one persistent element across the jump (the phone, a life line, the same colour) so the player knows it is the same story. NN/g's 500 ms "drag" limit is for UI feedback; narrative cuts can be longer when they carry meaning, but should not require a tap unless they are a chapter card.

### Gaps
- No 2026 primary confirmation of Safari's storage eviction rules was found; `navigator.storage.persist()` support on iOS was not verified.
- No completion or drop-off data for web interactive stories (The Pudding, NYT interactives, Neal.fun) was found in this round.
- iOS web push requiring a Home Screen web app (since iOS 16.4) was not re-verified for 2026.

## Concrete examples from shipped games

### Takeaway
The shipped games that work on phones share four habits: text in short chunks the player pulls with a tap, interaction that carries the emotion (Florence's puzzles, Emily is Away's typing), choices that are concrete and visibly weighted (Reigns), and pacing that is either fully player-paced or deliberately real-time (Lifeline, Bury Me, My Love), never half-automatic.

### Cited Findings
- Florence (Mountains, 2018): dialogue is a jigsaw puzzle in a speech bubble that gets easier as the relationship grows ("what if her speaking is a puzzle, of sorts, and it gets easier over time?"); the game has very little text and leaves the player to fill in the blanks - [Player.One interview with Ken Wong](https://www.player.one/florence-game-ken-wong-interview-123975). The first concept was rotating a 3D puzzle of human heads; the team moved to 2D because 3D rotation was too awkward - [Game Informer, GDC 2019](https://gameinformer.com/gdc-2019/2019/03/20/florence-started-development-as-3d-rubiks-cube-of-human-heads)
- 80 Days (inkle, 2014): passages of 100 words or less; pacing of text was inkle's distinctive contribution; a touch-to-continue rhythm on a small screen - [Game Developer: reading in games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games); [IF50: 80 Days](https://if50.substack.com/p/2014-80-days)
- Emily is Away (Kyle Seeley, 2015): choose a reply, then type it out key by key, seeing hesitation as typing and deleting; added a typing indicator for narrative potential - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away); [We Got This Covered interview](https://wegotthiscovered.com/gaming/interview-kyle-seeley-emily-is-away/)
- Reigns (Nerial, 2016): binary swipe weighted on four stats; short question, snappy answer - [Game Developer: Reigns deep dive](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-creating-an-adaptive-narrative-in-i-reigns-i-)
- Lifeline (3 Minute Games, 2015) and Bury Me, My Love (The Pixel Hunt, 2017): real-time, notification-paced messaging stories - [GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/lifeline-3-minute-games-interview); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/bury-me-my-love-tells-a-moving-story-with-text-message-1820228225)

### Inferences
- The phone overlay is the strongest borrowed convention here (Emily is Away, Bury Me, My Love, Lifeline): keep it faithful to real messaging apps (typing dots of 1 to 3 s scaled to message length, timestamps, read state), because players already know how to read it without teaching.
- Florence's lesson for this game: let the input itself change as the relationship changes (for example, replies that get easier or harder to type), instead of explaining feelings in captions.
- 80 Days' lesson: 100 words or fewer between interactions; for this game that is roughly five two-line captions before the player gets to do something other than tap.

- No usable interface sources were retrieved this round for A Normal Lost Phone, Simulacra, Sorcery!, Monument Valley, To the Moon mobile, The Pudding, NYT interactives or Neal.fun; their patterns are not represented above.
- The Florence GDC talk (GDC Vault) is paywalled; how Florence taught its interactions without text was not found.
