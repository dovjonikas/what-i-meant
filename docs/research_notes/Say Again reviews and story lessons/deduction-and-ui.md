# Deduction satisfaction and phone-first narrative interface craft (notes for "Say Again")

Scope: (A) what critics, designers and players say makes deduction click in Return of the Obra Dinn, Her Story, The Case of the Golden Idol, Papers Please, Outer Wilds and The Roottrees Are Dead; (B) interface craft in phone-first narrative games (Florence, Device 6, Lifeline, A Normal Lost Phone, Bury Me My Love, Sayonara Wild Hearts) plus platform figures on text speed, tap-to-continue, holds, touch targets and reading on a phone.

## KQ1. What does the "click" of a deduction feel like, and what design rules produce it (information on screen, player makes the final step, sparing confirmation, no guess-spamming)? How does Obra Dinn confirm in threes, how does Golden Idol structure its sentences, what did players call unfair or tedious?

### Takeaway
Critics describe the click as the moment of "making a connection" between small on-screen details, and the two designers who have spoken about it (Pope via secondary sources, Klavins of Color Gray directly) both engineered the confirmation step to reward being right without letting the player test one answer at a time: Obra Dinn locks fates only in threes, Golden Idol only says "two or fewer slots are incorrect". The complaints that recur are late-game process-of-elimination (Obra Dinn's Chinese topmen), ambiguous answer wording, and repetitive input (dragging names twice in Golden Idol).

### Cited Findings

**The feeling, in critics' words**
- Obra Dinn: "the agony and joy of scrutinizing tiny details and making connections between them"; the game "makes it just difficult enough to put together its answers that it had me instantly hooked"; "Obra Dinn is not easy"; "Its puzzles are never mean or particularly tricky"; "There is enough information to figure everything out without guessing" - Chris Kohler, [Kotaku review, 18 Oct 2018](https://kotaku.com/return-of-the-obra-dinn-the-kotaku-review-1829797772)
- Obra Dinn: "I truly felt like I inhabited Obra Dinn's world because of how strongly its detective gameplay gripped me"; "The goal seems straightforward, but Obra Dinn is hard"; "While this might sound like tedious backtracking, it's just the opposite"; "Whenever you're bogged down in a problem, you always have a way out - but you may have to question your assumptions"; score 8.75 - Javy Gwaltney, [Game Informer review, 18 Oct 2018](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/return-of-the-obra-dinn/hauntingly-good)
- Obra Dinn: "Obra Dinn provides a whirlwind sort of deduction, which makes you feel smarter than you really are"; "so you're never left feeling dumb" - Antony de Fault, [Film Stories, 15 Jun 2021](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83249)
- Obra Dinn: Polygon's Colin Campbell said it "takes the whodunit's conventions and twists them into kaleidoscopic narratives" and that it made him reach for "a notepad and pen"; GameSpot's David Wildgoose called the book interface a "masterpiece of interconnected design" (both as quoted on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn); Polygon and GameSpot pages not opened)
- Her Story: "What I loved the most was making a connection, and then typing it in to see an array of new videos to watch"; "a sense of achievement for catching even the smallest threads"; the author "never spells out anything" and "much is open to interpretation"; score 8.5 - Kimberley Wallace, [Game Informer review, 26 Jun 2015](https://gameinformer.com/games/her_story/b/pc/archive/2015/06/26/her-story-review-game-informer)
- Golden Idol: "There's an immense satisfaction in feeling that you came to correct conclusions on your own"; hints made the reviewer "less frustrated" but no less clever; "Tough but rewarding fill-in-the-blanks detective gameplay"; score 90 - Tim Rattray, [RPGFan review, 19 Sep 2024](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-case-of-the-golden-idol/)
- Golden Idol: The Guardian called it a game that "often requires extravagant feats of deduction" ([inkl mirror of the Guardian review](https://www.inkl.com/news/the-case-of-the-golden-idol-review-delicious-sherlockian-murder-mystery); snippet, page not opened)
- Roottrees: the interface is built to keep players "immersed in the detecting flow state"; the reviewer felt "like an actual detective"; the main tree is "pretty daunting when you zoom out to a sea of question marks" - Tom Sykes, PC Gamer, 8 Feb 2025, [syndicated on Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/roottrees-dead-review-180121009.html)
- Roottrees: PC Gamer (UK) called the gameplay "difficult and time-consuming, but extremely satisfying"; the A.V. Club called it "one of the most concentrated doses of gaming joy we received all year" (as quoted on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roottrees_are_Dead))
- Outer Wilds: realizations were "Ah-hah!" moments that felt like major triumphs; "There's no mechanical benefit to completing anything; your reward is writing to read, and knowledge for future loops" - Kris Cornelisse, [DualShockers, 4 Feb 2020](https://www.dualshockers.com/outer-wilds-emergent-gameplay-design-opinion/)

**Obra Dinn: confirmation in threes (the mechanism)**
- "to deter guesswork, correct 'fates' are validated only in sets of three, with the exception of the last six fates discovered in a playthrough, which are validated in sets of two" until the player has logged 58 of 60 - [Wikipedia, citing Kotaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- "You have to get a trio of crew members' fates correct before the journal validates them" - [Game Informer review](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/return-of-the-obra-dinn/hauntingly-good)
- "It's the rule of three: as soon as three deaths have been accurately logged, the game pulls me out of the action"; "This is an elegant way of dealing with the issue of player solution input in deductive games"; "waiting until you get three sets of three-part entries precisely correct prevents most brute-force verification"; but it still leads to "a reasonable amount of blindly fishing for answers": "you take a stab, perhaps selecting 'Huang Li', first in the list" and "that wasn't an actual deduction"; and a side effect: "you know for sure that any fate you've entered but which didn't get verified is incorrect. Once you learn this, you can use it to cycle between possible answers to the incorrect fates" - [Film Stories, 15 Jun 2021](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83249)
- GameSpot describes the confirmation beat as the screen going dark and "Well done" appearing after the third fate is entered (search snippet of [GameSpot's best of 2018 piece](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/best-games-of-2018-return-of-the-obra-dinn/1100-6463891); page not opened)
- Game Informer's player tips: "Fates validate in threes", so a correct answer may not trigger validation at once; if two or three validation sets pass without a result, revisit your reasoning; cross-referencing the crew sketch with the memories is useful for "process of elimination for figuring out late-game fates"; "a number of them are red herrings"; "several of the fates in Obra Dinn are misleading" - Javy Gwaltney, [Game Informer tips, 18 Oct 2018](https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/18/10-spoiler-free-tips-for-solving-puzzles-in-return-of-the-obra-dinn)
- Each fate has three parts (name, cause, killer where applicable): "Some modes of death preclude a killer, such as suicide or accident, but most 'fates', as the game calls them, require these three components" (search snippet, review page not opened; see [godisageek review](https://www.godisageek.com/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/))

**Golden Idol: the sentence structure and the near-miss signal (designer's own words)**
- Early prototypes tried "construct full sentences from generic phrases"; testing favoured "here is a bunch of phrases, where do you think they fit?"; the designers kept it so "the scrolling text is not completely blank" to give grammatical and semantic context; each scenario has "a limited amount of phrases" - Andrejs Klavins, Color Gray Games, in Joel Couture, [Game Developer, 1 Nov 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- Why not confirm each word: early on players "had no idea which of their deductions were correct", which frustrated them; the team "could not validate each phrase slot", but judging the whole puzzle as right or wrong also "frustrated the players"; the fix was the indicator "two or fewer slots are incorrect", which "served the same purpose of rewarding the player for being close to the solution" and also "reduced the difficulty and length of the game" - [same Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- On brute force: "With a limited input system, an option to brute force solutions appears"; the principle they cite: "Given the opportunity, players will optimize the fun out of a game"; they chose to "settle somewhere on that spectrum" between free text and multiple choice - [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- On the click: "a good balance of steady progress and getting stuck until an insight strikes"; "Those precious 'Aha!' moments are something that we strived for"; scenes include only clues "necessary to the solution" or that create "interesting misdirections"; when scenarios were too easy "we had to obfuscate things" - [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- In play: deductions are split into categories and the game shows "an indicator when you have two or fewer mistakes in a category"; "you must accurately chronicle every event, person, and bit of miscellaneous information in a level before the game will confirm if you're correct"; hints "provide vague directions that set you down the correct path for a chosen aspect of the level's mystery" - [RPGFan review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-case-of-the-golden-idol/)
- Reviewers describe the structure as "Mad Libs-style fill-in-the-blanks puzzles" with "a single solution exists among many word combinations" (search snippets from [stackup.org](https://www.stackup.org/post/review-the-case-of-the-golden-idol) and [Geek to Geek](https://geektogeekmedia.com/geekery/reviews/case-of-the-golden-idol-review); pages not opened)

**Her Story: search as the deduction verb, no verdict**
- "Every keyword you type only brings up the top five results"; later the process "slows to a trickle" and becomes "trial-and-error" - [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/games/her_story/b/pc/archive/2015/06/26/her-story-review-game-informer)
- 271 clips; "the player can find clips by searching in the database for words from the transcriptions"; "As the player selects clips, they can enter user tags, which are then available as searchable terms"; Barlow wanted the player to be "essentially Googling"; the ending leaves open whether the twin story is true - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Story_(video_game))
- Barlow on restraint: "if I start diluting that, it's going to be more confusing for players"; "If there are loads of cool things to do outside of typing in words, then it's going to diminish that"; on the open ending: "that I'm going to let you have your own ideas about it", "That way it continues to percolate in your head" - Kimberley Wallace, [Game Informer feature, 3 Jul 2015](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2015/07/03/inside-her-story-the-indie-mystery-game-that-people-can-t-stop-analyzing.aspx)
- Barlow on balancing the script for searchability: "I had the computer calculate how well connected clips were, looking at how often words were used"; "I also did number crunching to ensure that -- on a simple level -- the script was 'well balanced'" - Alex Wawro, [Game Developer, 17 Feb 2016](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-sam-barlow-s-i-her-story-i-)

**Roottrees: locking and the hint ladder**
- "Once the player has correctly guessed the entries in a stage, they are locked onto the screen and cannot be changed"; correct optional names and portraits are locked and removed from the pool; an "Intuition" tab sorts evidence by how many clues it contains; a Notable Roottrees list flags easier relatives with stars - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roottrees_are_Dead)
- "confirming your work by locking those entries in place", rewarded with a short fade to black; Roottreemania (the sequel case) is harder partly because the name pool "is larger and doesn't shrink" - [PC Gamer via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/roottrees-dead-review-180121009.html)
- "Thankfully, the game also has a hint system if you really get stuck"; even with hints "you'll need thinking, brainstorming, and deduction"; "The controls are intuitive, and the working area and the way our tools are arranged are logical and tidy"; "You will do a lot of reading, and you'll need to pay a lot of attention and do backtracking" - Kalina Mikova, [Ladies Gamers, 19 Jan 2025](https://ladiesgamers.com/the-roottrees-are-dead-review/)

**What players and critics called unfair or tedious**
- Obra Dinn: "Some potential causes of death are so similar that it can take a little bit of guessing what the game wants you to say" - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/return-of-the-obra-dinn-the-kotaku-review-1829797772)
- Obra Dinn: a GOG review tagged "Tedious" said identifying the Chinese Topmen is the most frustrating part; a Reddit user argued the game would have been better without needing all seamen and topmen names; a defender: "sometimes there is a process of elimination but that is vastly different from brute-force guessing"; another: the game only unblurs a face once you can deduce the identity (search snippets from [GOG reviews](https://www.gog.com/en/game/return_of_the_obra_dinn?page=17) and a [patientgamers thread mirror](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/patientgamers/comments/1e4oqw1/spoilers_return_of_the_obra_dinn_is_a_great_game/ldizoe7/?context=3); pages not opened)
- Golden Idol: dragging first and last names separately "doubles the amount of mouse work needed"; the hint-unlock mini-game "gets monotonous and can be easily brute-forced" and is "tedious bloat"; checked-area nodes "are hard to distinguish" - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-case-of-the-golden-idol/)
- Golden Idol: one reviewer "lost interest pretty quickly after 4 or 5 scenarios" (search snippet, [Way Too Many Games](https://waytoomany.games/2022/10/30/review-the-case-of-the-golden-idol/), page not opened)
- Roottrees: the reviewer "lost track of the number of times" they entered married names instead of maiden names; "With such a large cast of characters, and often cursory details about their lives" he knew "a lot and very little" - [PC Gamer via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/roottrees-dead-review-180121009.html); Game Rant found it "overstays its welcome a bit" (search snippet via [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/17990/the-roottrees-are-dead); page not opened)
- Her Story: GameSkinny's negative review said "novel gameplay is not enough to carry Her Story" (search snippet, [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/her-story-review-a-truly-wasted-three-hours/); page not opened)

### Inferences (labelled as such)
- The common engine across all six: the game never tells the player which single fact is right. It tells them a bundle is right (Obra Dinn), that they are close (Golden Idol), or nothing at all (Her Story, Outer Wilds), and the lock is permanent so confirmed knowledge becomes scaffolding. For Say Again: confirm a bearing fix only when both lines are laid and the tap lands, never per line; consider confirming a batch (e.g. a position plus a call sign) rather than one fact.
- Both designers who spoke went through the same arc: per-item confirmation was rejected because it enables brute force, whole-puzzle pass/fail was rejected because it frustrates. Golden Idol's "two or fewer wrong" is the compromise that reviewers praise; Obra Dinn's threes is the other. A Morse decoder with four letter choices is a 1-in-4 guess per letter; the equivalent protection is to confirm the whole word, not the letter.
- The named failure modes to design against: answer lists with near-synonyms (Kotaku), late-game items that can only be eliminated rather than deduced (topmen), and repeated manual input of the same thing (names dragged twice). Keep answer vocabularies distinct, make the last fixes deducible from new evidence, and never ask the player to re-enter a confirmed fact.
- The sticky notes in Say Again map to Roottrees' Notes and Her Story's tags: reviewers praise these when the tool is "logical and tidy" and when the player's own connections become searchable or reusable.

### Gaps
- Lucas Pope's own stated reason for threes could not be read first hand; it is attributed through Kotaku/Wikipedia and Film Stories. The RPS piece "How a book binds the Return of the Obra Dinn" (Alex Wiltshire, 7 Nov 2018), where Pope discusses the demo and the book, was blocked (RPS and archive.org).
- Eurogamer (Donlan), RPS and Guardian review text for Obra Dinn, Golden Idol, Her Story and Roottrees could not be opened; only snippets are cited.
- No source was found where Sam Barlow explains the five-result cap in his own words; the cap is documented by Game Informer's review only.

## KQ2. Teaching without tutorials: how Papers Please, Obra Dinn and Outer Wilds teach inside the fiction; what reviewers praised about learning by doing; what they said when a mechanic was taught too late or too early

### Takeaway
All three designers chose being thrown in over being taught: Pope wanted the player "overwhelmed at first", Beachum wanted curiosity to be "the only possible reason you could have to explore", and Obra Dinn's first three fates are structured as a constrained tutorial inside the fiction. The documented failures are both of timing: Outer Wilds' cut meteor opening came before players knew the village well enough to notice it, and Golden Idol's testers had "no idea which of their deductions were correct" until the near-miss indicator was added.

### Cited Findings

**Papers, Please**
- Pope's dev notes: "I wanted the player to be overwhelmed at first and to have to feel their way through the interface and ruleset"; "The game will make it easy to go back and replay the day so you can always look at this as an implicit tutorial"; he prefers learning on the fly under pressure and was hesitant to add a tutorial - quoted in Alain Dellepiane, [localization.it, 14 Sep 2014](https://localization.it/papers-please-what-the-game-told-us/)
- A fuller version of the same note (search snippet, [TIGSource devlog via mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=4879); the TIGSource page itself returned 403): "There are a lot of good arguments for a tutorial. But as a gamer I vastly prefer to learn things on the fly, even under pressure."
- What learning feels like in play: "leafing through the rulebook and daily policy change notices"; "Things look bland, but they feel frantic as you try to make evaluations both quickly and carefully"; "you're docked pay for mistakes"; "Each document must be manually dragged from the counter to your inspection table"; "Even the way you physically array a collection of documents can obscure a crucial piece of information" - Scott Juster, [PopMatters, 2 May 2013](https://popmatters.com/171036-papers-please-2495760180.html)
- Players split on it: a Steam user said "the first few days of story mode is essentially the tutorial"; another said the pages "tell you very explicitly what you have to do if you actually read them" (search snippets from [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239030/discussions/0/34094415864011994); pages not opened)

**Return of the Obra Dinn**
- The demo lesson: the 2016 GDC demo had "only six fates for the player to deduce"; the Nov 2016 PAX Australia demo added thirteen characters whose deaths were "presented out of chronological order", which confused players; the fix was ten narrative events that split the story into chapters, which led to the logbook as the timeline - [Wikipedia, citing RPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- Pope on what the fun is: "the real fun part would be figuring out who they are"; he had first focused on how people died, then realised that was usually easy to see; crew was cut from about 80 to 60: "I felt like every time I cut it down I was going to compromise the game" - Bryant Francis, [Game Developer, 26 Nov 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/for-lucas-pope-i-return-of-the-obra-dinn-i-was-a-bunch-of-appealing-design-problems)
- How the loop teaches itself: "In the simplest iteration, you examine a corpse and are taken to a static scene"; "Before you see each death scene, you might hear a few lines of dialogue, which will give you some clues if you pay attention"; "There was only one time when I had to take an IRL note on a piece of paper" - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/return-of-the-obra-dinn-the-kotaku-review-1829797772)
- Players on the opening: "The first set is misleading, it's set up as a tutorial and doesn't quite give you the same freedom of order as you have later when the game becomes more freeform" (search snippet, [selectbutton forum](https://selectbutton.net/t/cobra-dinn-is-not-a-real-country/7414); page not opened); a Steam thread says the tutorial messages end after asking you to open the book for the first time (search snippet; the [thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/653530/discussions/0/1640926712947577448) returned only a content warning when fetched)

**Outer Wilds**
- "The goal was to make a game where the only possible reason you could have to explore was for the sake of curiosity"; "So it really focuses on knowledge being the only real thing of value in this world"; the cut opening: "Oh a thing falls from space, that'll get them curious, right?", which failed because "players weren't familiar with the village yet"; lesson: "you can't expect someone to be curious about something specific unless they're already familiar" with their surroundings; the design motivates "without heavy-handed prompts telling them to 'go here' and 'go there'" (the last is the article's wording); playtest lesson from Loan Verneau: "if you want people to care about the text they're reading, it helps if the people they're reading about are going through the same things" - Kris Graft, [Game Developer, 18 Jun 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)
- Beachum's thesis pillar: "We wanted to make sure that players are exploring because they're curious about the world, and not for any other reason"; no ship upgrades, no collectables; the final opening has the player gather launch codes and set out (search snippets from [GamesRadar making-of](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-making-of-outer-wilds) and the [thesis PDF](https://sbekumod.github.io/Assets/OWtesi.pdf); pages not opened)
- In play: "the opening section serves as a tutorial for many elements of the game you'll come to experience"; NPCs make only light suggestions such as a practice trip to the moon; revisiting the opening revealed clues "hiding in plain sight"; "You can't make the puzzles easier by upgrading your jetpack or oxygen tanks"; "Your ship computer carries over any points of interest or notes that you discovered"; "The preservation of the ship log, the marker within if there's a remaining clue" - [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/outer-wilds-emergent-gameplay-design-opinion/)
- Criticism of where the help lives: reviewers wanted the log reachable from the suit outside the ship, or as a meta log from the main menu (search snippets from reviews listed at [nintendojo](https://www.nintendojo.com/?p=204374) and [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/598515152383419065); which review said which was not resolved)

**Golden Idol (the testing method behind teaching)**
- "During the testing (which we do at least 5-7 times) we would just sit there with our mouths shut, watching them get stuck, confused, and frustrated" - [Game Developer, 1 Nov 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- "We did not rely on any hard quantitative data; for us, playtest interviews were much more important"; test "As early as possible. This was to avoid putting effort into something that didn't work"; "we knew that we had to remove that clue because it was making parts of the game superfluous"; "people would have difficulty recognizing a visual of an apple, which would need redrawing" - Joel Couture, [Game Developer, 2 Mar 2023](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving)

**Florence (no tutorial by principle)**
- "I try to make games that anyone can play"; "I prefer games that are really simple, where you don't need to learn a bunch of special moves" - Ken Wong, [Milanote deconstruction](https://www.milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence)

### Inferences (labelled as such)
- The pattern: the first task is the full loop at minimum size (six fates, one passport day, one village), inside the fiction, with the real rule set revealed by doing it. For Say Again: the first radio exchange should need the knob, the hold-to-talk and one note, with nothing else on screen yet.
- Too early is as real a failure as too late: Outer Wilds' meteor was a mechanic shown before the player had a baseline to notice the change. For Say Again, introduce Morse only after the player has heard voice traffic long enough that silence and dots feel different.
- Replayability of the lesson (Papers Please "easy to go back and replay the day") is the safety net that lets a designer skip the tutorial. A cheap "say again" to replay the last transmission is the same net in this game's own vocabulary.
- Silent playtests (Color Gray's "mouths shut") are the only cited method for finding where teaching fails.

### Gaps
- No reviewer quote was found that explicitly says a mechanic was taught "too late"; the closest are the Outer Wilds log-accessibility complaints and the Golden Idol pre-indicator frustration (from the designer, not a reviewer).
- Pope's full TIGSource Papers Please devlog (forums.tigsource.com) returned 403; quotes are via localization.it and a search snippet.

## KQ3. Mobile narrative UI: text reveal speed (praised and hated), tap-to-continue signalling, hold gestures (how long is too long), touch target sizes, reading at arm's length, and what Lifeline did with real-time waits

### Takeaway
No authoritative study of narrative text-reveal speed exists; the strongest signal is a working visual novel developer's survey where most players chose "fast-but-not-instant" and the interactive-fiction community's near-unanimous rule that timed text is only acceptable if the player has "direct, immediate control over the display rate" (Plotkin). Platform long-press defaults are 400 ms (Android source) and 0.5 s (iOS); Android's accessibility setting exists because people trigger holds by accident. Target minimums are 44 CSS px (WCAG AAA), 48 dp with 8 dp gaps (Android), 1 cm physical (NN/g). Lifeline's real-time waits were loved as "a core part of the storytelling" and quit over when the game broke its own rule with back-to-back walls of text.

### Cited Findings

**Text reveal speed and tap-to-skip**
- Developer survey: Adrienne (In Stars and Time) asked her followers which text speed they preferred; most chose "fast-but-not-instant (FBNI speed!)" and instant was relatively low; she considered forcing FBNI "as Ace Attorney does" but added options instead ("If I can add options, might as well add them"); instant text is "kind of sad to see the text appear all at once" and can kill pacing; she adds "very slight pauses" even at instant speed; players like pressing A to reveal text immediately, and she disables this at important moments to "catch the player off guard"; auto-advancing text burned streamers who read aloud and at instant speed could vanish after "literally one frame of text", so she uses auto-advance rarely, slows text there and keeps it on screen long enough to read aloud "a few times" - [In Stars and Time devlog #3](https://armor-games-studios.itch.io/in-stars-and-time/devlog/386975/devlog-3-musings-about-text-speed) (no cps figures given)
- Ren'Py engine default: `preferences.text_cps` "0 is infinite, otherwise this is the number of characters per second to show"; the docs' example sets `default preferences.text_cps = 40`; auto-forward `afm_time` default 15 and off by default - [Ren'Py preferences docs](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/preferences.html)
- Interactive fiction writers on timed text (Jan 2024 thread): Andrew Plotkin: "The only way timed text can work is if the player has direct, immediate control over the display rate"; "I've played plenty of games spamming the text-pop button with my thumb"; AmandaB: "I have HATED timed text in every single game I've ever encountered, except one"; "I almost always hate SLOWLY timed text"; "I don't want to press keys to speed up text"; "I very well might press it too fast and miss something"; mathbrush: "Timed text to me usually indicates that the author is not confident that their text provides its own pacing"; HanonO: "It's almost never fun to watch text type out one letter at a time"; "This is not a movie, this is read prose and people read at different speeds"; Pebblerubble: "So important that I would give up a game early if I'm forced to wait too long"; ramstrong: "There should be a game setting option where all text is displayed without delay" and "What I don't want to do is press twice every time. - Reading speed baselines: Nielsen assumes 200 words per minute, 250 for highly literate users; visit time rises 4.4 seconds per extra 100 words; users read at most 28% of words on an average visit, "20% is more likely" - Jakob Nielsen, [NN/g, 5 May 2008](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/); Brysbaert's 2019 meta-analysis gives 238 wpm silent and 183 wpm aloud for English (search snippet of the [paper PDF](https://reader.ku.edu/sites/reader/files/2024-01/How%20many%20words%20do%20we%20read%20per%20minute%20(1).pdf); page not opened)
- Response-time limits: 0.1 s feels instantaneous; 1.0 s keeps the train of thought but the delay is noticed; 10 s is the limit for keeping attention, beyond which feedback on remaining time is needed - Jakob Nielsen, [NN/g, 1993/2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)

### Inferences (labelled as such)
- Say Again's 22 cps is in the "fast but not instant" band the developer survey favoured (Ren'Py's doc example is 40 cps; 22 cps is roughly 4 words per second, about 260 wpm, close to the 238 wpm silent reading mean, so the type-out slightly outruns a reader who starts late). The non-negotiables from the IF thread: one tap completes the line, the same tap on a completed line advances (never "press twice"), a scroll-back exists, and the rate is a setting with an instant endpoint. Hold-to-talk typing the words out while held is the rare case where timed text is "under direct, immediate control", which is why it is defensible when ordinary narration is not.
- A hold in this game is a radio PTT, which the fiction explains; what the platform figures say is that recognition must start at or before 400 to 500 ms or it will feel broken, and that some players will trigger holds by accident, so a hold must be harmless to release early.
- The notification-driven wait only survives if the game never violates its own clock (Kritiqal) and offers a fast mode from the first ending or earlier (Short, GamesBeat). In a web game without push notifications, the honest version is short in-fiction pauses with visible reason (static, "wait one"), not real hours.
- On a 390 px phone column, 44 to 48 px targets with 8 px gaps are the floor; the knob, the key and the four Morse letter choices are the controls most at risk because they are small, repeated and sequential, exactly the cases WCAG names.

- No peer-reviewed or NN/g figure on a maximum comfortable hold duration was found; the only hold figures are recognition thresholds (400 ms, 0.5 s, 1.0 s, 1.5 s accessibility options).
- No critic review quantifies a "right" cps; Ace Attorney, Undertale and Celeste figures that circulate online were not verified and are omitted.
- Lifeline has no public postmortem found; the GameAnalytics interview is the closest. The original 2015 TouchArcade and 148Apps Lifeline reviews could not be opened.
- Apple HIG's 44 pt line is not verified first hand this session.

## KQ4. Florence and Device 6: which interface moments critics singled out, and what the designers said they cut for clarity (plus Sayonara Wild Hearts, A Normal Lost Phone, award context)

### Takeaway
Florence's speech-bubble jigsaw is the moment every critic names, and Ken Wong's cuts were all for the same reason: an interaction stayed only if it was both interactive and a metaphor the player feels; a single-puzzle argument, an only-visual test level, intimate touch scenes and a reunion chapter were all dropped. Device 6's critics single out text as map and the physical turning of the device, with puzzles "usually only requiring button presses between two options". Simogo's Sayonara chose swipe input "so that the player avatar moves when the player actually moves their finger" and "very simple shapes and distinctive colors because it's just swooshing by".

## KQ5. Accessibility and clarity: colour and contrast on small screens, how to signal "you can tap this" without a tutorial, how games mark the one thing to do next

### Takeaway
The established answer is Norman's: "what people need, and what design must provide, are signifiers", and NN/g's eye-tracking work on flat UI shows that removing them costs attention and causes "click uncertainty". The games in this set mark the next thing by withholding rather than pointing: Obra Dinn unblurs a face only once it is deducible, Roottrees locks and removes solved names from the pool, Outer Wilds keeps the ship log with a marker where "there's a remaining clue", Golden Idol shows "two or fewer" when a category is close. Contrast floors are 4.5:1 (3:1 for 18 pt or 14 pt bold text), and Apple's advice for small screens is regular-or-heavier weights at 17 pt body and never below 11 pt.

### Cited Findings

**Signifiers**
- "I call any physically perceivable cue a signifier, whether it is incidental or deliberate"; "Forget affordances: what people need, and what design must provide, are signifiers"; "Designers need to provide these clues" - Don Norman, [jnd.org, 17 Nov 2008](https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/)
- "People treat clicks like currency and they don't spend it frivolously"; "Hunting for links takes effort and people won't do it for long"; "Life is too short to click on things you don't understand" (Nielsen, quoted); "Visual cues that align with people's expectations help them quickly determine which items to click"; keep buttons button-shaped ("retain a rectangular shape, preferably with rounded corners"); do not style non-clickable items like buttons; do not use the link colour for static items; make an image and its caption one target; icons as links must be "instantly recognizable" or get a text label; arrows are "the least favorable option" - Hoa Loranger, [NN/g, 8 Mar 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clickable-elements/)
- Lack of signifiers on clickable elements is "One of the biggest usability issues introduced by flat design"; flat styles have been "slowly reducing user efficiency" and cause "click uncertainty"; a participant: "When it's blue and underlined, that's how you know initially"; recommendation: "Flat 2.0", mostly flat with subtle shadows, highlights and layers; "you are not the user" - Kate Moran, [NN/g, 27 Sep 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-design/)
- Progressive disclosure: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options", "Offer a larger set of specialized options upon request"; "the very fact that something appears on the initial display tells users that it's important"; "people understand a system better when you help them prioritize features"; "You must get the right split between initial and secondary features" - Jakob Nielsen, [NN/g, 3 Dec 2006](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
- Hidden gestures (long press, swipe) are "systematic patterns, not edge cases" and have no visible cue (search snippet, [GhostUI, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19258); page not opened); a suggested fallback is a long press that opens a small menu plus "a brief peek animation on first load" (search snippet, [dolfy.ai](https://www.dolfy.ai/blog/swipe-to-delete-gesture-discoverability-mobile-apps); page not opened; low-authority source)

**Contrast and size on small screens**
- 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold); thin or unusual fonts may need extra contrast; 4.499:1 fails - [W3C SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- iOS default 17 pt, minimum 11 pt; "avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light font weights, which can be difficult to see, especially when text is small"; "using a larger type size, increasing contrast by modifying the text or background colors, or using typefaces designed for optimized legibility" when text is hard to read - [Apple HIG typography JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.json)
- Test link colours for colourblindness; blue is "the safest link color" but others work if they stand out from body text - [NN/g clickable elements](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clickable-elements/)
- Flesser on readable-at-speed art: "very simple shapes and distinctive colors because it's just swooshing by" - [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=33kvkagk)
- Golden Idol's checked-area nodes "are hard to distinguish" (a cited small-signifier failure) - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-case-of-the-golden-idol/)

**How the games mark the one thing to do next (without a tutorial)**
- Obra Dinn: the game only unblurs a crew member's face when you have the ability to deduce their identity (search snippet, [patientgamers mirror](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/patientgamers/comments/1e4oqw1/spoilers_return_of_the_obra_dinn_is_a_great_game/ldizoe7/?context=3); page not opened); the journal gives the general area of each chapter's first memory and the flies lead to it - [Game Informer tips](https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/18/10-spoiler-free-tips-for-solving-puzzles-in-return-of-the-obra-dinn)
- Outer Wilds: "The preservation of the ship log, the marker within if there's a remaining clue"; NPCs offer only light suggestions - [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/outer-wilds-emergent-gameplay-design-opinion/); the team avoided "heavy-handed prompts telling them to 'go here' and 'go there'" - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/live-die-repeat-how-i-outer-wilds-i-piques-curiosity-in-an-ambivalent-solar-system)
- Roottrees: solved entries are locked and removed from the pool; the Intuition tab orders evidence by clue count; stars mark easier relatives - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roottrees_are_Dead); a short fade to black marks a confirmed section - [PC Gamer via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/roottrees-dead-review-180121009.html)
- Golden Idol: "an indicator when you have two or fewer mistakes in a category"; hints give "vague directions" per aspect - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/the-case-of-the-golden-idol/)
- Her Story: each clip names new people, places and things to search, which the reviewer found rewarding; tags become searchable terms - [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/her_story/b/pc/archive/2015/06/26/her-story-review-game-informer), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Story_(video_game))
- Device 6: interactivity is conveyed by the text itself moving as you scroll and the device needing to be turned; puzzles are "button presses between two options" - [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/device_6/b/ios/archive/2013/10/29/the-next-step-in-interactive-fiction)
- A Normal Lost Phone: puzzles "tease out new functionality, like a Wi-Fi connection"; questions raised in one app are answered "deeper in the phone" - [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/46575)
- Florence: the first interaction of each chapter is the chapter's metaphor (jigsaw, toothbrush, boxes); "There's intentionally not much animation in the game" so the interactive thing is the thing that moves - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)

### Inferences (labelled as such)
- For a phone column with a drawn radio: the knob, PTT and key need "Flat 2.0" signifiers (a shadow, a bevel, a highlight on the one live control) because they are not standard web controls; the thing to do next should be the only thing with motion or the only unlocked control (Florence's rule), not an arrow.
- The "withhold until deducible" pattern (Obra Dinn's blurred faces, Roottrees' shrinking pool) is the cheapest way to mark the next step without a prompt: keep the chart's crossing tap disabled until two lines exist; keep the sticky-note field inert until a fact has been heard.
- A hold gesture has no signifier by nature; the fallback cited in the literature is a first-load peek animation plus a visible label ("hold to talk"), and a tap on the PTT should do something harmless (a click of static) rather than nothing.
- Contrast on the chart: bearing lines and the crossing marker are non-text graphics; aim for 3:1 against the sea colour at minimum and check in greyscale, since colour alone is the one signifier NN/g warns is unsafe for colourblind players.
