# Life stories and memory stories: structure, interaction, endings

Scope: how interactive stories about a whole life, or about memory, hook the player, make the player feel they are the character, use small mechanics and phone-message interfaces, and end. Target: a second-person, phone-first (390 px, iPhone Safari) web story about "your" life from birth to old age, with a "life in lines" view and the thread "text me when you get home".

Method note: WebSearch and WebFetch only. Where a point comes from a search-engine summary of a page rather than a page I read in full, it says "per search summary". Quotes are kept under 15 words, one at most per source.

## 1. "Your life in lines" (@lessonsinline) and related life visualisations

### Takeaway
The @lessonsinline account exists (Instagram title "Lessons in Line (@lessonsinline)"), but nothing about its maker, its numbers or its format could be verified from the open web; the format description in the brief stays the only description. The closest documented relatives are Jason Rohrer's Passage (the screen width is the lifetime, the past piles up behind you), Tim Urban's "Your Life in Weeks" and "The Tail End" (most time with parents is spent before you leave home), and Channel 4's "People in Order: Age".

### Cited Findings
- The Instagram profile page returns the title "Lessons in Line (@lessonsinline) - Instagram photos and videos"; the page served to a crawler holds no bio, follower count or captions - [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/lessonsinline/)
- The YouTube handle URL redirects to a consent wall and the TikTok URL returns only a generic title, so neither could be read - [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@lessonsinline); [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@lessonsinline)
- Searches for "lessonsinline", "Lessons in Line" and "your life in lines" returned no articles, interviews or press about the account (five query variants, standard and extended search). An unrelated YouTube playlist called "Life in Lines" (animated life-lesson videos) appeared, not confirmed as the same maker - [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZ6hfog2g4Qs)
- Tim Urban, "Your Life in Weeks" (2014): one row per year, a 90-year life drawn as 4,680 week boxes on one page; he calls each week precious and suggests a physical "Life Calendar" to mark turning points and chapters - [Wait But Why](https://waitbutwhy.com/2014/05/life-weeks.html)
- Tim Urban, "The Tail End" (2015): at 34 he counts what is left if he lives to 90 (about 60 winters, about 300 books at five a year, about 15 more elections). Key number: he spent nearly every day with his parents until 18, now sees them about 10 days a year, and had used up about 93% of his in-person time with them by the end of high school. Similar math leaves about 15% of his time with his sisters and about 7% with high school friends - [Wait But Why](https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/12/the-tail-end.html)
- The Tail End closes with three takeaways: live near the people you love, your relationships reflect your priorities so set them on purpose, treat limited time with someone as precious - [Wait But Why](https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/12/the-tail-end.html)
- Passage (Rohrer, 2007): the wide screen is a lifetime; young, you see the hazy future ahead and nothing behind; in middle age memories pile up behind you; near the end you see little future and a lifetime behind. The view narrows with age - [Rohrer, Passage creator's statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- "People in Order: Age" (Lenka Clayton and James Price, Channel 4, 2006): 100 people lined up from age 1 to 100; viewers spot someone their own age or a relative's age - [King Richard's school page, per search summary](https://www.kingrichards.leicester.sch.uk/10221-people-in-order)

### Inferences
- The lines view and Passage share one idea: the trail behind the dot is memory, the empty space ahead is the future. Rule: draw the trail to the left of "you", let it grow; keep the right side empty and slightly hazy (Passage's blurred future), so the screen itself ages.
- The Tail End gives a design-ready, sourced fact for the parents' lines: they run beside yours from the start and most of the shared time is spent before you leave home. Rule: let the parents' lines sit close to yours in childhood and visibly drift (thinner, further, dimmer) after the leaving-home memory, long before they end. The gap between "still alive" and "still near" is the emotional content, and it sets up the night nobody texts.
- Week grids and counts work as a cold statistic; the lines view works as a picture of people. Use the age counter as the only number on screen; avoid percentages or "time left" counters in the story itself (they read as a lecture, and The Tail End already exists).
- Credit: if the story's lines view is openly inspired by @lessonsinline, credit the account by handle in the about/credits page; its maker's name could not be found.

### Gaps
- Who makes @lessonsinline, when it started, follower counts, video lengths, the exact pacing of ages, how people enter and leave, and whether there is a YouTube or TikTok counterpart: not verifiable via WebSearch/WebFetch (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube pages are not readable without a browser). Everything about the format beyond the brief is unverified.
- No article or interview about the account was found.

## 2. Games that tell a whole life or memories: the decisions that made them land

### Takeaway
The works that land do it by making one small mechanic carry the emotion and then changing or reversing that mechanic at the key moment (Florence's conversation puzzles, Brothers' left trigger, Edith Finch's right hand, Passage's spouse who makes you less agile, Before Your Eyes' blink). Memory stories also lean on a frame (a ferryman, two doctors, a found phone, boxes) that explains why we are moving through a life out of order, and on restraint: little or no text, simple art so the player projects their own life.

### Cited Findings

Passage (Jason Rohrer, 2007)
- A whole life in about five minutes, written after a close friend's death and as Rohrer turned 30; he calls it a memento mori game and says players' readings matter more than his intent - [Rohrer statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- The spouse is optional, met near the start; once joined, you must travel together, are less agile, and some chests next to you become unreachable; exploring together scores double. If she dies, grief slows you. Chests and exploration are balanced so no path is optimal; score shows above a tombstone - [Rohrer statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- Death happens once, at the end, cannot be avoided, a deliberate contrast to games where you die repeatedly - [Rohrer statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- Passage is in MoMA's permanent collection - [Music & Literature](https://www.musicandliterature.org/features/2014/2/27/jason-rohrers-passage)

Florence (Mountains / Ken Wong, 2018)
- 20 chapters grouped into six acts, linear, about 30 minutes, little written dialogue; opens on a 25-year-old's monotonous routine of work and social media; one morning her phone dies and she follows a cello to Krish - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- Linearity was chosen partly so players with more life experience would not simply make the "correct" choices - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- Speech-bubble shapes stand for conversation; when they argue, the bubbles change to show they "talk to each other differently" (Wong) - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)
- Players act events out instead of tapping through text: dragging, tapping to remove things, placing items on a shared shelf, shaking Polaroids; taking items off the shelf marks the turn - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)
- Music replaces voices: cello for Krish, piano for Florence; simple comic art and restrained palette so players project their own experience - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)
- Puzzle pieces per bubble drop as the couple gets comfortable: six on the first date, then four, three, two - [GameSpew](https://www.gamespew.com/2020/02/florence-review/)
- In the fights insults come easily and the puzzles are quick, reflecting careless words - [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2023/03/14/interactive-graphic-novel-florence-is-the-perfect-heartbreak-for-2-99/); in heated exchanges Krish builds his bubbles at a pace that pushes you to finish first to be heard - [Fandom, per search summary; the page returned HTTP 402](https://www.fandom.com/articles/florence)
- Arguments shown with grey clothing and red accents - [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/florence/b/ios/archive/2018/02/20/florence-game-informer-review.aspx?amp)
- Began as a 3D puzzle of human heads, became 2D puzzles, then a series of touch mini-games (a cross between WarioWare and interactive comic books, per search summary); the team asked what all the touch-screen verbs were - [Game Informer GDC 2019](https://gameinformer.com/gdc-2019/2019/03/20/florence-started-development-as-3d-rubiks-cube-of-human-heads); iterated each mechanic until it served the feeling, playtested with people who were not told what the game was - [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-florence-postmortem)
- Commercially modest despite awards (Apple Design Award, BAFTA, Game Awards Best Mobile); 88% of sales on iOS - [Game Informer GDC 2019](https://gameinformer.com/gdc-2019/2019/03/20/florence-started-development-as-3d-rubiks-cube-of-human-heads); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- The ending drew the main criticism (art winning over the relationship; one cliché swapped for another) - [Wikipedia, citing Destructoid and Eurogamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- Context: a lead developer accused Wong of emotional abuse during development; he apologised publicly - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))

Before Your Eyes (GoodbyeWorld Games, 2021)
- A blink (via webcam) is the only way forward; scenes do not end while your eyes stay open; staring can reveal extra context; key story beats were put early in each scene - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)
- A metronome icon at the bottom warns that the next blink ends the scene; per search summary it was added because players could otherwise skip scenes by keeping their eyes shut and needed a way to rest - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes); [Pocket-lint, per search summary](https://www.pocket-lint.com/games/news/156365-before-your-eyes-blinking-game-goodbye-world-interview)
- Designers asked how to tell a coherent story "even if you skip most of it"; some scenes were lengthened with ad-libbed lines because testers found them too short; eye strain is intentional, the pain pushes you to let go - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)
- Team stated the point is inevitability: the story is about accepting that time moves on (Parkes) - [Prima Games, per search summary](https://primagames.com/feature/before-your-eyes-interview-goodbyeworld-games)
- Crying causes a flood of unintended blinks; handled by tracking more facial points - [Prima Games / Pocket-lint, per search summary](https://primagames.com/feature/before-your-eyes-interview-goodbyeworld-games)
- Frame: Benny's soul, unable to speak, is pulled from a dark sea by a Ferryman who reads his blinks; mid-game the "successful painter" life is revealed as a lie, the Ferryman makes you replay honestly, and the real life (terminal illness, a short life) comes out; the ending centres on his mother telling his story - [Wikipedia / TV Tropes, per search summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes)
- Reviewed as a game that pushes players to be more present - [NYU News](https://nyunews.com/arts/videogames/2021/04/23/before-your-eyes-review/)

To the Moon, Finding Paradise, Impostor Factory (Kan Gao / Freebird Games)
- To the Moon: two doctors move through a dying man's memories from old age back to childhood to find why he wants to go to the moon; started in 2010 when Gao's grandfather fell seriously ill - [YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan_Gao)
- The trip backward is framed as a question of identity: is a person just memories that could be changed - [Adventure Classic Gaming](https://adventureclassicgaming.com/index.php/site/interviews/712)
- Loop: click objects in a memory to collect "memory links", feed them to a memento (an object that ties two memories), solve a tile puzzle, jump further back - [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/to-the-moon-review); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/to_the_moon)
- Neil is the comic foil who mocks the cheesy moments; tone swings from light to bitter within scenes - [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/to_the_moon); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/to-the-moon-review)
- Simple art leaves room for music to carry the mood (Gao) - [YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)
- Split verdict on interaction: minimal puzzles keep the story flowing vs tile puzzles feel like a needless distraction - [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/52620/to-the-moon-switch-review); [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/to-the-moon-review/)
- Impostor Factory: called a fitting finale with loose ends (RPGFan 91) and an underwhelming story (New Game Network); fans read the layered simulation as reframing the earlier games, which is speculation - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/impostor-factory); [New Game Network](https://www.newgamenetwork.com/?p=118928); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1182620/discussions/0/2952663522489805002)

What Remains of Edith Finch (Giant Sparrow, 2017)
- Lewis's story (triggered by reading his therapist's letter) is set in a fish cannery; a critique notes that the repetitive motion you have been doing with your right hand becomes what kills him, and the reversal carries the weight of the slow hour before it - [Mechanics of Magic, per search summary](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/04/23/critical-play-walking-simulators-123/); [RPGFan narrative analysis](https://www.rpgfan.com/feature/narrative-design-analysis-what-remains-of-edith-finch/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/what-remains-of-edith-finch-perfectly-illustrates-gamings-storytelling-power/)

Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons (Starbreeze / Josef Fares, 2013)
- One controller, one stick and trigger per brother. After the older brother dies, the younger one, who could not swim, must cross water: holding the left trigger, the older brother's input, makes him paddle. Kotaku calls it a payoff built over the whole game - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/brothers-tale-of-two-sons-remake-ending-older-younger-1851302128); [Game Developer, "All the Feels in One Input"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/brothers-all-the-feels-in-one-input)

Spiritfarer (Thunder Lotus, 2020)
- Goal: talk about dying "in a cozy and wholesome way"; team met patients and caregivers in end-of-life care; spirits built from people the team had lost; the hug is a core verb to make care mechanical; the Everdoor farewell is a bridge meant to be neither frightening nor too whimsical - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-)
- You learn each spirit's history before you take them to the Everdoor, and the loss stays even as new friendships ease it - [Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/spiritfarer-a-game-of-hugging-and-letting-go-is-perfect-for-right-now)

Gris (Nomada Studio, 2018)
- Starts in black and white; each chapter unlocks a colour (red, green, blue, yellow, then full); colour discovery was a mechanic from the start (Roset). The colour-to-grief-stage mapping varies by source and is interpretive - [Gamereactor interview](https://msn.gamereactor.eu/what-does-gris-mean-we-talk-to-nomada-studio/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gris)

Old Man's Journey (Broken Rules, 2017)
- Wordless: an old man sets out after a letter (never shown); you reshape hills to build his path; memories surface in landscape paintings; themes of loss, regret, reconnecting with family; critiques: short, little interaction - [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/old_mans_journey/b/ios/archive/2017/05/31/a-poignant-adventure-through-memory.aspx); [Troy Media](https://troymedia.com/?p=116769); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/46479)

A Short Hike (adamgreenbaum, 2019)
- Claire waits for a call; reception only at the top of Hawk Peak. At the summit her mother calls, admits she had surgery, says she is proud; an updraft rises and her mother tells her to ride it - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)
- The call is planted at the start without urgency; players forget it among side errands; the ending reframes the wandering as Claire working up to the call - [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/05/06/i-forgot-i-was-waiting-on-a-phone-call/)

Journey (thatgamecompany, 2012)
- Built on a three-act film structure and a planned emotional arc (Kellee Santiago); pacing judged by feel, the way a film editor works - [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/kellee-santiago-describes-the-making-of-thatgamecompanys-journey/); prototyping began with the music - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-)
- Strangers can travel together but cannot talk or see names until after the credits - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game))

Emily is Away (Kyle Seeley, 2015)
- Five chat conversations, one per year, 2002 to 2006 (high school senior to college senior), in a Windows XP / AIM-style client; away messages, icons and bios change between years to show time passing; Emily logs off each time, and in the last year, for the first time, you log off - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)
- In 2006 the protagonist deletes earnest questions and falls back on talk about the weather - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)

A Normal Lost Phone (Accidental Queens, 2017)
- Played entirely as a found phone: messages, photos, calendar, a social app; you learn who the owner is by reading it; made at the 2016 Global Game Jam - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Normal_Lost_Phone); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-indie-studio-accidental-queens-emerged-from-a-game-jam)

Unpacking (Witch Beam, 2021)
- A life told through about 1,000 objects over seven moves, no characters on screen; "No item? No trait!"; item order shows progression; the diploma that finds no place in a partner's flat (over the toilet, under the bed) flags a relationship that will not last; the final house has a nursery - [Game Developer, GDC 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)
- The team accepted that events between moves cannot be shown; players fill the gaps - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items); Brier later spoke on designing without fail states or scores - [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/comments/9269)

Bury me, my Love (The Pixel Hunt, 2017)
- You are Majd, texting your wife Nour as she flees Syria; choices of reply shape her route; over 100,000 words, written in Ink; several endings - [CMF interview](https://cmf-fmc.ca/?p=69550)
- Real-time mode: messages arrive at intervals that can stretch for hours, pushed as notifications among your real contacts; a fast mode removes the waits - [CGM press release, per search summary](https://www.cgmagonline.com/newswire/syrian-refugee-story-bury-love-launch-ios-android-october-26); [GameLuster](https://gameluster.com/bury-me-my-love-the-story-of-a-syrian-refugee/)
- Maurin's GDC talk was about confronting players with helplessness through lack of information and loss of control - [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024942/contactUs)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern across Florence, Brothers, Edith Finch and Passage: teach one gesture early, let it become habit, then change what it means. For this story the gesture already exists: the "text me when you get home" message. Rule: early on the player receives it and must tap a reply (the chore); in the middle the player waits for it and it never comes; at the end the player types it, with the same composer and the same keyboard animation the parents' messages used. That is the Brothers left-trigger move in phone form.
- Florence shows a mechanic can track a relationship without words. Rule: let one interaction get easier as closeness grows (fewer taps, shorter waits, auto-filled replies with a partner), then break turn-taking in conflict (the other side types over you).
- Passage's spouse rule maps to the lines view: when a partner's line joins yours, the pair moves as one and some side paths (dots that were reachable) drift out of reach. A choice that costs something, shown not told.
- Before Your Eyes' lesson for a tap-to-advance story: put each scene's key beat first, so a player who taps fast still gets the story; lingering (not tapping) can reveal one extra line. Never lock the story behind lingering.
- To the Moon's mixed reception on puzzles: interaction that is not about the feeling reads as a chore. Every tap should be a thing a person in that memory would do (hold a hand, close a door, hit send), not a puzzle.
- Unpacking and Old Man's Journey show that objects and landscape can carry years the story skips. Rule: show time jumps with a changed object (the same phone, a newer model; the same kitchen, a different mug), not with captions.
- Humour matters in long sad arcs (Neil in To the Moon). The parent's messages can be funny (caps lock, wrong emoji, "ok" with a full stop) so the later silence hurts more.

## 3. The first minute and the ending

### Takeaway
The best openings put the player inside an ordinary, slightly sad routine or a strange frame and plant one small, unexplained thread (a phone that dies, a call that is coming, a ferryman listening) instead of explaining. The best endings pay that thread off and reframe what the player already did (A Short Hike's call, Before Your Eyes' mother telling the story, Emily is Away's first log-off by the player). Endings that send players back to their real lives work when players do it on their own (people wrote "call your mom" essays after A Short Hike); critics call it manipulation when the emotion serves only itself or relies on fake-outs.

### Cited Findings
- Florence opens on a monotonous routine of work and social media; the inciting event is that her phone dies and she follows a sound - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- A Short Hike plants the call in its opening with no urgency; the player forgets it; the ending ties to Claire being ready, not to finishing a task - [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/05/06/i-forgot-i-was-waiting-on-a-phone-call/)
- Before Your Eyes opens after death, in a boat, with a listener who judges the life; the middle reveals the told life was false and makes you replay it - [Wikipedia / TV Tropes, per search summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes)
- To the Moon starts with the man dying and goes backward to find the reason for his wish - [YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)
- Old Man's Journey starts with a letter whose content is never shown - [Troy Media / reviews, per search summary](https://troymedia.com/?p=116769)
- Emily is Away ends with the player logging off for the first time after four years of Emily doing it - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)
- Journey reveals the strangers' names only after the credits - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game))
- Brothers' last playable moment reuses the dead brother's input - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/brothers-tale-of-two-sons-remake-ending-older-younger-1851302128)
- A player essay on A Short Hike ends by telling readers to call their mothers; itch.io comments on other games describe endings as reminders to call one's mom - [Blog essay](https://accordion-sprout-l88t.squarespace.com/blog/a-short-hike-or-another-great-reason-to-call-your-mom); [itch.io comment, per search summary](https://itch.io/post/836249)
- The Tail End ends with explicit takeaways (live near people, set priorities, treat the time as precious) - [Wait But Why](https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/12/the-tail-end.html)
- Critique: PopMatters calls The Last of Us emotional manipulation where characters are moved like pawns to produce tragedy, cites Cocteau on "sentimental blackmail", and concedes all art manipulates; the question is whether the feeling serves a purpose - [PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/173815--2495737897.html)
- "Emotional" endings that fell flat are often twist or fake-out deaths that undercut grief - [WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/gaming/10-emotional-video-game-endings-that-fell-completely-flat)
- Florence's ending was its most criticised part - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- "Text me when you get home" is the title of Kayleen Schaefer's 2018 book on female friendship; she found that every woman she asked says it, and frames it as safety but more as solidarity, knowing what it is like to walk into an empty apartment - [TPR / NPR interview](https://www.tpr.org/2018-02-11/text-me-when-you-get-home); [The Dartmouth](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2018/04/text-me-when-you-get-home-celebrates-female-friendship)

### Inferences
- Opening rule (from Florence and A Short Hike): no title card, no instructions. First screen: a phone lock screen at night with one notification, "text me when you get home", from Mom; the player's first tap is to dismiss or answer it. The thread is planted in the first five seconds and nothing explains it.
- Starting at the end works for memory stories (To the Moon, Before Your Eyes). Option: open on an old hand holding the phone, the composer half-typed, then cut to birth. Risk: it gives the ending away. Softer option: show only the lock-screen time and an unsent draft with no visible text, and reveal the words at the end.
- Ending rule (from Emily is Away, Brothers): the last action is the player's, and it reverses a role. The player types the message they used to receive, to someone whose line started near the end of theirs. Do not show the reply. The not-knowing is the point the night-nobody-texts scene set up.
- A Short Hike's lesson for the arc: the thread can disappear for long stretches (teenage years, work years) as long as it returns at the right moments; forgetting it is part of the design.
- Call to action: the evidence suggests players act on their own when the ending earns it, and the PopMatters line of critique punishes being told what to feel. Rule: no "now go text your mom" button, no guilt line, no stats screen. If anything follows the ending, keep it quiet and optional, for example a blank screen with the phone's real-time clock, or a last line that is the player's own lock screen. Any share or "send this to someone" prompt should be off the ending screen.
- Schaefer's book shows the phrase is widely shared and not only parent-to-child; it is also friend-to-friend. The middle of the story can use a friend sending it, which widens the thread and keeps it from being only about parents.

### Gaps
- No interview found where a designer of these games talks about deliberately adding a real-world call to action and measuring whether players acted on it.

## 4. Making the player feel they are the character

### Takeaway
The player becomes the character through their own small physical actions (blinking, holding, typing, choosing a reply), through minimal or absent faces (Unpacking shows no one, Florence's art is deliberately simple, Journey hides names), and through losses the player causes or cannot stop (Before Your Eyes' blinks skip moments; Passage's spouse costs agility). Waiting and silence work when the interface already taught the player what a reply looks like (Bury me, my Love, Lifeline).

### Cited Findings
- Before Your Eyes makes the player's body move time: a blink ends the scene, the strain of holding your eyes open pushes you to let go; you miss things whether you want to or not - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)
- Unpacking puts no character on screen; the player infers the person from what they place, and the gaps are filled by the player - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)
- Florence uses simple art and restrained colour so players project their own experience; music replaces dialogue so text does not get in the way of feeling - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)
- Florence's linear path keeps experienced players from simply picking the right answers - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- Bury me, my Love casts the player as the person at home holding the phone; no tutorial is needed because people already know how to text (Maurin) - [CMF interview](https://cmf-fmc.ca/?p=69550); its design aimed at helplessness via lack of information and loss of control - [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024942/contactUs)
- Lifeline paused its story until a notification said the character had woken up, so players lived the story in real time - [CMF interview](https://cmf-fmc.ca/?p=69550)
- In Bury me, a six-hour gap in the story might become a couple of hours of anxious checking of your phone - [GameLuster, per search summary](https://gameluster.com/bury-me-my-love-the-story-of-a-syrian-refugee/)
- Emily is Away makes the player mash keys to "type" the chosen line; the character types, deletes and retypes, sometimes sending something softer or different from what you picked - [Medium review](https://medium.com/mammon-machine-zeal/emily-is-away-a-review-4e609247ca91); [Honest Gamers](https://www.honestgamers.com/13261/pc/emily-is-away/review.html); [Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2015/10/16/no-longer-if-comp-2015-emily-is-away/)
- Passage: joining the spouse is a choice with a mechanical cost and a mechanical gift; grief after her death slows you down - [Rohrer statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- Spiritfarer makes care a verb (the hug) and makes you the one who rows each friend to the farewell - [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-); [Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/spiritfarer-a-game-of-hugging-and-letting-go-is-perfect-for-right-now)
- Journey's companions are faceless and nameless during play - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game))
- The typing indicator signals presence without content; when it vanishes it reads as an unfinished promise - [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2016/05/20/tech/typing-awareness-indicators-and-a-certain-kind-of-chat-app-hell-4df2cd948001); [Substack history, per search summary](https://aliveinsocialmedia.substack.com/p/a-story-of-three-dots-and-typing)

### Inferences
- Second person plus a faceless "you": draw "you" as a figure seen from behind or only as hands and the phone (Firewatch-style flat art suits this: Firewatch keeps its protagonist mostly off-screen too, though that was not researched here). Draw other people with faces but simple ones (Florence's reason: room to project).
- Physical verbs on a phone, each tied to a real gesture: hold to stay (a hug, a hand), tap to reply, swipe to dismiss a notification, press and hold the send button, do nothing to let a moment pass. Keep the verb set small (four or five) and reuse them so the final reuse lands.
- Absence as a mechanic: the night nobody sends it. The player has learned that after a night-out scene a notification arrives. This time the screen stays on the lock screen, the clock moves, nothing comes. Give the player nothing to tap for a few seconds (Bury me's waiting, compressed). Then the next memory. Do not explain.
- Let the player miss one thing on purpose, Before Your Eyes style: a call from a grandparent arrives while the player is in another scene (a notification banner slides in and out); if not tapped in time it is gone, and the grandparent's line ends a few years later. The story must not punish either choice in text; the lines view shows it.
- The Emily is Away type-delete-retype is the most direct way to show what the character means but does not say. Use it for the teenage reply to the parent ("ok" typed after deleting a longer sentence) and for the adult message to the parent that is never sent.
- Choices should be few and should cost something visible (Passage). One or two real choices across the life are enough; the rest is linear (Florence's reason).

### Gaps
- No source read on Firewatch's design (avatar, second-person feel); the Firewatch comparison above is from general knowledge, not verified here.
- No data on how players react to deliberately "dead" seconds on mobile web (whether they think it is broken). This needs a playtest; a subtle cue (the lock-screen clock ticking) is the usual guard but that is an assumption.

## 5. Pacing for a 20 to 40 minute phone story

### Takeaway
Florence, the closest phone-native reference, is 20 chapters in six acts in about 30 minutes, so about 1.5 minutes per chapter. Mobile play sessions are short (median around 4 to 6 minutes per session in GameAnalytics data), so a 20 to 40 minute story will be played across several sittings by many players and must save after every chapter. Interaction should be in every scene, but each interaction must mean something in the story.

### Cited Findings
- Florence: 20 chapters, six acts, about 30 minutes per playthrough - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game)); a reviewer notes you could race through it in under an hour - [The Guardian via inkl, per search summary](https://www.inkl.com/news/florence-review-powerfully-relatable-snapshots-of-first-love)
- Florence's GDC postmortem argued that making short games is not a bad thing - [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-florence-postmortem)
- Passage: a whole life in about five minutes - [Rohrer statement](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)
- Emily is Away: five conversations, one per year; reviewers praised it but noted it is short - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)
- Before Your Eyes keeps scenes short to mirror fading memory, but lengthened some after testers found them too short - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)
- Journey's pacing was set by feel against a three-act arc, not to a target runtime - [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/kellee-santiago-describes-the-making-of-thatgamecompanys-journey/)
- GameAnalytics 2025 report (2024 data): top-tier mobile games average 8 to 9 minute sessions, median tier 5 to 6; another 2024 snapshot gives a global median of 4.45 minutes; sessions have been shrinking (about 30 seconds shorter across many genres in 2019) - [GameDevReports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks); [GameRefinery on 2019 report](https://www.gamerefinery.com/5-key-takeaways-from-gameanalytics-h1-2019-mobile-benchmarks-report/)
- Bury me, my Love offers a fast mode that removes real-time waits, alongside the real-time mode - [CGM press release, per search summary](https://www.cgmagonline.com/newswire/syrian-refugee-story-bury-love-launch-ios-android-october-26)
- To the Moon: critics split on whether its puzzles interrupt or pace the story - [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/52620/to-the-moon-switch-review); [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/to-the-moon-review/)
- Unpacking: the team wanted every single item to add to the story - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)

### Inferences
- Shape: about 15 to 25 memories (beats), each 1 to 2 minutes, grouped into 5 or 6 life acts (childhood, teens, leaving home, partner and work, parent, old age), each act about 4 to 7 minutes, so one act fits one mobile session. The lines view runs between acts (and briefly between memories) as the breather and the clock.
- Interaction density: at least one meaningful tap per memory, ideally the memory's key beat; no screen should need more than a few seconds of reading before the next tap. Put the key beat first in each memory (Before Your Eyes).
- Save after every memory in localStorage, wrapped in try/catch (private mode on iOS Safari can throw). On return, open on the lines view at the saved age with a single "continue" tap, so the re-entry itself re-orients the player. This is a design inference; the Safari storage caveat is general web practice, not researched here.
- Waiting: use real seconds, never real hours. Bury me's hours-long real-time mode needs push notifications a web page does not have, and even Bury me offered a skip. Silence of 3 to 8 seconds is the web equivalent (an assumption to playtest).
- Drop-off control: the thread is the hook across sittings; end each act on an open message (an unread notification, a typing indicator that stops) so the next session starts with a reason.
- Short is fine: Florence won major awards at 30 minutes, and its postmortem defended short games. Aim for 25 to 30 minutes, cut before adding.

### Gaps
- No completion-rate data for short narrative mobile games or web stories (Florence, Bury me, Emily is Away do not publish completion rates in what I found). Steam achievement rates could be checked in a browser but were not accessible here.
- No source on the ideal number of interactions per minute for narrative mobile games.

## 6. Phone-message scenes: concrete UI notes

### Takeaway
Players already know the grammar of a messaging app (Maurin: if you have texted, you know how to play), so the UI can carry story through its native signals: the typing indicator that appears and disappears, the delayed reply, the timestamp gap, the away message that changes, the text that is typed and deleted, and the log-off. Emily is Away proves a single chat window can cover four years by changing small details between conversations.

### Cited Findings
- No tutorial needed for a messaging story because players already know how to text (Maurin) - [CMF interview](https://cmf-fmc.ca/?p=69550)
- Emily is Away: Seeley kept the old AIM look but added a "is typing..." line the original client lacked, because it creates engagement and narrative possibilities - [We Got This Covered](https://wegotthiscovered.com/gaming/interview-kyle-seeley-emily-is-away/2)
- Emily is Away: you pick one of three lines, then mash keys to type it; the character types, deletes and retypes, sometimes sending a softer or different line; sometimes two different choices end in the same "goodbye" - [Medium](https://medium.com/mammon-machine-zeal/emily-is-away-a-review-4e609247ca91); [Honest Gamers](https://www.honestgamers.com/13261/pc/emily-is-away/review.html); [Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2015/10/16/no-longer-if-comp-2015-emily-is-away/)
- Emily is Away: one conversation per year; away messages, icons and bios change between years to show time; the final year has the protagonist deleting earnest questions for small talk - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)
- Bury me, my Love: messages from Nour arrive as notifications among the player's real contacts, at intervals; story time gaps are compressed into real waiting - [GameLuster, per search summary](https://gameluster.com/bury-me-my-love-the-story-of-a-syrian-refugee/)
- Florence: conversation shown as speech-bubble puzzles with no readable words; pieces per bubble fall as intimacy grows; in arguments the other side builds faster - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love); [GameSpew](https://www.gamespew.com/2020/02/florence-review/); [Fandom, per search summary](https://www.fandom.com/articles/florence)
- Florence's opening shows her on her phone in a social-media routine, and her phone dying is what opens her life up - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_(video_game))
- A Normal Lost Phone: the whole story is a phone UI (messages, photos, calendar, a social app) explored by the player - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Normal_Lost_Phone)
- Typing indicator history: traced to late-1990s chat clients; sources disagree on the first (IBM developers vs MSN Messenger's David Auerbach); it signals that something is being done without saying what, builds anticipation and often disappoints; it can feed anxiety; Signal ships it optional and off by default - [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2016/05/20/tech/typing-awareness-indicators-and-a-certain-kind-of-chat-app-hell-4df2cd948001); [Substack, per search summary](https://aliveinsocialmedia.substack.com/p/a-story-of-three-dots-and-typing); [inkl, per search summary](https://www.inkl.com/news/why-do-we-feel-awkward-when-someone-is-typing-and-then-stops)

### Inferences
- One thread, many years: keep a single chat thread with Mom (and later Dad) that persists across the whole story, as Emily is Away keeps one window. Each visit scrolls to the bottom and shows a new timestamp separator; the jump between separators ("2009" then "2016") is the time skip. Old messages stay above; the player can scroll up and see their own teenage "ok" under her long paragraph.
- The contact name and avatar change with the years the way AIM away messages did: "Mom" with a photo, later "Mom" with an older photo; the parent's typing style changes (caps, emojis, then shorter messages as she ages). Small, unexplained changes carry time.
- Typing indicator as drama: show the parent typing for a long time and then send only "ok". Late in the story, show the indicator appear and vanish with nothing sent (the unfinished promise the sources describe). Use it two or three times in the whole story at most, so it keeps its weight.
- The player's typing: Emily is Away's mash-to-type adapts to the phone as "tap the keyboard to type": any key tap advances a predetermined line one or two characters at a time. The line can delete itself and become something shorter before send. This keeps authorship of the words while the player physically types.
- The unsent draft: one message in the story should be typed by the player and never sent (the app closes, the screen locks, the scene ends). It can reappear later still in the composer, untouched, which on iOS-style UIs is plausible behaviour (assumption).
- Delivered and Read labels: use "Delivered" under the player's last message on the night nobody texts, and never let it turn to "Read" in that scene. Note: no source was read on iMessage receipt behaviour; this is based on the common iOS pattern and should be checked against the real UI before drawing it.
- Notifications as interruptions: a banner sliding in during another memory is the phone-native way to make the player miss something (see section 4). Keep banners to one line, with the contact name and the first words, as on a real lock screen.
- Keep the in-story phone recognisably generic, not an Apple copy: flat shapes, the same palette as the rest of the art, bubbles left (them) and right (you). Florence and A Normal Lost Phone use stylised phones rather than exact replicas (Florence from the art direction source; A Normal Lost Phone from its description as a simulation, not verified as stylised).
- Final scene UI: the composer at the bottom, the keyboard up, the player taps out "text me when you get home", presses send, the bubble moves up, "Delivered" appears, and the story ends before any "Read" or reply.

### Gaps
- No primary source read on how Florence or Emily is Away time their message delays (milliseconds per character, pauses between bubbles); pacing values must be set by playtest.
- No source on iMessage's actual "Delivered"/"Read" semantics or on how typing indicators behave in iMessage specifically; the notes above use common knowledge and are marked as assumptions.
- Bury me, my Love's specific UI details (emoji, selfies, typing indicator) are not covered in the sources read.
