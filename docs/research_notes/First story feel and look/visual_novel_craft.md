# Visual novel craft: openings, visual storytelling and emotional peaks (for a phone-first second-person web story)

Scope note. Sources were gathered with WebSearch and WebFetch only. Several key pages (Fuwanovel blogs, TV Tropes, the Steam store and community pages for Being a DIK, nomnomnami's writing tips, the Katawa Shoujo writers' podcast page) returned 403 or an age gate, so some points rest on search-result snippets; those are marked "(snippet)". Points that come from general knowledge of the games and were not confirmed by a source in this pass are marked "(unverified)". All sexual content in the adult titles is left out on purpose. Quotes are kept under 15 words.

## 1. Openings: how praised visual novels start (first screen, first line, first tension, cold opens)

### Takeaway
The praised openings do one of three things fast: show the dark end first and then go back to ordinary life (Ace Attorney, Higurashi, Katawa Shoujo), set a ticking clock in the first minute (Long Live the Queen), or wrap a hidden wrongness in a warm, familiar surface (Doki Doki Literature Club, Planetarian). The openings critics attack are the ones that spend hours on setup with no early payoff (Steins;Gate, Higurashi, VA-11 Hall-A's slow early talk).

### Cited Findings
**Show the event first, then the ordinary**
- Ace Attorney: every case starts with a short cinematic of the murder, and only then is the player handed the defence of the suspect - [Wikipedia, Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Wright:_Ace_Attorney) (snippet). In the first case the killer is shown on screen, so the tension is "how do I prove it", not "who did it" (unverified, widely known).
- Ace Attorney's first case was written after the second; Takumi judged the second unfit as an opener, and the first case's only goal was a quick tutorial - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Wright:_Ace_Attorney); [Nintendo Everything, Takumi interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/ace-attorney-creator-on-initial-prototype-and-nearly-suspending-development-maya-and-mias-origins-more/2) (snippet).
- Katawa Shoujo: the prologue has Hisao collapse with a heart attack right after a classmate confesses to him; he is then moved to a school for disabled students a week before its festival - [Wikipedia, Katawa Shoujo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo). The confession takes place in snow, outdoors, and is cut off by the collapse (unverified detail).
- Higurashi (Onikakushi): one critic notes the mystery does not really start until about halfway through Chapter 6, with the earlier chapters given to slice-of-life fun; the critic calls the long opening "very contentious" - [Notes on Higurashi, Medium](https://medium.com/@primeforillusion/notes-on-higurashi-question-arcs-bb1ffc63ee4b). A VN review calls the start deceptively lighthearted before it spirals into paranoia - [GameRamble](https://gameramble.com/Higurashi-When-They-Cry-Hou-Ch-1-Onikakushi-PC-Game-Review) (snippet). Higurashi opens with a short ominous fragment before the cheerful village days (unverified).
- A fan wiki says Ryukishi07 took the pattern of happy ordinary scenes before sad ones from Key's games - 07th Expansion wiki mirror via search (snippet, low reliability).
- Umineko: a Fuwanovel blog post analysing the opening scene exists, but the page returned 403 - [Fuwanovel, Umineko's opening scene](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/blogs/entry/3173-uminekos-opening-scene). It opens on the family head raging alone in his study about a witch, then cuts to the cheerful family arriving on the island (unverified).

**A clock or a stake in the first minute**
- Long Live the Queen: the mother's death puts 14-year-old Elodie in line for the throne, and the goal is to keep her alive for 40 weeks until her coronation at 15 - [Wikiwand, Long Live the Queen](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Long_Live_the_Queen_(video_game)); [Indie Game Fans](https://indiegamefans.com/?p=5587) (snippet). Critics praise the choice depth, but PC Gamer says the art and sound could be much more interesting - [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/review/long-live-the-queen-review) (snippet).

**A warm or familiar surface with something off underneath**
- Doki Doki Literature Club: Salvato's idea was a VN that perfectly mimics tacky romance tropes with something wrong underneath - [Wikipedia, DDLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doki_Doki_Literature_Club!) (snippet). He expects players to start out mocking it as a tropey anime game and end up caring about the characters - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999).
- Salvato: the genre was never meant to be the surprise; the surprise is the specific ways the game chooses to scare - [Prima Games interview](https://primagames.com/gaming/doki-doki-literature-club-plus) (snippet). The horror runs on a growing sense that things are wrong, not jump scares or gore - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999).
- DDLC opens with a content warning screen before a bright, ordinary walk to school (unverified, widely known).
- Planetarian: a weary scavenger enters a dead, endlessly rainy city and meets a cheerful robot guide who has run a planetarium for 30 years without knowing the world ended - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/); [Caltrops](https://www.caltrops.com/2014/09/19/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippets). One reviewer says the ending can be guessed in the first 20 minutes - [AniList review](https://anilist.co/review/26908) (snippet).
- Coffee Talk: reviewers name the lo-fi beats and the first guest, plus a Seattle sunset, as what sets the mood at the start - [GameLuster](https://gameluster.com/review-coffee-talk-the-perfect-virtual-coffee-shop-experience/) (snippet). Steady rain, warm shop light and a sense of shelter from the outside make the place worth returning to - [Bonus Stage](https://www.bonusstage.co.uk/archives/158833) (snippet).
- Clannad opens at the foot of a hill lined with cherry trees, where Nagisa asks herself aloud whether she likes the school (unverified, widely known).
- Steins;Gate opens at a time-travel lecture in Akihabara and, within the first chapter, gives a body, a sent text message and a world that has quietly changed (unverified).

**Slow openings get punished**
- Steins;Gate: critics say the first half front-loads setup with no payoff until the second half; a Steam player says the early hours could have been told in half the time - [A Piece of Anime](https://apieceofanime.com/2017/12/17/the-meticulous-structure-of-steinsgate/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/412830/discussions/0/3055112985294212802) (snippets). Hardcore Gamer: slow to start, then well paced, giving out information in a way that keeps you reading - [Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-steinsgate/80692/) (snippet).
- VA-11 Hall-A: PC Gamer says the talks drag on with only a few words per click - [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/va-11-hall-a-review/) (snippet). RPGSite notes that some characters unload backstory too fast, before the player has earned their trust - [RPGSite](https://www.rpgsite.net/reviews/8447-va11-halla-cyberpunk-bartender-action-switch-review) (snippet).
- Being a DIK: the developer's Steam text frames it as a young man from a low-income family who joins a fraternity at college, and warns it is not meant for players going through a dark period - [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1126320/) (snippet; the page itself was behind an age gate).

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern for this project is Ace Attorney's and Katawa Shoujo's: open on one sharp event, then go back to ordinary life. For "text me when you get home", the first screen can be the message itself, a phone notification over a dark landscape, with the question already planted: will they get home, and who is waiting.
- Ace Attorney wrote its opener last, as a tutorial. Writing the first two minutes after the rest exists, and using them to teach the one interaction (tap to advance, how a reply is chosen), is a cheap way to make the opening both a hook and a lesson.
- DDLC, Planetarian and Coffee Talk show that warmth can be the hook if one detail is off. A whole-life story can open warm (a child, a kitchen, a dusk walk) as long as the first screens carry one line or image that points forward to loss.
- Long Live the Queen's 40 weeks show the value of a visible clock. A life has one built in: showing the age or the year on scene changes keeps the reader aware time is running.
- The slow-opening complaints (Steins;Gate, Higurashi, VA-11 Hall-A) say: on a phone, where leaving costs one swipe, the first real tension needs to come within the first few taps, not after a long setup.
- Design rules for the opening:
  1. Screen 1: one image, one line, already inside the story. No title card with lore.
  2. The first question is planted by tap 3 to 5.
  3. A short cold open (the end, or a moment near it), then a hard cut back to the start of the life.
  4. Teach the reply mechanic inside the fiction in the first minute.
  5. If the story will touch grief or loss, one calm content line before screen 1, the way DDLC and Being a DIK warn up front.

### Gaps
- Exact first lines and first screens could not be confirmed from sources for Clannad, Steins;Gate, Danganronpa, Umineko, Higurashi, DDLC, Butterfly Soup, VA-11 Hall-A and Being a DIK; the text pages that would show them (TV Tropes, Fuwanovel) were blocked.
- Nothing usable was found on the openings of Heaven Will Be Mine or A Year of Springs.
- No source gave timing data (how many seconds or clicks before players quit a VN opening).

## 2. Visual storytelling: CG reveals, camera moves on stills, transitions, colour and light, how long a picture is held

### Takeaway
Visual novels make still images feel alive with a small toolkit: slow camera moves (push-in, pan, dolly with a blurred background), a few transitions with known timings (0.5 s dissolve, 1 s fade through black, 0.25 s shake, a fast white flash), and full-screen illustrations (CGs) saved for scenes the sprites cannot carry. Pictures change on beats, not on every line, and the changes are spread out instead of fired all at once.

### Cited Findings
**Transition timings (Ren'Py defaults, the engine most indie VNs use)**
- `dissolve` 0.5 s; `fade` 0.5 s to black and 0.5 s back in (1 s total); `hpunch` and `vpunch` shake the screen for 0.25 s; `blinds` and `squares` 1 s; `zoomin`, `zoomout` 0.5 s; there is no built-in flash, and the docs' example is a fade to white of 0.1 s, then back in over 0.5 s - [Ren'Py documentation, Transitions](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/transitions.html).
- A common convention: fade when a new scene is established, dissolve when a character appears in the same scene, and dissolve to show time passing quickly - search snippets from Ren'Py tutorials ([PygameVN basic animations](https://staff6773.github.io/PygameVN/basic_animations); [videlais.com Ren'Py series](https://videlais.com/category/renpy/page/19/)); exact attribution not confirmed.
- One developer found transitions freeze the engine for their length, so Juniper's Knot hardcoded a soft fade between animation frames instead - [stefangagne.com](https://stefangagne.com/2017/10/23) (snippet).

**Camera on still images (Of Sense and Soul devlog)**
- Close shots on one sprite for introspection, introductions, or a character making a point; full shots of the whole room for distance or a foreign feel - [Of Sense and Soul devlog](https://forsythiaproductions.itch.io/of-sense-and-soul/devlog/480454/sprites-camera-action-vn-cinematography-in-of-sense-and-soul).
- Quick pans between speakers give banter its back-and-forth; hard cuts with no pan feel "more jarring" and are used for arguments and heavy silences - same source.
- Dolly zoom (sprite pulled closer while the background defocuses): under one second it heightens emotion; over a few seconds it reads as distraction or a pause for inner thought - same source.
- Blurring, darkening, desaturating or hiding the background adds intimacy and weight - same source.
- A blurred heartbeat pulse paired with a heartbeat sound is used for tense moments - same source.
- Small body animations (a bounce for excitement, a lean-in for teasing or whispering, a stretch for laughing, a bow for politeness) add emphasis and reduce fatigue from constant camera movement - same source.
- Shot speed sets urgency: slow moves feel leisurely, near-instant cuts feel urgent - same source.

**CGs and visual beats (Complex Relations devlog)**
- CGs are one-off art, so studios are tempted to skimp; they are used for scenes the sprites cannot show, which tend to need more complex movement - [Complex Relations devlog, CGs and visual beats](https://amai-works.itch.io/complex-relations/devlog/206135/cgs-and-visual-beats).
- The writer paces CG scenes with "generous use of visual beats", aims for a balanced back-and-forth between text and image, and deliberately desynchronises image changes that most VNs would make at the same moment - same source.
- Writing the scene first and fitting the art afterwards risks scenes that feel unfinished; the scene should be written to fit the art that can be made - same source.
- Another developer notes that even a small pan of the camera, the character or the background "can do wonders" - search snippet, [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/12400169) (attribution not confirmed).

**How the picture relates to the text box**
- ADV (small box, a few lines) keeps backgrounds, sprites and CGs mostly visible, so the art carries more of the story; NVL (full-screen text) pushes the picture behind the text, and its art leans toward clear, uncluttered shapes so it does not get lost - [NVL vs ADV, Towards the End Sky](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2022/10/nvl-vs-adv-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html).
- NVL allows fine control such as a single word centred on an empty screen, but such tricks lose impact when overused - same source.

**Mood, colour, light**
- Coffee Talk's mood is built from rain outside, warm shop light inside, and lo-fi music, which reviewers say makes the place a refuge to return to - [Bonus Stage](https://www.bonusstage.co.uk/archives/158833); [GameLuster](https://gameluster.com/review-coffee-talk-the-perfect-virtual-coffee-shop-experience/) (snippets).
- Planetarian sets a cheerful robot against a city where rain has hidden the stars for the hero's whole life - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippet).
- Being a DIK is built in Ren'Py with visuals rendered in DAZ 3D - [Steam community thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1126320/discussions/0/2278205083633830951) (snippet).

### Inferences
- With faceless silhouettes, the Of Sense and Soul toolkit matters even more: posture changes (lean in, turn away, shoulders drop), distance between figures, and background defocus have to do what faces do in other VNs. Two silhouettes moving closer on the horizon is the flat-art version of the z-axis move.
- A "CG reveal" for this project can be a full-detail landscape (stars, a lit window, a sunrise) after many plain, muted screens. The effect depends on scarcity: Complex Relations treats CGs as rare, and the NVL/ADV piece warns that tricks used often stop working.
- Transition grammar to adopt (inferred from the Ren'Py defaults and conventions):
  - 1 s fade through black for a jump in years.
  - 0.5 s dissolve within a scene.
  - A white flash (about 0.1 s in, 0.5 s out) only for a memory or a realisation.
  - A 0.25 s shake almost never; in a gentle story a shake is a gunshot.
- Hold one landscape across many lines and change it on beats: a light turning on, the sky shifting a step toward night, a figure leaving the frame. Change one thing at a time (the Complex Relations desync idea) rather than background, figures and music in one tap.
- A slow push-in over several seconds suits inner thought; a fast push under a second suits a shock. Generative art in code makes this cheap: animate a CSS or canvas scale of 1.00 to 1.04 over 6 to 10 s for "the reader is leaning in" (the exact values are this note's suggestion, not from a source).
- Time of day can be the life clock: dawn tones for childhood, bright noon for youth, golden hour for the middle, dusk and night for the late years. No VN source states this rule directly; it is an inference from how Coffee Talk and Planetarian use rain and light as mood.

### Gaps
- No source gave a measured rate of picture changes per minute or per line in praised VNs.
- No reliable source was found on Key or Type-Moon CG placement (for example how many plain screens come before a key CG in Clannad).
- Nothing was found on silhouette-only character art in VNs specifically; Firewatch-style flat art is covered by the parallel visual-language notes, not here.

## 3. Being a DIK: what makes its emotional scenes land (craft only)

### Takeaway
What reviewers credit is not the adult content but the cast, choices that change how characters treat the player, and smooth turns from comedy into drama, plus music moments. Animated key scenes, lighting and facial acting are widely talked about in the community but could not be confirmed from accessible sources in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- On an aggregate of Steam reviews: 91% positive sentiment; "well-written characters and story" is the most-cited praise (99 mentions); "meaningful choices and consequences" 46; "complex story and character development" 49; "impactful player choices" 19; "emotionally engaging experience" 12 - [Vaporlens, Being a DIK review summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320).
- Same aggregate: reviewers say the story moves smoothly between comedy and drama, and that story, music and characters together give the emotional impact; playing musical instruments together is singled out as a distinct element - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320).
- Same aggregate: the most common complaint is no voice acting and little sound (22 mentions) - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320). Some fans also criticised the soundtrack - [Metacritic, Being a DIK Season 1](https://www.metacritic.com/game/being-a-dik-season-1/) (snippet). So music is praised and criticised at once.
- The developer's description: choices shape how other characters treat you; the female characters have different values and react to the player according to them; the protagonist can be played as caring or as blunt and daring - [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1126320/) (snippet).
- A Metacritic user says the game takes you through happy, funny, surprised, sad and empathetic moments, and praises a funny but loyal friend character - [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/being-a-dik-season-1/) (snippet).
- A Steam forum poster says the game makes them feel real emotion and compares its depth to some Japanese VNs; another says it made them reflect on their own choices and whether the hurt caused to others was worth it - [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1126320/discussions/0/3191364956936750205/) (snippet; the page needs a login).
- Structure: released in episodes and later sold as seasons; reviewers note a large amount of content and 26 say they are waiting for Season 3 - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320).
- Production: Ren'Py engine, visuals made in DAZ 3D - [Steam community thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1126320/discussions/0/2278205083633830951) (snippet). Another VN creator names Dr PinkCake's earlier work as an influence on their own - [SAVE.POINT](https://savepoint.twoaveragegamers.com/games/being-a-dik) (snippet, context unclear).
- The game uses a two-way personality choice (DIK versus CHICK) plus per-character relationship tracking, and ends episodes on cliffhangers (unverified; widely discussed by players but not confirmed in an accessible source).

### Inferences
- The lesson that fits a non-adult story: attachment comes from a large cast of friends with their own small arcs, from choices that change how people treat you, and from comedy that is allowed to turn sincere inside the same scene. The "reflect on whether the hurt was worth it" reaction shows that choices which can hurt someone the player likes carry the strongest feeling.
- The voice and sound complaint suggests that a text-only story should lean harder on music and ambient sound, since silence where a voice would be is noticed.
- Episodic cliffhangers kept a community waiting between releases. A web story released in parts could end each part on an open question (a message sent, no reply yet).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from an accessible source: the role of short animated scenes at key moments, render lighting, facial expression work, specific music cues, or which named scenes players cite as the emotional high points. The Steam store, Steam discussions, TV Tropes and Patreon pages were blocked or age-gated. Reddit was not reachable with the allowed tools.
- No professional critic review of Being a DIK was found; the evidence is player-review aggregates and forum posts.

## 4. Text and pacing: line length, tap rhythm, silence, auto versus tap, music and sound timing

### Takeaway
One idea per text box, one sentence per tap as the base rhythm, extra taps to slow a moment that matters, and music used sparingly, with a deliberate cut to silence just before or at the biggest beats. ADV (a few lines at a time) feels fast and forward; NVL (full-screen text) feels slow and inward; the best works switch between them by scene.

### Cited Findings
**Line and box length**
- Keep each text box to one idea or emotion; if an idea needs four sentences, split it - search snippet attributed to [nomnomnami, tips for game writers](https://nomnomnami.com/blog/posts/2023-04-18-tips-for-game-writers) (page returned 403; attribution not confirmed).
- Long walls of text tire readers; break up overly long sentences - same search (snippet).
- An ADV box with only a few lines makes readers feel they are rapidly and constantly progressing - [NVL vs ADV](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2022/10/nvl-vs-adv-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html).
- Counterpoint: one player complains about devs using only the top line of a box that fits three or four, and argues a full paragraph is sometimes fine - search snippet, [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/7534718) (attribution not confirmed).
- VA-11 Hall-A was criticised for showing only a few words per click, so talks dragged - [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/va-11-hall-a-review/) (snippet).
- Readers rarely flip back or open the history, so restating a key idea in new words slows an emotional beat and makes sure it lands - search snippet, likely [Minotaur Hotel writing manual II](https://itch.io/devlog/169215/minotaur-hotels-writing-manual-ii-descriptions-tension-mechanics-blocking-scope-and-length.amp) (attribution not confirmed).
- Avoid vague pronouns across consecutive boxes, since readers lose track of who "she" is - same search (snippet).

**Tap rhythm (His Own Being devlog)**
- A wait tag at the end of most sentences gives one sentence per click, the most natural base rhythm - [His Own Being devlog](https://davisgsee.itch.io/his-own-being/devlog/1007030/controlling-nvl-mode-sentence-pacing-in-renpy).
- Pausing at every comma frustrates readers - same source.
- Showing two sentences together makes them read as one fast unit (suits rushed or confused thought); a pause after every short fragment mirrors halting thought; a pause after each list item gives each item weight - same source.

**ADV and NVL as two speeds**
- ADV suits action and external interaction but makes slowing down hard; NVL slows the story, invites rereading, and suits inner thought; switching between them by scene works but takes skill and can confuse - [NVL vs ADV](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2022/10/nvl-vs-adv-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html).

**Music, silence and sound effects**
- VNs use few sound effects, so music carries most of the emotional weight and usually states the intended feeling plainly - [Soundtrack anatomy, Towards the End Sky](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2025/12/soundtrack-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html).
- A pause in the music heightens the tracks on both sides: the last one lingers, the next arrives as release; overused tracks lose their effect, and repeated tracks become the memorable ones - same source.
- Silence can hit harder than music at major reveals or hard decisions - [8bitdigi, composing for VNs](https://8bitdigi.com/5-advice-composing-visual-novel-soundtrack/) (snippet).
- A sudden music stop draws attention and builds dread; near-silence with an eerie ambience works as long as the change is jarring - [TV Tropes, Sudden Soundtrack Stop](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SuddenSoundtrackStop) (snippet).
- Some players feel sudden silence breaks immersion; Fate/stay night is praised for replacing music with a ticking clock, footsteps, silverware or leaves - [Fuwanovel, Sound design in VNs](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/topic/4615-sound-design-in-visual-novels) (snippet).
- One VN (Stretch) dropped background music entirely and used over 350 ambient sounds, chosen per scene, so players feel present in the place - [Stretch devlog](https://kirishimashoichi.itch.io/stretch-en/devlog/966792/a-visual-novel-with-no-bgm-why-we-chose-silence).
- Key's music is described as well timed with the art to move the reader - search snippets around [Jun Maeda, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun_Maeda) and [JHU News-Letter](https://www.jhunewsletter.com/article/2011/11/the-visual-novel-medium-proves-its-worth-on-the-battlefield-of-narrative-arts-16068) (attribution not confirmed). Planetarian's music is praised as placed for maximum emotional impact - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippet).
- A Steam VN fan says just hearing Utawarerumono: Mask of Truth's ending theme still moves them years later - [Steam group discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/IloveVNS/discussions/0/1762482479184226904) (snippet).
- Culture-specific instruments set the place without exposition (Piofiore's jazz, Winter's Wish's Japanese instruments); Piofiore reuses its jazz theme in a sombre arrangement for sad moments - [Soundtrack anatomy](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2025/12/soundtrack-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html).

### Inferences
- On a phone screen: 1 to 3 short lines per tap, one idea per tap. This sits between VA-11 Hall-A's too-few words and an NVL wall of text. As a starting point, under about 90 characters per tap, with exceptions for a "pause" screen of a single word (this number is a suggestion, not from a source).
- Tap to advance by default, never auto-advance during normal text; the reader controls the pace, and the emotional slow-downs come from extra taps (one fragment per tap), not from timers. A held, untappable beat of 1 to 2 s is fine only at a peak (for example after a message is sent).
- An empty screen with one word, or just the landscape and no text, is the NVL "single centred word" trick: strong once or twice in the story, weak if repeated.
- Music grammar for generative audio:
  - One warm leitmotif that returns, rearranged with age (Piofiore's sombre jazz is the model).
  - Cut to ambience (wind, rain, a kettle, a car door) a beat before the big line, not on it.
  - The motif returns after the silence as release.
  - Sound effects as punctuation: a phone buzz, a door, a key in a lock. A key in a lock is the sound that answers "text me when you get home".
- Restating the key line in new words late in a scene is a legitimate tool, because phone readers tap fast and do not scroll back.

### Gaps
- No source gave measured characters-per-line or lines-per-box numbers from successful VNs, or data on phone readers' tap speed.
- No source compared auto-advance with tap-to-advance in emotional scenes directly.
- Nothing citable was found on exact timing of music changes against specific lines in Clannad, Steins;Gate or Katawa Shoujo.

## 5. Choices and attachment: affection tracking, flags, callbacks, late payoffs of small early details

### Takeaway
VNs make players care through choices about how to treat people (not stat optimisation), through a short open stretch that then commits to one relationship, and through small early details that turn out to matter. A phone-message mechanic like Steins;Gate's, where you reply by tapping words in a received text, maps almost exactly onto this project's premise.

### Cited Findings
- Katawa Shoujo: Act 1 is one week and is the only part with wide-open branching; the girl Hisao spends the festival with decides the route; if the player never commits, Hisao spends the festival drinking on the roof and dies in a fall (the Act 1 bad ending) - [Wikipedia, Katawa Shoujo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo); [renai.us, Act 1](https://renai.us/game/katawashoujoact1) (snippet). Each route has good, neutral and bad endings - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo).
- In Hanako's route the outcome depends on how Hisao handles her emotional fragility, with the good ending built on mutual honesty; in Lilly's, a reconciliation at the airport only happens if earlier choices allow it - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo).
- Steins;Gate "phone trigger": the player can answer or ignore calls; incoming texts have underlined blue words, like hyperlinks, which the player taps to choose a reply; most messages need no reply, but some moments require action; how you reply steers the plot - [Steins;Gate, Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Steins%3BGate) (snippet). On iOS the phone was opened by a swipe up on iPad, or by turning the phone to portrait on iPhone - [TouchArcade game page](https://toucharcade.com/games/steins-gate-hd-en-english) (snippet).
- Steins;Gate: a reviewer says the first episode is confusing but becomes a crucial plot point later, and that many easily-missed details play a big role - MyAnimeList review via search (snippet; anime, not VN). Fans argue the second half hits hard because of the first - [A Piece of Anime](https://apieceofanime.com/2017/12/17/the-meticulous-structure-of-steinsgate/) (snippet).
- VA-11 Hall-A: the drinks you serve act as the choice system and shape how conversations go - [Keen Gamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/va-11-hall-a-review/) (snippet). Coffee Talk has almost no fail state: a wrong drink costs a frown, and you can reload - [Xboxera](https://xboxera.com/2022/09/29/review-coffee-talk) (snippet).
- Being a DIK: choices change how characters treat you and whether they are drawn to you, based on their own values - [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1126320/) (snippet); "meaningful choices and consequences" is among the most-cited praise - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320).
- DDLC: Salvato writes characters who seem to have rich inner lives shown only in glimpses, which makes players attached and protective - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999).
- Long Live the Queen: a stat-and-schedule system with many deaths and branching endings; praised for replay value, called obtuse by one critic - [3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/?p=148582); [GamesHedge](https://www.gameshedge.com/?p=58921) (snippets).
- Planetarian has no choices at all and is still praised - [Caltrops](https://www.caltrops.com/2014/09/19/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippet).

### Inferences
- The Steins;Gate phone trigger is the closest existing model for this story. Received messages with one or two tappable words make replying feel like texting, not like picking from a menu, and fit a phone-first layout. Most messages can be read-only, so the few that ask for a reply feel heavy.
- Katawa Shoujo's lesson: a short open stretch, then commitment, and not committing is itself a choice with a cost. In a life story, not answering, or answering late, can be the costly choice.
- Track care, not points: Hanako's route turns on how you handle someone fragile. A small set of hidden flags is enough (did you text back, did you say the warm thing, did you show up), each paid off once later in the life.
- Callbacks: plant 3 to 5 small concrete details early (a nickname, a song, a phrase, a porch light) and return each one exactly once near a peak. This is the Steins;Gate "missable detail that matters" pattern, applied with restraint.
- Planetarian shows that a linear story with no choices can still move people; choices are optional to the feeling, so each one should earn its place.

- Katawa Shoujo's Four Leaf Studios dev blog and the writers' podcast were not reachable, so the developers' own reasoning on Act 1 choices is missing.
- Being a DIK's points and path systems could not be confirmed from an accessible source (see section 3).

## 6. Humour and warmth beside sadness; how routes build to the emotional peak

### Takeaway
The best-known tear-jerkers build a long, funny, everyday stretch first, then turn; the contrast is the engine. The risk named by critics is that a long comedy stretch reads as padding (Steins;Gate, Higurashi) or the turn as manipulation (Clannad). What protects against both is that the everyday scenes are good on their own terms and the comedy belongs to characters the reader has come to love.

### Cited Findings
- Jun Maeda's Key games (ONE, Kanon, Air, Clannad) are credited with founding "nakige", the crying game: light slice-of-life comedy that slowly gives way to tragedy and catharsis; recurring themes are family, inevitable loss, and small miracles in mundane settings - search snippets around [Jun Maeda, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun_Maeda) and [Operation Rainfall, Clannad](https://operationrainfall.com/?p=186516) (attribution not confirmed; fan guides, not scholarship).
- Clannad After Story: a blog argues the light first season is what makes After Story hit, because viewers have spent many episodes in the characters' ordinary lives, and calls that contrast the emotional engine - [icrave-anime blog](https://icrave-anime.pages.dev/posts/2026-03-15-clannad-after-story-review-tomoya-arc-2026) (snippet; provenance unclear; anime). A MyAnimeList reviewer names character attachment as the main reason people cry - [MAL review](https://myanimelist.net/reviews.php?id=107159) (snippet).
- Counter-view on Clannad: some reviewers think the tear-jerking pulls on emotions to lift an average show; others say its real subject is what it means to be a parent and to forgive one - [MAL reviews](https://myanimelist.net/reviews.php?id=188118) (snippet).
- Higurashi: one critic argues the slice-of-life days should be read on their own terms, not as a mask to tear off, and that the blend of slice of life and horror is what makes the tragedy - [Notes on Higurashi](https://medium.com/@primeforillusion/notes-on-higurashi-question-arcs-bb1ffc63ee4b).
- Katawa Shoujo: a user review says many routes are very sad even when they end happily - search snippet via [TV Tropes / allthetropes](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo) (attribution not confirmed).
- Butterfly Soup: players single out awkward teenage "cringe" comedy and meme humour, and in the same breath say the ending made them cry and helped with their own identity; the most common complaint is that it is too short - [Butterfly Soup itch.io comments](https://brianna-lei.itch.io/butterfly-soup) (snippets; self-selected fans).
- Being a DIK: reviewers say it moves smoothly between comedy and drama - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320).
- Planetarian: the cynical scavenger's hardness slowly gives way to the robot's warmth; one reviewer says it is disarming to watch her break through his shell - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/); [Caltrops](https://www.caltrops.com/2014/09/19/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippets). Critics also call it cliché and abrupt in places - same sources.
- DDLC handles a character's depression in a way that avoids cheap sympathy, and Salvato argues a dark tone need not mean hopelessness - [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999).
- Coffee Talk: the cast grows on you with each calm conversation, though some themes are handled with occasional preachiness - [Xboxera](https://xboxera.com/2022/09/29/review-coffee-talk) (snippet).

### Inferences
- For a life story, the "comedy first" stretch is childhood and youth: small, specific, funny family moments (a parent's bad joke, a sibling's lie, a friend's dumb plan). They must be worth reading even if nothing sad ever came, which is the Higurashi critic's point.
- Do not front-load all the comedy and then switch to all sadness. Butterfly Soup and Being a DIK are praised for turning within a scene. Let a joke sit next to a sad line, and let a later sad scene contain a callback to an early joke.
- Planetarian's structure (a closed, hard person slowly warmed by a sincere one) maps onto a parent-child arc across a life: the "text me when you get home" sender is the sincere one, and the reader's character is the one who learns to answer.
- Avoid the manipulation charge Clannad gets: no sad music under scenes that have not earned it, no death used only to make the reader cry, and keep the turn tied to a theme (being a parent, being a child, coming home).
- Crisis-safe framing: DDLC's point that dark need not mean hopeless fits a story about coming home. The peak should land on connection, not only loss.

### Gaps
- No primary VN-specific source (as opposed to anime reviews) was found for Clannad's structure; the Clannad points come from anime reviews and fan guides.
- Nothing was found on how Katawa Shoujo's writers paced routes toward their climaxes.

## 7. The moments players say made them cry or gave them chills, and why

### Takeaway
Players rarely credit one trick. They name (a) a character they spent long, ordinary time with, (b) music placed exactly at the turn, often a returning theme or an ending song, and (c) a payoff that only works because of setup the reader once thought was filler. Specific "crying scenes" for the famous titles are common knowledge in fan circles but could not be confirmed from accessible sources here.

### Cited Findings
- Planetarian: reviewers credit the music, placed for maximum effect, plus voice acting, and the contrast of a sincere robot with a dead world - [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/) (snippet).
- Clannad After Story: reviewers name character attachment built in everyday episodes as the reason for crying - [MAL review](https://myanimelist.net/reviews.php?id=107159); [icrave-anime blog](https://icrave-anime.pages.dev/posts/2026-03-15-clannad-after-story-review-tomoya-arc-2026) (snippets; anime).
- Steins;Gate: fans say the second half's emotional beats hit hard because the slow first half built attachment and taught the rules of time travel - [A Piece of Anime](https://apieceofanime.com/2017/12/17/the-meticulous-structure-of-steinsgate/) (snippet).
- Butterfly Soup: players report crying at the ending, after a game full of comedy - [Butterfly Soup itch.io comments](https://brianna-lei.itch.io/butterfly-soup) (snippets).
- Utawarerumono: Mask of Truth: a fan says its ending theme still moves them years later; the same fan names Subarashiki Hibi, Umineko and Muv-Luv Alternative as their most emotional VNs - [Steam group discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/IloveVNS/discussions/0/1762482479184226904) (snippet).
- Being a DIK: "emotionally engaging experience" cited 12 times; story, music and characters credited together - [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320). A Steam player says it made them reflect on whether hurting others was worth it - [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1126320/discussions/0/3191364956936750205/) (snippet).
- Katawa Shoujo: routes described as very sad even when they end happily - search snippet (see section 6).
- Commonly named moments in fan culture, not confirmed by a source in this pass (unverified): Clannad After Story's late father-and-daughter scenes; Steins;Gate's repeated attempts to save the same friend; Planetarian's ending at the planetarium; Katawa Shoujo's airport scene in Lilly's route; the end of DDLC's first act.

### Inferences
- The shared mechanism is long ordinary time, then a turn, then music that has been heard before in a happy context. For this project, the generative motif heard in the warm early scenes should come back, slower or thinner, at the peak.
- The "text me when you get home" message is a natural callback object: sent many times across a life in plain scenes, so that one late sending (or one late reply) carries the whole story. That is the Steins;Gate "setup that looked like filler" pattern.
- Ending songs and returning themes give chills more than new music at the peak; introduce the peak theme early and quietly.
- The Being a DIK reaction (questioning whether the hurt was worth it) shows that a peak built on the player's own earlier choice hits harder than one that only happens to the character.

### Gaps
- The specific scenes most often named as tear-jerkers in Clannad, Steins;Gate, Katawa Shoujo, Planetarian and DDLC could not be confirmed: TV Tropes "Tear Jerker" pages, Reddit and Fuwanovel threads were blocked or not reachable with WebSearch and WebFetch.
- No survey or poll data on which VN scenes make players cry was found; all evidence here is reviews and anecdotes.
- Nothing was found on chills or tears for Heaven Will Be Mine, A Year of Springs, Danganronpa or Ace Attorney.
