# Make the feeling the thing you tap

Story games move people when the feeling is something the player does, not something they are told. The strongest examples pick one small physical verb that resembles the emotion (a puzzle that gets easier as two people grow close, a dead brother's button, a juice box that soothes nothing), repeat it until it is automatic, then change it or take it away at the peak, with no score and no way to fail. People remember an experience by its peak and its ending, and its length barely counts: a meta-analysis of 174 effect sizes found r = 0.58 for peak and end and close to zero for duration. That means a five-minute story can hit as hard as a long one, and Passage did it in five minutes and ended up in MoMA. People are pulled in by a nameable person and a question they can almost answer, on a first screen that appears instantly. They are held by consistency and by choices the story visibly answers. They come back because people tend to resume what was interrupted, not because of a memory trick: the Zeigarnik effect failed a 2025 meta-analysis. In practice that means one-tap resuming and an episode end that leaves a question about someone they like. A one-creator collection becomes distinctive through honest, narrow slices held together by a fixed set of controls under changing looks, and a menu that is itself a collection object. Realistic reach is tens to low thousands of plays, mostly through itch.io tags, browser play and cover art. The recommended stack is a Vite 8 and TypeScript multi-page site on GitHub Pages, every story's words in ink files run by inkjs, and a renderer chosen per story by its look: DOM, CSS and GSAP for tap and scroll stories, Phaser 4 for pixel walk-arounds, Three.js on WebGL2 for realistic ones. Everything seen or heard should be the author's own guitar, voice, photos and scans, because 85% of surveyed gamers rate generative AI in games below neutral.

## One taught verb, turned at the peak, carries the feeling

The designers behind the most moving narrative games describe the same move: they choose one small action that resembles the emotion, and they choose it for feeling rather than depth or challenge. Ken Wong made Florence for emotional impact. He says challenge, agency and long sessions did not guide the design; his rule was **"Use whatever mechanics feel right for the story,"** and he admits none of the mechanics are deep ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-)). Florence's conversations are jigsaws. On early dates a speech bubble comes in many pieces, the pieces get fewer as the couple grows close, and in arguments the shapes turn jagged and building them becomes a race against the other person ([GameIndustry.com](https://www.gameindustry.com/interviews/ken-wong-florence-love-story-mobile/); [Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence)). Wong found touch screens especially good for mechanics that mirror the player's emotional state, and he brought mechanics back later in the game to show that the characters had changed ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-)). He dropped an earlier argument idea because "it was only visual and not interactive" ([Milanote](https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence)).

The same pattern runs through the best of the genre. In Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons each brother has his own stick and trigger. After the older brother dies, the younger one must cross water he fears, his own button does nothing, and **the player has to press the dead brother's button** ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-emotional-story-bond-success/)). Before Your Eyes moves the story on each time you blink, so you try to hold on to a moment but "inevitably you're going to have to blink"; even the early prototype made people cry ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)). In That Dragon, Cancer you try to soothe a crying baby through a hospital night by bouncing him and offering juice, and nothing works ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/463224149)). Unpacking tells a life by having you unpack objects, under the rule **"No item? No trait!"** In the boyfriend's apartment her diploma ends up over the toilet, a red flag shown with no dialogue at all, because "item progression is character progression" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)). Venba turns cultural loss into a puzzle: the mother's recipe book is damaged, and the player works out the missing steps ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/03/08/abhi-developer-venba-indian-cooking-game-interview/)). Lucas Pope set the job of checking documents against the survival of the inspector's family. He notes the moral weight exists only if the player imagines the family as real ([GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/features/lucas-pope-papers-please-return-of-the-obra-dinn-and-no-sequels-20220523)).

Inference: read together, these cases give a three-step recipe. First, pick one verb that physically resembles the feeling. Second, repeat it until it is automatic. Third, change what it means or take it away at the peak. The peak works only because the verb was taught first. Deliberate futility (an input that cannot fix anything) is one of the strongest tools in the set, because helplessness felt in the hands hits harder than any cutscene.

The second shared choice is restraint about reward. Edith Finch's cannery scene has "no meter or explosions when you do it perfectly" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)). Gris has no deaths, no game-over screens and no text, so that non-players could follow a girl through grief ([indiegame.com](https://indiegame.com/en/archives/22258)). Venba has no failure state ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/03/08/abhi-developer-venba-indian-cooking-game-interview/)). Anna Anthropy moved away from hard games because skill requirements limit who can reach the story ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Anthropy)). Celeste tells players its Assist Mode is not cheating ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/my-biggest-revelations-of-2018-came-from-an-indie-video-game/)); Maddy Thorson wanted to "show the same kindness to the player that we want to show ourselves" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)). Inference: a score tells the player the moment is a test, not a feeling.

Where to start matters for a writer. Giant Sparrow built a mechanic players could explore first, and only then asked "who this event would occur to" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)). Pope built Papers, Please from one constraint ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/lucas-pope-on-making-games-out-of-restrictions)). A creator whose stories are their own thoughts starts at the other end. Inference: the workable middle path is to start from the thought, search for the one action that makes it felt, and drop or park the thought if no action fits. Unpacking did exactly that with a subplot about the parents' divorce. It was cut because "we simply couldn't convey it without any words" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)).

The failures designers describe are failures of focus. Florence's team spent two or three weeks on a chapter prototype "only to find out the emotion of the story wouldn't come across," and dropped 3D because it "wasn't adding anything to the storytelling" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-)). Edith Finch's cannery mechanic overloaded players once it was combined with everything else in the scene, and a rejected prototype with a flipped screen created "more of a disconnect" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)). Before Your Eyes' blink was "in danger of taking away from the story" because it was too powerful ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)). In Journey, items only one player could pick up quietly set strangers competing. The fix was to make them respawn for the second player ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)). It was a small case of what Clint Hocking called ludonarrative dissonance in BioShock, where the story asks for selflessness while the mechanics reward self-interest ([Looper](https://www.looper.com/295787/what-is-ludonarrative-dissonance/)). Some Brothers players felt the controls changed at the climax without warning ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/225080/discussions/0/846964364045398435/)). No designer source in the research speaks directly about melodrama or too much text. The lessons against melodrama are therefore inferred from what the best games do: they show ordinary routines rather than speeches, they let objects imply the drama, and they leave causes unstated. Edith Finch tells one story only through the last page of a divorce contract, because "there wasn't any benefit from going into the details" ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/what-remains-of-edith-finch-interview/)).

Inference, for a phone: verbs a solo creator can build need only touch, text, simple 2D and one music track. Drag pieces together (Florence). Hold to keep and let go to lose (a touch version of the blink). Place 15 to 30 objects in one room (Unpacking). Pick one line per verse of a song. Kentucky Route Zero does this with Junebug's song: every combination stays a song about a lover who left, and the composer wanted the player to take part "rather than making you just sit and watch" ([FACT](https://www.factmag.com/2016/08/23/kentucky-route-zero-ben-babbitt/)). Or fill in a damaged letter (Venba). What needs a big team: Journey's online pairing (about three years for a roughly two-hour game ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-))), Edith Finch's many custom control schemes, and webcam blink detection. One technical inference also matters: the Vibration API that could copy Brothers' rumble does not work in iOS Safari, so no peak should depend on vibration.

## The peak and the last screen decide the memory, not the length

How an experience is remembered depends mostly on its most intense moment and its ending. A 2022 meta-analysis pooled **174 effect sizes and found a large peak-end effect (r = 0.58)**. It was stronger than the effect of the experience's overall trend or its ups and downs, while **duration had essentially no effect** ([OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4NG6JZvl)). The meta-analysis covers experiences in general. No study in the research tests the rule on stories or interactive narratives, so applying it here is an inference, though a well-grounded one. It explains why short pieces can hit hard. Jason Rohrer's Passage (2007) holds a whole life in five minutes. You start young at the left edge, age as you move right and die. Walking with a spouse earns more points but limits where you can go, and grief slows you after they die. Rohrer said "I guess it made a lot of people cry," and MoMA added it to its collection in 2012 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passage_%28video_game%29); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/145533); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/lifestyle/names/2016/02/10/jason-rohrer-video-games-might-make-you-cry/4zaZOHTrgKgfDsQIbyEvBN/story.html)). Dys4ia covered about six months of hormone therapy as a string of quick microgames in the browser, in the style of WarioWare ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dys4ia)). Before Your Eyes' designers drew the lesson "keep the game short" and put each scene's key beat at its start. They asked how to tell a coherent story "even if you skip most of it" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)). Short does not mean cheap, though. Adam Robinson-Yu shipped A Short Hike alone within a four-month deadline. He did it by defining the smallest complete game and adding optional extras that grew or shrank to fit his real pace of work ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A)).

Inference: the most reliable peak for a personal story is a moment of sudden closeness. **Kama muta**, the emotion people call being moved or touched, was tested in 19 countries, in 15 languages, with 3,542 participants. It is a positive emotion set off when closeness between people suddenly deepens. People feel it as warmth in the chest, moist eyes, chills and a lump in the throat ([Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/kama-muta-conceptualizing-and-measuring-the-experience-often-labe/)). Calling something moving does nothing by itself. In four pre-registered studies with 1,049 people, messages designed to be moving had no direct effect; the effects ran only through how moved people actually felt ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1112910)). People also seek out sad and mixed stories on purpose. Across four studies, Oliver and Raney showed that pleasure and meaning are two separate, common reasons people choose entertainment ([University at Buffalo](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/entertainment-as-pleasurable-and-meaningful-identifying-hedonic-a/)). Researchers use "appreciation" for the reward of poignant stories, which "enjoyment" describes poorly ([Oliver chapter](https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/jaro2021/PSYb2990/um/doporucena_literatura/10.4324_9780429491146-17_chapterpdf.txt?lang=en)). So a sad ending is not a cost, as long as it means something.

The overall shape of the story matters less than the peak. Text analysis of 1,327 novels found six main emotional shapes ([EPJ Data Science](https://epjds.epj.org/articles/epjdata/abs/2016/01/13688_2016_Article_93/13688_2016_Article_93.html)). Across 6,174 film scripts, the fall-then-rise "Man in a Hole" shape earned the most at the box office. But those films were the most talked about, not the best liked, and the authors warn against treating the finding as a recipe ([University of Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/95602489/Del_Vecchio_et_al_2020_Improving_productivity_in_Hollywood_Journal_of_the_Operational_Research_Society.pdf)). Across about 40,000 stories, thinking words such as think, realize and because grow more frequent toward the end. Yet stories that followed this usual structure closely were not more popular ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)). Inference: fall then rise is a sensible default, and the final screens are where the reader makes sense of what happened.

Warmth earns the sadness. Toby Fox took EarthBound as his model because it is "funny and strange, but also sincere and emotional" ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/behind-humor-toby-foxs-undertale/)). His rule is "I just write what's funny" ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/interview-undertale-game-creator-toby-fox/)), and he made the scary king goofy after thinking of a friend ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgore)). Spiritfarer was meant to treat death "in a cozy and wholesome way," and its designer named the challenge as "balancing drama and lightheartedness" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-)). Ian Dallas aimed Edith Finch at something "very beautiful but also a little unsettling." He refuses to moralise about whether a longer, duller life is better: "I don't think there's an answer to that" ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/what-remains-of-edith-finch-interview/)). Inference: spend most of a story on warmth, play and small jokes the reader would remember, and a small part on the turn. Humour drawn from real, specific habits carries into the sad moment; generic jokes do not.

Sound does much of the emotional work when the visuals are simple. Jenova Chen starts prototyping with music because it is "the most effective and powerful medium that can create emotion" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-designing-a-new-emotional-experience-in-i-journey-i-)). Kan Gao says To the Moon's atmosphere relies almost entirely on its music because the visuals are simple ([YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)). Martin Stig Andersen kept Limbo near silent and made the boy's own sounds relatively loud, so the world would feel quiet ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/interview-with-martin-stig-andersen-composer-and-sound-designer-on-playdeads-limbo/)). Before Your Eyes layers music that changes with each blink ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)). Inference: one recurring melody, rearranged for the peak and the ending, is the cheapest strong tool a solo creator has. The author plays guitar, so they can record the melody and its variations themselves. Quiet stretches are something to spend carefully, because they let one sound land.

Endings that land share a few moves. Florence's early ending had a chance reunion decades later. It was dropped because "this part of Florence's life needed to end as it began," with her on her own. Wong: "You don't always have to have that traditional happy ending" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-)). Brothers' climax is a single press of the controls taught all game ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-emotional-story-bond-success/)). Celeste ends in acceptance: Madeline is "not just magically better" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)). Outer Wilds ends in understanding, not victory ([USC](https://digitallibrary.usc.edu/archive/Outer-Wilds--a-game-of-curiosity-driven-space-exploration-2A3BF163YX5A.html)). In That Dragon, Cancer the player's choices cannot change the outcome, "because we are telling a true story" ([Today](https://www.today.com/today/amp/tdna17506)). Small callbacks count too. In Firewatch, if teenagers call Henry a creep, he later uses the same word about a stranger, because the game picks "the truest and most specific thing" that can happen next ([GDC Game Narrative Review](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/GameNarrativeReview/Aimee%20Zhang_Firewatch%20GDC%20Game%20Narrative%20Review.pdf)). Inference, as a checklist: return to the opening image or verb; give the last action to the player; refuse a reunion or an explanation that would be dishonest; stop soon after the peak. In a branching story, give every path its own peak and its own strong end, since each reader remembers only their own path.

## Readers judge in seconds, stay for a person and come back to finish

Transportation, the state of being lost in a story, is one of the best-supported ideas in media psychology. A meta-analysis covered 132 effect sizes from 76 articles and over 21,000 participants. The levers on the story's side are **a character the reader can easily identify, a plot the reader can picture (a sequence of events, a climax and an outcome) and events that seem possible**. Transported readers feel more and argue back less ([City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/); [OnFiction](https://www.onfiction.ca/2014/02/research-bulletin-summarizing-over.html)). Busselle and Bilandzic argue that inconsistencies, with the real world or inside the story, break the reader's picture of the story and pull them out ([Communication Theory](https://academic.oup.com/ct/article/18/2/255/4098709)). That is a theory paper; the research did not check the experiments testing it. Making the hero resemble the audience buys little. Two meta-analyses found only small effects of similarity on identification (g = 0.19 and d = 0.14), and similar feelings and outlook mattered more than similar age, gender or background ([Current Psychology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-023-04842-4); [Communication Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502231204834)). Writing in first or third person made no difference in two experiments ([Frontiers in Communication](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2020.611935)), while one study found that present tense led to more identification with the narrator than past tense ([Diegesis](https://www.diegesis.uni-wuppertal.de/index.php/diegesis/article/download/214/294?inline=1)). Inference: personal, true stories come with built-in believability that invented polish can lose. Keep the real, specific details, and write the feeling everyone has had rather than aiming the hero at an audience profile.

The first seconds matter, but not for the reason usually given. The "8-second attention span, shorter than a goldfish" claim traces back to a report whose source pointed to no study ([Freakonomics transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/freakonomics/220-is-your-attention-span-shrinking-no-stupid-questions)). The popular "first 3 seconds" figures come from creators and vendors, not research. The solid evidence is different. A study of over 2 billion web page visits found that people screen a page quickly, and if it survives that screening they keep reading ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/understanding-web-browsing-behaviors-through-weibull-analysis-of-dwell-time/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Judgements of how appealing a page looks, made in 50 milliseconds, matched judgements made after longer viewing ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html)). The strongest cause-and-effect number in this area is about speed. Across 6.7 million viewers, **people began leaving videos that took more than 2 seconds to start, and each extra second raised the share who left by 5.8%**. Short videos were less forgiving than long ones ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/11/121113134805.htm)). Reading on screens costs some understanding (g = -0.21 across 54 studies). The cost is larger for factual text and under time pressure than for stories read at the reader's own pace ([Delgado et al.](https://www.uv.es/lasalgon/papers/Delgado%202018%20dont%20throw%20away%20your%20printed%20books.pdf)). Inference: the first line must appear almost instantly, with no splash screen or intro animation before it. The first screen should read as one clean image with one point of focus. The person and the question should be on screen within the first one or two taps. And the reader, not a timer, should move the text on.

Curiosity holds what the opening catches. Loewenstein describes curiosity as a felt gap in what we know ([Stafforini](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)). In trivia experiments, curiosity peaks when people half know the answer, a result repeated with new questions in 2023 ([Spitzer et al.](https://opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/117064/1/s00426-023-01841-9.pdf)). While curiosity is high, memory improves even for unrelated things shown at the same moment ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/)). Economists describe suspense as how much the next moment could change your expectations, and surprise as how much the last moment just did ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jpolec:doi:10.1086/677350)). Inference: every screen should either change what the reader expects or raise what rides on the next one. A screen that changes no expectation is a candidate for cutting. The line the author most wants remembered belongs just before a reveal (this is carried over from trivia experiments and has not been tested in stories).

What brings readers back is the urge to resume, not memory. Two studies with nearly 500 readers of written stories found that **a cliffhanger ending raised the wish to read the next part**, and readers did not enjoy it significantly less than a happy ending. One of the authors put it this way: "We don't like suspense. We like when suspense is resolved" ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2023/06/hahn-cliffhangers.html)). A study with 133 viewers of TV episodes found that cliffhangers raised excitement but not the intention to keep watching ([Wirz et al.](https://sonar.ch/documents/319990/files/2022_Wirz_Role.pdf)), so the effect does not hold everywhere. The famous Zeigarnik effect says unfinished tasks are remembered better. **A 2025 meta-analysis found no general support for it. What held was the Ovsiankina effect: people generally tend to go back to tasks that were interrupted** ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). Inference: for episodes, the job is not tricking memory but making it effortless to pick up again. Save the reader's place automatically and make "continue" one tap. Make the next episode the most obvious thing on the last screen. End an episode on an open question about a character the reader likes, and answer it early in the next one, because a hook left hanging is a debt.

Choice helps only when the reader can see it mattered. In one experiment, a linear text story that clearly acknowledged the reader's choices gave, in most cases, **the same sense of control as a story that truly branched** ([Fendt et al.](https://www.cs.uky.edu/%7Esgware/reading/papers/fendt2012achieving.pdf)). Readers feel more in control when choices lead to noticeably different content ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12716)) and when they care about the outcomes ([AAAI](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12437/12437-52-15965-1-2-20201228.pdf)). In one experiment with 216 people, adding choices to a story changed neither how much readers identified with the character nor how absorbed they were. A follow-up made the choices meaningful, and only then did they make a difference ([Frontiers in Communication](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.966944)). Interactive versions of a story mainly helped readers who enjoy effortful thinking ([University at Buffalo](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/need-for-cognition-transportability-and-engagement-with-interacti/)). Across four studies of games in general, feeling free to choose went with enjoyment and with wanting to play again, and easy, intuitive controls supported that feeling ([University of Rochester](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)). In story-choice games such as Life is Strange, players felt in control when deciding but often felt the game gave no meaningful sign that the decision mattered ([University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/games-institute/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/karinaarrambide_player-agency-decision-making-and-morality-in-ceinemetic-choice-based-adventure-games.pdf)). Unpacking rejected branching to avoid players' fear of missing out ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)). Inference: for a solo creator the evidence favours a fixed emotional backbone, with branches that rejoin it. Answer every choice on the very next screen and reflect it back at the end. Where a plot choice would threaten the peak, offer choices about how something is said or felt instead. Cut choices that change nothing, because on a phone every choice costs the reader effort and has to pay for itself.

## Honest, narrow slices under one fixed set of controls make the collection distinctive

Makers of personal games describe the same discipline. Nina Freeman, who made Cibele, says: "It would be easy to make myself look cool in a game about myself, but that's not my goal." She waited years "until I felt that I could write honestly about it," and narrowed the story to "the specifics of how and why I met up with this guy." Her filter is to ask **"what the most important details are that will communicate this experience as clearly as possible,"** and to cut the "extra stuff that is important to me as a person" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)). She says her method is closer to fiction writing than to autobiography. Abhi says Venba is "not 100% autobiographical," and he chose the mother's point of view because most immigrant stories are told from the children's side ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/07/19/venba-interview/)). Thorson did not know how Celeste would end "because I was still dealing with this stuff while we were making it" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692)). Papo & Yo's team found that a deeply personal story guided the game's direction but made its size harder to pin down ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-papo-yo-i-behind-the-scenes-making-a-game-that-matters)). Anna Anthropy treats frustration as material: "STRUGGLE is at the heart of any story worth telling" ([Autostraddle](https://www.autostraddle.com/anna-anthropy-the-autostraddle-interview-143395)). Yet she objected when a teacher used Dys4ia to show students what it is like to be trans, arguing that playing it is not the same as learning empathy ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/empathy-games-dont-exist/)). Inference: honest here means not flattering yourself; it does not mean including everything. Self-indulgence is the uncut version, every detail that mattered to the maker. Each story is best framed as one person's version, never as "what it is like to be" anyone.

Collections with a different look per story stay one work by keeping a few things the same underneath. What Remains of Edith Finch has 13 stories, each with its own art style and its own way of playing, reached through more than 13 prototypes ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/gdc-2018-see-edith-finchs-devs-weaved-13-prototypes-1-game)). Yet **every story uses only two sticks and one trigger**, and all movement uses the same springy motion, so everything feels alike. Lead designer Chris Bell: "Discovery and surprise comes from how using the same buttons gets wildly different responses" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-the-30-different-control-schemes-of-i-edith-finch-i-)). Its attitude ties the stories together too: each one is "someone's version of what happened" ([Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/interviews/interview-ian-dallas-talks-about-ambiguity-cut-features-and-magic-in-what-remains-of-edith-finch)). Sayonara Wild Hearts presents each level as a track on an album ([Uppercut](https://uppercutcrit.com/soundscapes-as-stories/)). Its composer described the process as "Level changes to fit music, music changes to fit level, repeat" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/09/hands_on_sayonara_wild_hearts_is_pop_perfection_in_video_game_form)). Love, Death and Robots shows the minimum that still works. Its creator says "there is no connective tissue," yet reviewers felt the episodes belong together through a shared tone and the title ([Syfy Wire](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/sxsw-love-death-robots-tim-miller-love-letter-to-nerds); [Loyola Maroon](https://www.LoyolaMaroon.com/10022790/life-times/review-netflixs-love-death-and-robots-is-a-burst-of-creativity/)). Inference: four things bind a collection. The first is a fixed set of controls; on a phone that means a few gestures such as tap, drag and hold. The second is one motion feel. The third is a frame object that is already a collection. The fourth is a shared attitude. With those four fixed, the art style can change freely from pixel to photoreal.

The menu can be part of the story. In Edith Finch, each dead family member's bedroom is sealed as a shrine, with a peephole and a nameplate showing their name and dates ([Hypercritic](https://hypercritic.org/collection/what-remains-of-edith-finch-2021-review)). The house's one-way climb from the basement to the loft makes time visible ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-4d-finch-house)). Sayonara's level menu is a track list. Unpacking's levels are a timeline of house moves ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)). Cibele is framed as a computer desktop of emails and journal entries ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-star-maid-s-i-cibele-i-)). Writing on in-world menus warns that they fail when they are hard to read ([Indieklem](https://indieklem.substack.com/p/19-the-diegetic-dilemma-benefits)). Inference: a good menu is a collection object from real life, its order means something, and you can see which stories are finished and which are not. On a phone, a clear vertical list styled as that object is safer than a scene the player has to explore to find the stories. The project's title already suggests one: an index of things the author meant to say. Like the Finch nameplates, each entry can tell a little of its story before it opens.

A distinctive collection also has to stand out in a scrolling feed. Marketing practitioners agree on a hook you can say in one line and show in one image, paired with something familiar that turns interest into a play ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=448), seen only through a search summary). The indie curator Joe Henson says the posts that stop people scrolling have "a strong visual identity" or "a mechanic that really stands out" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-secrets-to-viral-success-in-game-marketing-according-to-indie-game-joe)). Wordle is the small-web example. Josh Wardle saw players typing coloured squares to share results and built that grid into the game. It let people share a result without spoiling the puzzle, and the game went from under 1,000 players to 2 million in weeks ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2256733)). Inference: in a story collection, the feeling-verb is both the hook and the short clip. A share card should show the shape of the reader's path or feeling, never the content.

## Being found means tens to low thousands of plays, mostly through itch.io

Honest numbers from small free story games are modest. Tales from the Road, a Twine story, logged **3,010 views, 689 plays in the browser and 317 downloads over its lifetime. 98% of its traffic came from itch.io itself**, and its only marketing cost was $30 of art. Moving it from a download to a browser version lifted its weekly numbers from about 30 views and 5 downloads to 60 to 80 views and 30 to 50 plays ([itch.io devlog](https://iforgotmybrain.itch.io/tales-from-the-road/devlog/148788/3000-views-and-1000-plays-downloads)). For one solo journaling zine, itch.io tags were the "number one source of page views and downloads," while BlueSky "didn't produce much interest" ([postmortem](https://slasherepoch.bearblog.dev/toilet-skeleton-postmortem/)). Other baselines: a free visual novel had 169 downloads in its first month ([devlog](https://katy133.itch.io/mystery-miniaturist/devlog/283362/mystery-miniaturist-postmortem)), and a solo browser game had 250 views and 55 plays after a couple of months ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/11304564)).

itch.io sends players to games through its browse pages and tags. A staff comment, seen through a search summary, says the Popular section brings more traffic than Recent uploads ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/13235936)). A third-party guide says recent ratings, reviews and video reviews push games onto the Popular lists, and specific tags beat broad ones ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide)). Personal story games already gather under tags such as Bitsy combined with diarygames, introspective or vignette ([itch.io](https://itch.io/games/top-rated/free/tag-bitsy/tag-diarygames)). Game jams bring a guaranteed first audience: Porpentine's Twiny Jam, which asked for stories of about 300 words, drew 239 entries ([No Time To Play](https://notimetoplay.org/engines/easy-as.html)). The cover image shows up in browse pages, search and embeds, so it must read at thumbnail size with one point of focus ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide)). The Tales from the Road developer suspected that missing cover art was costing views. Chat apps build link previews from the page's preview image (og:image), which works best at 1200 by 630 pixels with text kept away from the edges ([DEV](https://dev.to/grabbit/open-graph-image-sizes-and-dimensions-the-complete-2026-guide-1k16)).

Short video is a weaker bet for this kind of project than it looks. The success stories with numbers are mostly from 2022 and 2023 and are about Steam games, such as one TikTok video that took a game from 400 to 20,000 wishlists ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/blogs/from-400-to-20-000-wishlists-with-1-video-how-tiktok-can-make-your-game-go-viral)). No 2025 or 2026 data turned up for free web story games. Inference: expect tens to a few thousand plays in the first months. Anything bigger depends on one share-worthy moment catching on with a community, a curator or a video maker, and no source shows that as predictable. The cheapest gains that repeat across the postmortems are playable in the browser, proper cover art, specific tags and jams. GitHub Pages can stay the home while itch.io works as an extra shop window (a browser upload of the game, or a page linking to the site). Each story can also travel on its own in a chat, with its own address, preview image and first screen, designed together as one piece.

## A text-first stack keeps phones fast and the coding agent effective

The engine choice comes down to how heavy it is on an iPhone and how well an AI coding agent can work with it. **Phaser 4** (v4.2.1, July 2026, MIT licence) is 345 KB compressed for the full build ([GitHub](https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser); [Phaser](https://www.phaser.io/download/phaser4)). **PixiJS 8** is about 200 KB ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-engines-comparison)). **Three.js** r186 (September 2026) trims down to about 150 to 200 KB for simple scenes ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases); [PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/threejs-vs-react-three-fiber-vs-babylonjs-3d-webgl-2026)). Godot 4.7 is the best full game engine for the web, but each exported story is about 9 MB compressed ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-engines-comparison)). On the web it runs only its simpler WebGL2 renderer and cannot export projects written in C#, and its own docs say Safari has WebGL 2.0 problems other browsers do not ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). A February 2026 bug report has a Godot web game crashing Safari on an iPhone 12 after repeated sound playback ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/116750)). Unity 6 is about 8 MB and has paid tiers above a revenue threshold ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-engines-comparison)), and forum advice is to keep memory use on iPhones "well below 500 MB" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/is-unity-6s-mobile-web-stable-enough-for-production-html5-games/1592637)). Defold is impressively small (0.83 MB) but uses the Lua language and its own editor ([Defold](https://defold.com/product/)). Kaplay's new major version is still in alpha ([GitHub](https://github.com/kaplayjs/kaplay/releases)). WebGPU, the newer graphics standard, reached iPhones only with Safari 26 on iOS 26 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). A WebGL2 path is therefore still required, which rules out WebGPU-only engines such as Babylon Lite as the only option ([Babylon Lite](https://www.babylonjs.com/lite/)). One caution: Phaser's download page links its engine licence to a Polyform Shield page, while the code repository states MIT ([Phaser](https://www.phaser.io/download/phaser4)). The Polyform link most likely covers Phaser's paid products, but that is unconfirmed and worth checking before relying on it.

Mixing engines in one site costs nothing if each story is its own page. Vite 8 has been stable since 12 March 2026 and now uses a faster bundler, Rolldown, written in Rust ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/05/vite-v8-rust/)). It builds a site with many pages without extra setup. Each story's index.html is listed as an entry, and the base path is set to the repository's address for GitHub Pages ([Vite docs](https://vite.dev/guide/build)). Phaser then loads only on the Phaser story, Three.js only on the 3D story, and the menu page stays tiny. GitHub Pages allows a published site of up to 1 GB, roughly 100 GB of traffic a month and deploys of up to 10 minutes. It forbids commercial use, and its limit of 10 builds per hour does not apply when deploying through a custom GitHub Actions workflow ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)). Inference (a suggested budget, not a published rule): keep the menu page under about 200 KB, the first playable screen of each story under about 2 to 5 MB, and the engine code under about 400 KB compressed. Phaser, PixiJS and Three.js fit; Godot and Unity do not.

The story text belongs in ink, inkle's scripting language for branching stories. inkjs 2.4.0 is the maintained, MIT-licensed JavaScript version, with TypeScript support, a small runtime kept apart from the compiler, and a command-line tool ([GitHub](https://github.com/y-lohse/inkjs)). Yarn Spinner still had no JavaScript version on its January 2026 roadmap ([Yarn Spinner](https://yarnspinner.dev/blog/yarn-spinner-in-2026)). Twine (2.12.1, October 2026) is excellent for pure text, but each story becomes a page Twine controls, which does not fit custom pixel or 3D scenes ([GitHub](https://github.com/klembot/twinejs/releases)). ChoiceScript forbids commercial use without a licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/dfabulich/choicescript)). Inference: ink also protects the author's voice in a project where an AI agent writes the code. The author writes plain .ink files with no code around the sentences. The agent connects tags such as "# bg: forest" or "# sfx: rain" to whatever drawing engine a story uses, so the same ink can drive a tap-through page, a Phaser walk-around or a Three.js scene. Stories are converted to JSON when the site is built, so phones download only the small runtime. A test can also walk through every choice and catch dead ends before any browser opens.

Phones set two traps. Secondary sources quoting WebKit say that Safari deletes a site's saved data, localStorage included, after 7 days of Safari use without a visit, unless the site has been added to the Home Screen ([browser-storage.com](https://www.browser-storage.com/browser-storage-fundamentals-quotas/storage-quotas-eviction-policies/ios-safari-7-day-storage-eviction-workaround/)). Saves therefore need a one-tap backup code and a gentle prompt to add the site to the Home Screen. Safari also treats web audio as background sound, so the iPhone's silent switch mutes it ([WebKit bug 237322](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322)). A WebKit engineer suggested setting navigator.audioSession.type to "playback" to fix this ([WebKit bug 251532](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=251532)). Audio still pauses when the page goes to the background ([WebKit bug 261554](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261554)), and browsers block sound until the first tap ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). Inference: a "tap to begin" title card for each story switches the sound on and also sets the mood.

Each look has a cheap, current way to make it. **Pixel art:** Aseprite costs $19.99 once, and its FAQ allows compiling it from source for free and using the result for commercial game art ([Aseprite FAQ](https://www.aseprite.org/faq/)). Pixelorama is the free, MIT-licensed alternative and is still actively developed ([itch.io](https://orama-interactive.itch.io/pixelorama/devlog/1504423/pixelorama-v1110-is-out-v12-enters-its-beta-stage)). To stay crisp on phones, pixel art must be scaled by whole numbers; at 2.5x some pixels draw 2 screen pixels wide and others 3 ([Cinevva](https://cinevva-site.pages.dev/tutorials/responsive-game-canvas)). It also needs the CSS setting image-rendering: pixelated and a canvas sized to the screen's real pixel density ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/canvas-hidipi)). **A realistic look:** build in Blender, export to glTF, and compress both the models (meshopt or Draco) and the textures (KTX2). On one test model, ETC1S texture compression cut 5.03 MB to 2.65 MB, while UASTC grew it to 13.44 MB ([GitHub](https://github.com/klich3/threejs-gltf-with-compressions-sample)). Lighting can be baked into the textures in Blender, so it costs the phone nothing while playing ([Educative](https://www.educative.io/courses/learn-threejs-for-computer-graphics/blender-to-bake-lightmaps-and-ambient-occlusion)). **Photoreal places are now cheap:** Scaniverse makes free 3D scans on the phone in a newer format called Gaussian splats ([SkyeBrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/polycam-vs-scaniverse)). Spark, an MIT-licensed tool for showing splats in Three.js, claims it runs fast even on low-powered phones ([Spark](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/overview/)). A third-party guide suggests staying under about 1 million splats per scene on phones ([Polyvia3D](https://polyvia3d.pages.dev/guides/gaussian-splatting-web-viewer)). These speed claims come from the makers and have not been independently tested. **Other looks** can come from one swappable final filter on the same Three.js setup. A Kuwahara filter gives a painted look ([Maxime Heckel](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-crafting-painterly-shaders)), and on its own it carried a whole watercolour project ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=113145)). A dithering filter gives a retro look ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/04/building-a-real-time-dithering-shader/)). Return of the Obra Dinn keeps its dithered look steady by fixing the dither pattern to a sphere around the camera and drawing outlines in a separate pass ([Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10365)). **Motion:** GSAP is now completely free, including the plugins that used to be paid; the one restriction is on building no-code animation tools that compete with Webflow ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). Rive's playback code is MIT-licensed; exports on the free plan show a splash screen, which the $9 a month Cadet plan removes ([Rive](https://rive.app/pricing)). Spine is the one tool where the site owner needs a paid editor licence just to play its animations on a website ([Esoteric forum](https://esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/27833-spine-web-player)). **Text:** the font Atkinson Hyperlegible Next has a free open licence (OFL) and covers 150+ languages ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/atkinson-hyperlegible-next/about)), and Apple's guidance sets 17 pt as the default text size on iPhones ([Apple HIG mirror](https://unpkg.com/apple-hig-mcp@1.1.3/content/typography.md)).

The sound should be his own. Howler.js is enough for music and effects, even though Snyk rates its maintenance "inactive" because its last release was in 2023 ([Snyk](https://snyk.io/advisor/npm-package/howler)). Tone.js is worth adding only where music has to follow the story on the beat ([Pistack](https://www.pistack.xyz/posts/2026-08-23-javascript-audio-libraries-howler-tonejs-wavesurfer-comparison/)). AAC audio in .m4a files is the safe choice for iPhones; WebKit's bug tracker shows years of problems with the Opus format in Safari ([WebKit bug 238546](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=238546)). The ITU puts typical loudness for games on portable devices at about -18 LKFS, plus or minus 2 ([ITU-R BS.2434](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BS.2434-2018-PDF-E.pdf)), with peaks kept at or below -1 dBTP ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-loud-should-your-game-be)). REAPER costs $60 for a personal licence after a 60-day full trial ([REAPER](https://www.reaper.fm/purchase.php)). Freesound, filtered to CC0, and Kenney's CC0 packs cover sound effects with no credit needed ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-sound-effects-music); [GTSTU](https://gtstu.com/?p=5011)).

The case against AI-generated art, music and voice is both legal and social. The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report says that writing prompts does not make you the author of the output. What the human made, and creative choices about selecting and arranging AI material, can still be protected ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/)). In the EU, Article 50 of the AI Act has required labels on deepfakes since 2 August 2026 ([MLGTS](https://www.mlgts.pt/en/knowledge/legal-alerts/Transparency-of-AI-Generated-Content/27005/); [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)). In GDC's 2026 survey of over 2,300 developers, **52% think generative AI harms the industry**, up from 18% two years earlier, and the most common use was research and brainstorming (81%) ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260129438528/en/2026-State-of-the-Game-Industry-Report-Reveals-Widening-Effect-of-Layoffs-Broader-Perspectives-on-Generative-AI-Unionization-Tariffs-and-More)). In a survey of 1,799 gamers who chose to take part, **85% rated generative AI in games below neutral**, and art and audio drew the most opposition ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games)). Steam games that disclosed AI use got 4 reviews on average in their first month, against 7 for games that did not. That points the same way but does not prove AI was the cause ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/steam-games-that-disclose-ai-use-may-sell-far-less-new-study-finds/)). Inference: writing code with an AI agent is the least contested use. Anything the reader sees or hears should be the author's own guitar, voice, photos and scans, which are also the cleanest legally and the most believable emotionally. Any AI-made asset that does ship deserves a plain note saying so.

The same choices suit an AI coding agent. PixiJS ships 25 official instruction packs for AI coding agents ([playfox](https://playfox.org/news/html5-engine-2026/)). A free MIT add-on connects Claude Code to a live Godot editor ([Godot Asset Library](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/5050)). One opinion piece names Godot and the web libraries (Phaser, Babylon, Three.js) as the most reliable choices for agents that work through text files ([Medium](https://chierhu.medium.com/ai-coding-tools-for-video-game-development-a-first-principles-analysis-of-what-actually-works-90dfa10edd13)). Testing closes the loop. Playwright can screenshot a page and compare it against a saved reference, with tolerances and masked areas ([Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots)). WebGL pages in a headless browser need a software graphics option (SwiftShader) switched on explicitly ([Chromium](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/docs/gpu/swiftshader.md)). In another project, removing one such setting changed the image enough to fail a screenshot comparison ([luma.gl](https://github.com/visgl/luma.gl/issues/2874)). Inference: stories built from plain web pages are the easiest to test, because a test can read their text directly. Canvas stories need a small built-in hook a test can call to make a choice, instead of tapping screen coordinates. On the development PC, Playwright's Safari engine does not start and only Edge works (from the project's environment notes). The Safari check is therefore a manual run on a real iPhone before each release. One more inference that the research notes do not cover: in a public repository anyone can read the ink files of unreleased episodes. Drafts that should stay unseen until release belong outside the public repository.

## The recommended stack and the principles to build by

The stack below follows from the evidence above. Everything is text (TypeScript, .ink, JSON) that an AI agent edits well. It costs nothing beyond optional one-off tools. Each story pays only for its own engine, and every piece runs on iPhones without WebGPU.

| Layer | Pick | Alternative or avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Site and build | One Vite 8 and TypeScript site with many pages: a menu page plus one page per story (stories/[slug]/index.html), base path set to the repository address | Splitting into several packages only if one story needs its own build tools |
| Hosting | GitHub Pages, deployed by the official GitHub Actions flow; an itch.io page as an extra shop window | Heavy video or uncompressed 3D (1 GB site cap) |
| Words | ink files, compiled with inkjs 2.4 when the site is built; tags drive each story's drawing | Twine only for quick text prototypes; avoid Yarn Spinner (no JavaScript version) and ChoiceScript (licence) |
| Tap-through and scroll stories | Plain HTML, CSS and SVG, animated with GSAP | A game engine where none is needed |
| Pixel walk-around stories | Phaser 4, scaled by whole numbers, with image-rendering: pixelated | PixiJS 8 for stories driven by artwork; avoid Kaplay 4000 (alpha) |
| Realistic or 3D stories | Three.js on WebGL2: compressed glTF models (meshopt) and textures (KTX2 ETC1S), lighting baked in Blender, Spark for scanned splats, one final filter per look | Babylon.js 9; avoid Unity 6 on the web and engines that need WebGPU |
| Characters | Sprite sheets drawn in Aseprite or Pixelorama; Rive for characters that react | Spine (needs a paid licence even to play animations) |
| Audio | The author's guitar and voice, recorded as separate tracks in REAPER; Howler to play them, Tone.js only for beat-synced music; AAC .m4a files; about -16 to -18 LUFS loudness with peaks at -1 dBTP; tap-to-begin card that switches sound on (audioSession "playback") | AI-generated music or voice in released stories |
| Type | Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for body text, one display font per story, fonts hosted on the site | Pixel fonts for long text |
| Saves | A small JSON record per story (episode, ink state, flags) in localStorage, a copyable backup code, a Home Screen prompt | Saves with no backup on iPhones |
| Testing | A script that walks every ink choice; Playwright on a 390 px wide phone screen, with test hooks and a few reference screenshots; a run on a real iPhone before each release | Tests that tap screen coordinates on a canvas |
| Shared parts, built once | Saves, the sound unlock, the ink runner that turns tags into drawing commands, the episode loader that fetches only the current episode's files, the menu | Rebuilding these for each story |

The runner-up is a Godot 4.7 web export for the one story that needs a visual editor or real 3D level building, sitting behind the same Vite menu page. Use the single-threaded export and the lighter WebGL2 renderer, trim the export template, and write dialogue in GDScript, since C# cannot be exported to the web. Test it early on a real iPhone. Inference: the honest way to settle doubts between engines is a quick test of three scenes (one plain web page story, one Phaser scene, one Three.js scene) on a real iPhone, because no source measures frame rates on real iPhones.

The principles boil the research down to rules to check each story against.

| Principle | What it means in practice | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| Find the verb that is the feeling | Start from the thought, look for the one touch action that acts it out, and drop or park the thought if none fits | Florence, Edith Finch, Unpacking's cut subplot |
| Teach it, then turn it | Repeat the verb until it is automatic; at the peak, change what it means or take it away | Brothers, Florence, Before Your Eyes |
| Never score the feeling | No points, timers, game-overs or skill tests; an action that fails on purpose is allowed | Edith Finch, Gris, Venba, Anthropy, Celeste |
| Build the peak and the last screen first | A moment of sudden closeness, shown rather than announced; end soon after; return to the opening image or verb | Peak-end meta-analysis, kama muta, Florence |
| Earn the sadness with warmth | Most of the story is warmth and real, specific humour; no lesson at the end | Undertale, Spiritfarer, Edith Finch |
| Open on a person and a question, instantly | The first line appears with no splash screen; one focal image; a want the reader can almost guess | Transportation research, fast page screening, load-delay data, curiosity research |
| Let the reader set the pace | Taps move the text on, never a timer; keep one action available even in heavy moments; no peak that depends on vibration | Screen-reading meta-analysis, cutscene critiques |
| Answer every choice | Show the result on the next screen and again at the end; keep the emotional backbone fixed; cut choices that change nothing | Fendt et al., Waterloo dissertation, Unpacking |
| End episodes open, start them by closing | End on a question about someone the reader likes; answer it early next time; save automatically and resume with one tap | Cliffhanger studies, Ovsiankina effect |
| Tell one narrow, honest version | Keep what communicates; do not flatter yourself; one person's version, never a lesson about a group | Freeman, Anthropy, Edith Finch |
| Change the look, never the controls | Same few gestures, motion feel, frame and attitude in every story; the menu is a collection object that shows what is finished | Edith Finch, Sayonara Wild Hearts |
| Let each story travel alone, and keep it the author's own | Each story has its own link, preview image and cover readable at thumbnail size; a share card with no spoilers; the author's own sound and images | Wordle, itch.io postmortems, AI surveys |
| Test the feeling, not the fun | Watch real readers to see whether the peak moves them; check on a real iPhone | Florence's discarded prototype, the null result for messages labelled as moving |

## Conclusion

The project's limits work in its favour. Phones, free static hosting and code written by an AI agent all push toward short, touch-driven, text-first pieces that load fast and let the reader set the pace. That is the same form the research on emotion and attention favours, so technology is not the real risk. The real risk is whether each chosen verb actually carries its feeling, and the only way to find out is to watch a few real readers, as Florence's team did when they threw away a prototype that looked right but did not move anyone.

A few decisions also do triple duty. The feeling-verb drives the emotion, makes the clip that stops a scroll, and gives the share card its shape. The frame behind "What I Meant" ties the different styles together, turns the menu into part of the story and doubles as the one-line pitch. Some things remain unknown. No study tests the peak-end rule on interactive stories, no source measures how fast these engines run on real iPhones, the cliffhanger evidence differs between written stories and video, and reach for free personal web games is small and hard to predict. Inference: the sensible order is to test the three engines on a real iPhone first, then finish one complete short story and test its peak on readers who know nothing about it, and only then start the second story, instead of building all the shared machinery up front.
