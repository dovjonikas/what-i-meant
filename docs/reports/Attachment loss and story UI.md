# Let each loss break a ritual

Players grieve a character in proportion to how close the bond felt, and the time spent with that character adds little: in a survey of 381 viewers, the felt intensity of a bond predicted expected distress at losing the character at r = .58, while hours of viewing reached r = .15. For "Text me when you get home" that means each person who dies needs a few two-way moments (a line said to "you", a habit the player sees at least three times, an act the player performs for them) and then a death that breaks the habit, told in one cold line, with the extra time spent afterward on the routine with a hole in it and the thing left behind. Being moved, the feeling that makes sad stories worth playing, rises with closeness and care shown at the loss and drops when the viewer is pushed to reappraise it, so the cheap versions are deaths that order the player to mourn, name the feeling, or come so close together that each dilutes the next. Measured this way, Dad's thread in the draft already works, while the dog has no name and shows its gate ritual in full only once, Grandpa never speaks to "you", and Mom dies a few taps after Dad inside the same run of years with no close scene of her own. On the phone, nothing the player must read should leave on a timer, a choice needs a look that no caption shares, every dot needs its name beside it, and every jump back to the porch needs the same marks. Colour amplifies feeling more than it creates it (saturation sets intensity, brightness sets pleasantness), so it does most here as a ledger of people: each person's hue sits in the world while they live, leaves when they die and returns once when the old you sends "home" to Mom. Much of this rests on film clips, colour patches, small samples and designer accounts, and no study has varied how many scenes a game character gets before dying, so each change at the end comes with a playtest.

## Bond intensity predicts distress at a character's loss at r = .58, viewing hours at .15

Attachment to a fictional person forms quickly and then grows slowly. In a four-week study of 80 viewers watching a season at home, parasocial relationships were "moderately strong after initial exposure to the character", with only small gains in later weeks ([UNB Scholar](https://unbscholar.lib.unb.ca/items/27f818b0-0f5a-4902-bf8a-98a5aaba416b/full)). The pain of a loss tracks how intense the bond feels. In Cohen's survey of 381 viewers, the intensity of the parasocial bond correlated with anticipated distress at losing a favourite character at **r = .58**, hours of viewing correlated at **r = .15**, and controlling for hours left the first figure unchanged ([Cohen 2004](https://www.gwern.net/doc/culture/2004-cohen.pdf)). Real bereavement has the same shape: among 211 students grieving a pet and 146 grieving a person, **closeness to the one who died was by far the strongest predictor of grief**, and most other predictors lost significance once closeness was in the model ([Death Studies](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c460t-ca8190e850401a790e69db87132f2c9cade08815d94dc7be77573052a6c01fb93/Details)). A meta-analysis of about 120 studies tied parasocial bonds to perceived similarity, identification and transportation, and found no link to loneliness ([Tukachinsky, Walter and Saucier](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/comm_articles/53/)).

The levers that raise that intensity are fairly well mapped, though several rest on one study each. Being addressed helps: in an experiment with 198 viewers, a performer who turned to the audience with body and voice produced a more intense parasocial experience ([Hartmann and Goldhoorn](https://typeset.io/papers/horton-and-wohl-revisited-exploring-viewers-experience-of-2x3qk052xt)). A single identified victim with a name and a picture drew considerably more donations than an unidentified one, while naming each member of a group of eight did nothing ([Kogut and Ritov](https://pluto.huji.ac.il/%7Emsiritov/KogutRitovIdentified.pdf)). People asked to strike a small toy robot hesitated longer when it came with a name and a few habits, a "Frank" who likes to play and sometimes escapes but never gets far ([MIT record](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/mit-1721.1%2F109059); [Yes Magazine interview](https://www.yesmagazine.org/health-happiness/2015/07/15/should-we-be-kind-to-robots-kate-darling)). Forty-five minutes of escalating self-disclosure made strangers closer than small talk did, though its authors doubted it builds loyalty or commitment ([Aron et al.](https://stafforini.com/docs/Aron%20et%20al%20-%20The%20experimental%20generation%20of%20interpersonal%20closeness.pdf)). Dependence counts: **240 students felt more empathy for a beaten puppy or adult dog than for a beaten adult, and as much as for a one-year-old child** ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/472644)). So does the player's own investment. Satisfaction and investment predicted commitment to TV characters such as Homer Simpson ([Branch, Wilson and Agnew](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/psychpubs/63)), and people valued things they built themselves more, unless the build was left unfinished or destroyed ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/12136084)). Couples who laughed together more felt closer, in a correlational study of 71 couples ([Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_laughter_brings_us_together)). The popular idea that doing someone a favour makes you like them rests on a small 1969 study and is a hypothesis at best ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Franklin_effect)).

Game makers found the same levers by testing. Portal's playtesters would not carry a box through a long puzzle; the fix was a level where the box could not be destroyed, more events built around it, and lines calling it the player's only friend, after which, in Kim Swift's words, "no one ever forgot the box" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/best-of-gdc-the-secrets-of-i-portal-i-s-huge-success)). Florence's team traced a key revision to realising they "hadn't shown why these characters are good for each other" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)). The Last of Us built Ellie's AI so she could never expose the player, because a companion who does that has "broken the relationship" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020364/Ellie-Buddy-AI-in-The)), and Rockstar made the horse in Red Dead Redemption 2 vulnerable, with dead horses staying dead, "so it feels personal and real" ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/red-dead-redemption-2-horse-permadeath/)). A survey of 213 players sorted attachment to game characters into seven forms, from admiring a capable character to "concern for one's protégé" ([Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/6cef35fe-1653-4627-912d-79a8947070fe); [DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2787/2771/2830)), and among 74 players, attachment to non-player characters followed their friendliness and practical usefulness ([Middlesex](https://repository.mdx.ac.uk/item/83q53)). In 121 accounts of moving game moments, most players said they enjoyed and valued the sadness ([Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/negative-emotion-positive-experience-emotionally-moving-moments-i/)).

None of this needs a face. Viewers of Heider and Simmel's 1944 film of two triangles and a circle described a social story full of intentions, and a 2021 study with new coloured videos found the same ([original paper](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/others/1944/heider1944a.pdf); [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/33678806/)). Ten actors with covered faces conveyed anger, fear, happiness and sadness through movement alone, but **sadness was the one emotion that exaggerated movement did not make clearer** ([Perception](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p5096)). Thomas Was Alone's coloured rectangles, each with a personality tied to its one ability, drew more care than its maker expected ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/50410/a-tale-of-friendship-the-making-of-thomas-was-alone/)). What a faceless design gives up is the baby face: infantile facial features raised cuteness ratings and gaze in young children for human, dog and cat faces alike ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00411/pdf)). Pets otherwise draw on the caregiving system built for children and offer an unconditional regard rare between people ([Archer](https://www.scilit.net/publications/c3fa3e57d0e0259ef4e21e130ae3c698)), and a dog's greeting grows with absence: twelve dogs filmed alone at home greeted their owners with more tail wagging and contact seeking after two or four hours than after thirty minutes ([Rehn and Keeling](https://slunik.slu.se/kursfiler/DO0031/40007.1213/The_effect_of_time_left_alone_at_home_on_dog_welfare.pdf)). Teenagers expect to be more upset than adults by losing a favourite character ([Cohen 2003](https://www.csus.edu/faculty/m/fred.molitor/docs/parasocial-breakups.pdf)), and the player is sixteen when the dog dies.

Inference: the author's verdict on the draft is that the deaths do not hurt enough. By the evidence above that is a problem of intensity, so more minutes per person will help less than a few two-way moments. A working budget for each person who dies, untested: a quirk the player can recognise in one line, a habit seen at least three times, one want the player learns, one thing said to "you" that would not be said to everyone, one or two acts the player performs for or with them, and a last, slightly broken repetition of the habit just before the death. Against that budget the draft is uneven.

| Person, age at death | Already in the draft | Missing by the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Grandpa, 12 | A quirk in one line (an apple peeled in one unbroken curl), shown three times, the third time broken; after his death Grandma peels, and hers break | He never speaks to "you" and the player never does anything with him; everything after the garden is told while the years run |
| The dog, 16 | The puppy who steals shoes, the gate where he waits, a last walk the player knows is the last and holds, Dad's two flat lines, the empty gate | A name; the gate greeting seen in full only once before it fades; no earlier act of care for the last-walk hold to echo |
| Grandma, 29 | A smell, a jar at every visit, "come visit more" said and later texted in capitals, the weekend choice, a promise she dies before, a jar left in the fridge | Little; a disclosure is the one lever unused |
| Dad, 58 | "ok." with a full stop, "I've got you" returned decades later, "proud of you." as his one disclosure, years of "home" typed by the player's own thumb, the last one unanswered | Nothing structural; this thread is the model for the others |
| Mom, 66 | Signed texts, the lit kitchen and "I couldn't sleep", the thumb photos, her own "Home." after a visit, a weather text as the unknowing last message, her side of the night revealed at the end | A close scene between Dad's death and hers; on screen she dies a few taps after him in the same run of years |

Inference: Mom is where the budget and the twist pull apart. Her disclosure is the reveal itself (she was awake, typing and deleting), and a disclosure while she lives would spend it early, so what her thread lacks is a close scene, and the disclosure can stay with the ending. The seven forms of attachment also argue for losses that differ in kind: the dog is a protégé the teenager is responsible for, Dad a paragon whose approval was rationed, Grandma the one who always asked for more of you. Since faceless people act through motion, each needs a signature movement kept across ages, and the sad beats should rest on stillness, absence and text, because sadness is the emotion bodies carry worst. The puppy's youth has to come from the body: smaller and rounder, a large head, a clumsy bounce, a short high sound (the body side of the baby schema was not checked in a source here). The Ellie rule applies to every scene just before a death: nobody should be annoying, preachy or a chore right before they go.

## Deaths move when love sits beside them and the telling stays cold

People enjoy sad stories for being moved, and sadness is its fuel. In a cinema study with 76 viewers and 38 clips, sadness and enjoyment correlated strongly, but **feeling moved almost fully mediated the link** ([openHSU](https://openhsu.ub.hsu-hh.de/handle/10.24405/10791)), and the pleasure of sad music runs through being moved in the same way ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00439/full)). Appraisals of closeness and moral acts explained being moved better than they explained sadness ([Seibt et al.](https://biomed.reviewer.ly/app/publication/051d490e-e7e9-4d7d-9941-6c703e2c9aa6)), and in 144 viewers strong kama muta came with goosebumps, warmer skin and smiling-muscle activity, a physiology distinct from sadness ([Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/psyp.13662)). Fiske's theory of kama muta names memorials, keepsakes and photographs as triggers because they link the living with the dead ([Taylor and Francis](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9780367220952-10/disasters-memorials-mementos-alan-page-fiske)). In film scenes viewers chose themselves, tears and goosebumps arriving together marked the top of a graded scale of peak moments, and prosocial behaviour was central to both ([Frontiers](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00041/text)).

Being moved is fragile. **Viewers told to suppress their feelings were less moved and cried less than viewers given no instruction, and viewers told to reappraise were moved less still** ([ISCTE repository](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/handle/10071/11974?locale=en)). The Distancing-Embracing model explains why people seek negative feeling in art: the frame of art and control over how long one stays give distance, and a mix of positive and negative feeling, aesthetic pleasure and meaning-making then let people embrace it ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FAAF19734BAB559CD439BB46F8BDC571/S0140525X17000309a.pdf/div-class-title-the-distancing-embracing-model-of-the-enjoyment-of-negative-emotions-in-art-reception-div.pdf)); critics call parts of it too loose to test. Laughter belongs inside grief: bereaved adults who laughed genuinely while talking about a dead spouse months later showed less anger and better social relations ([Keltner and Bonanno](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/keltner.laughter.jpsp.1997.pdf)). The effect can reach past the game. Among 361 students who watched an abridged *Atonement*, more sadness led to more writing about real people they were close to, which raised their happiness, while viewers whose thoughts turned to themselves gained nothing ([Ohio State News](https://news.osu.edu/smiling-through-the-tears-study-shows-how-tearjerkers-make-people-happier---ohio-state-research-and-innovation-communications/)). Memory of an episode keeps its peak and its end, with length barely counting ([Fredrickson and Kahneman](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/fredr-kahneman-jpsp1993.pdf)).

Practitioners converge on short, cold deaths with the setup paid off afterward. Chekhov advised a writer in 1892 to be colder when depicting sad people, because restraint gives their grief a background to stand out against ([ArtsJournal](https://www.artsjournal.com/aboutlastnight/2018/04/almanac-chekhov-on-writers-of-fiction-and-their-characters.html)). Pixar whittled **30 to 40 minutes of material** for Up's married-life sequence down to music and pictures, and Pete Docter found that "the less we had the more emotional it felt" ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/924676/30-minutes-had-to-be-cut-from-the-opening-sequence-of-up); [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2022/06/14/movies/up-first-10-minutes-making-of)). What Remains of Edith Finch puts a family tree screen after each death to give the player a pause, and Ian Dallas calls the known inevitability of each death "oddly liberating" because the player cannot fail ([God is a Geek](https://www.godisageek.com/2017/04/interview-with-ian-dallas-creative-director-on-what-remains-of-edith-finch/); [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/feature/interviews/interview-ian-dallas-talks-about-ambiguity-cut-features-and-magic-in-what-remains-of-edith-finch)). Josef Fares mapped each brother in Brothers to one hand so that after the older one dies "you actually lose one physical hand", and calls the final button press "the essence of the whole game itself" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)). Tetsuya Nomura argued that death should be "sudden and unexpected" and that killing more of the party would dilute Aerith's, and Final Fantasy VII's team cut a plan to kill most of the party back to her alone ([Fanlore](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Aerith); [Critical Hits](https://criticalhits.com.br/games/squaresoft-quase-matou-um-monte-de-gente-em-final-fantasy-vii-e-nao-apenas-aerith/)), both secondary sources. The documented cheap version is Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare's "Press F to pay respects", mocked for giving grief the same command form as reloading and for refusing to go on until the button is pressed ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/call-duty-doesnt-understand-grief-then-who-does/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/all-due-respect-press-f-for-farce)).

Grieving players set the outer limits. Twelve bereaved adults who played Spiritfarer and Bear's Restaurant for a week most often reviewed the meaning of their loss (24% of coded instances), but the two who avoided engaging had both lost someone close suddenly, and death scenes like their own pushed them away. **Games with no time limits, fail states or penalties supported reflection**, scenes of care and support prompted gratitude, and 16% of coded instances were plans for closer relationships ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426378/)); the study is small and claims no clinical benefit. Grief research now reads continued contact with the dead as healthy ([Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12124-022-09679-3)): mourners address the dead on their social profiles ([Stanford](https://sml.stanford.edu/publications/2011/i-said-your-name-empty-room-grieving-and-continuing-bonds-facebook)), and a 2025 scoping review lists instant messaging among the tools people use to talk to the dead ([Utrecht](https://ddd17.sites.uu.nl/wp-content/uploads/sites/1084/2025/09/Digital-DeathTEch-Communication-Scoping-Review.pdf)). In three experiments, people who recalled or performed a ritual after a loss grieved less, through regained control, and in the lottery study reading about a ritual did not help ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23398180/)).

Sound shapes which kind of hurt a loss gives. Film music that matched the story deepened immersion, and music that conflicted with it erased the effect ([OnFiction](https://www.onfiction.ca/2015/08/research-bulletin-how-music-affects.html)); 177 students read characters' emotions in line with the music under them ([Sonicfield](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/viewers-interpretations-of-film-characters-emotions-effects-of-presenting-film-m)). In 30 viewers of *Joker*, scenes without music disrupted alignment with the protagonist and brought discomfort and re-evaluation ([CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/film-music-silence-and-moral-alignment-a-joker-case-study-in-northern-cyprus/)). No study gives a number of seconds to linger on a loss.

Inference: the author also called the deaths too fast and too abrupt. The craft evidence supports short deaths; what several deaths in the draft lack is the before and the after, and control over time. A working shape for each loss has three beats. Before: an ordinary or funny moment inside the habit that will break. The moment: one cold, factual line or image, with no feeling named. After: the habit with the hole in it, the thing left behind, and a pause the player ends. The extra time belongs in the third beat. The ladder below keeps the top of the scale for the ending, as the graded-peak result suggests.

| Loss | Rung | The moment in the draft | The after in the draft | Sound in the draft, and the fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grandpa, 12 | Low; teaches what a ring on the life lines means | "the first one", while the years run, then a line explaining that you get it later | Grandma's peels that break | No change; the explaining line works like reappraisal, which suits only the lowest rung |
| The dog, 16 | Middle | Dad's two flat lines | The empty gate | Grief cue at the gate; fits |
| Grandma, 29 | Middle | Mom's text that never says the word | One jar left, unopened | The music drops out at the text; better kept for Dad |
| Dad, 58 | High | "home", Delivered, no "ok." | The thumb still types "home" after visits | The dropout; the one place where unease is the goal |
| Mom, 66 | High | Her weather text, signed | The porch, then rain and a jacket | The dropout again; her own motif returning fits a loved loss |
| The ending | Peak | The kid's "home.", her side of the night, then "home" sent to her | The light off, sleep | The theme in full |

Inference: the two parents need the full three beats because the ending pays them off, and Grandpa and Grandma can stay brief on purpose. Five deaths in under half an hour is far denser than Final Fantasy VII's one, so Nomura's dilution warning bites here: Mom's death needs its own stretch of years and one close scene after Dad's death, and her after-beat belongs in a scene, such as a place that was always lit when you came home, now dark. Every loss beat should wait for the player's tap, which gives the control over duration that both the Distancing-Embracing model and the bereaved players point to. The last walk with the dog is care, so a hold there is right; a prompt that orders a goodbye with no way past it would be the Press F failure. The continuing-bonds research makes the old you's message to Mom's number a real behaviour that can be played straight, and the ritual studies suggest it helps when the player performs it, which the draft's typed-by-hand "home" already does. The kid's "home." should stay relief, so the full return of colour and music lands on the send to Mom and the peak is not split.

## On a phone, nothing the player must read should leave on a timer

Adults read silently at an average of **238 words per minute for non-fiction and 260 for fiction**, across 190 studies and 18,573 people ([Brysbaert](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/linguistics/2019-brysbaert.pdf)). Subtitle standards, which pair text with pictures, budget far less: Netflix allows up to **20 characters per second for adult programmes and 17 for children's**, at most 42 characters per line and two lines, and at least five sixths of a second per subtitle ([Netflix](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide); [Netflix](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements)), and BBC guidance is reported at 160 to 180 words per minute ([Clevercast](https://clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines), secondary). Ren'Py, the common visual novel engine, shows text instantly by default, keeps auto-forward off and lets skip pass only text already read ([Ren'Py](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/preferences.html)). Readers need to look back: masking each word once the eyes had passed it lowered comprehension ([Schotter, Tran and Rayner](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797614531148)). inkle's Jon Ingold warns that players given "at least half an excuse not to read something" will not, and often pick a choice without reading the text before it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games)). The Game Accessibility Guidelines ask at their basic tier that players move through text at their own pace ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)).

Inference: a word-by-word reveal is harmless while it runs faster than reading and a tap completes it. At the time of writing the draft adds a word every 55 ms (about 1,100 words a minute) and a tap shows the whole line, so it holds nobody back. What reads as too fast are the things that leave on their own: a chat that closes a few seconds after its last message, a lock screen that fades when it carries no line, years that keep running while a name appears. The rule that follows is that nothing the player must read leaves without a tap. Where a timer has to stay, subtitle arithmetic gives the floor: 0.35 seconds per word plus half a second to find the text, never under 0.83 seconds. A history view of the last 20 to 30 captions and messages, the pattern Ren'Py ships ([Ren'Py](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/history.html)), covers anyone who tapped past something.

Touch sets its own limits. WCAG 2.2 sets 24 by 24 CSS pixels as the minimum target and recommends the stricter 44 by 44 for important controls; Apple's minimum is 44 points and Material's 48 dp ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html); [Front-End Checklist](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/accessibility/touch-targets)). In 1,333 observations, **49% of people touching a phone held it in one hand** ([UXmatters](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)), and thumbs do about three quarters of phone interactions, with the effortless zone at the bottom of the screen ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-hold-our-gadgets/)). Feedback should arrive within about 100 ms to feel physical ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). WebKit has never shipped the Vibration API on iOS, and Chrome allows it only after a user gesture ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/vibration/reports/implementation.html)), so no meaning can rest on vibration.

Choices carry agency when they are acknowledged and later echoed. In a non-branching story that answered each choice at once, players felt about as much agency as in a branching one, differing only on whether the story would have been different (p = 0.030), the item a later reference to earlier choices addresses ([Fendt et al.](https://www.cs.uky.edu/%7Esgware/reading/papers/fendt2012achieving.pdf)), and moral, social and consequential choices felt meaningful and raised appreciation ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/ItenSO18)). Reigns gave plain swipes weight by tying them to four stats and writing short questions with snappy answers ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-creating-an-adaptive-narrative-in-i-reigns-i-)). No controlled study of timed choices was found; one critique argues that short, vague prompts under a timer make players act against their intentions ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=225595)). Inference, for the author's note that choices were unclear: at the time of writing the draft marks a real choice with a small "your call" and hand typing with "tap to type", both 11 px and dim, and it draws one-option replies (which work as send buttons) with the same chips as real choices. Ingold's account says players skip that kind of small print. A choice needs a state no caption shares, changed in several ways at once: the tap dot gone, the mark in the same place every time at a readable size, bordered chips in the bottom third. One-option replies should look like a send. Background taps while a choice waits should pulse the options, the picked option should stay lit for 300 to 600 ms, and the next line should answer it. Choices stay untimed.

The life lines are a chart, and chart research applies. Labels placed beside the data cut comprehension time and eye movements compared with a legend ([Huestegge and Philipp](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-011-0155-1.pdf)), and the gain holds once a graph passes a threshold of complexity ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13414-018-1484-0)). Animated trends were the least effective form for analysis and the fastest and most enjoyable for presentation, "but it does lead to many participant errors" ([Robertson et al.](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/%7Estasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf)); on phones, 96 crowdworkers were faster with small multiples than with animation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03919)). WCAG asks for 4.5:1 contrast for text and 3:1 for graphics needed to understand content, never colour alone, and a way to pause content that moves by itself for more than five seconds beside other content ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html); [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html); [W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html); [W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). **About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a colour vision deficiency** ([Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)). Inference, for the note about names on dots: every dot that matters carries its name whenever it is on screen, at 14 px or more and weight 600, on a halo or pill that reaches 4.5:1 over any painting; a name pushed more than about 12 px from its dot gets a short leader line; on exit the name outlasts the dot, so a loss is read and not missed. A run of years that introduces a name should dwell on it for 1.2 to 1.5 seconds, a tap should pause the years and another resume them, and holding can keep speeding them up as it does now. No study compares direct labels and legends on phones or in animated timelines, so these values are starting points.

The jump back to the porch needs the same marks every time. The accessibility guidelines list "avoid any sudden unexpected movement or events" ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)), and NN/g's 100 to 500 ms range for animation applies to controls, so a scene cut can run longer ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). Inference, for the note about the return to the present: a jump with no visible cause reads as a reset. Each return should carry a fade through black of about 600 to 900 ms, the lock screen with the time and the minutes since the send, the clock tick when sound is on, and the phone as the element that persists across the jump. The draft has these marks; what remains is that a lock screen with no line leaves after a few seconds, that Noon ends without a return, and that memories and the porch now share the present tense. One study found present tense raised identification ([Diegesis](https://www.diegesis.uni-wuppertal.de/index.php/diegesis/article/download/214/294?inline=1)), so keeping it for memories is reasonable, but then pictures and sound alone have to mark the frame.

Sessions on the web are decided early. Most departures from a page happen in the first 10 seconds, and visitors who stay 30 seconds often stay two minutes or more ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Since iOS 26 every site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/?p=1042117)), but only Chromium browsers fire an install prompt ([Can I use](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_beforeinstallpromptevent)). Safari deletes a site's script-written storage after 7 days of use without visiting it, with a separate day counter for Home Screen apps; the sources are from 2020 and were not confirmed for 2026 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)). Lifeline's real-time waits worked as pacing on a first play ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/lifeline-3-minute-games-interview)) and, by one review, fell apart on replays ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/?p=170487)). Readability studies favour body text of 18 points or more ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/RelloPM16)), and Apple's minimum for any text is 11 points ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Inference: the start screen is the first painted scene with Start in the thumb zone, the first input is a plain tap, and a small act on the phone comes inside 30 seconds, which the cold open already allows. The story saves on every beat and reopens with a one-line recap. An install offer appears once, on a chapter card or the end card, and never on the lock screen, which is part of the fiction and where a reminder of being a player would break immersion ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/05/120507131948.htm)). The small labels that carry plot, above all the "Read" that starts the final wait, deserve more than 11 px.

## Saturation drives intensity and brightness drives pleasantness, but colour alone moves little

The lab evidence gives a plain rule. In ratings of Munsell chips, **pleasure rose mostly with brightness (0.69 brightness plus 0.22 saturation) and arousal mostly with saturation (0.60 saturation minus 0.31 brightness)** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/)). A tighter study of 62 people looking at large colour patches for 30 seconds each, with ratings, skin conductance and heart rate, found saturation the strongest lever on arousal (partial eta squared .693); hue raised arousal from blue to green to red only at medium and high saturation; medium saturation was the most pleasant; brightness raised pleasantness from low to medium with no gain above; and greys were the least pleasant and briefly slowed the heart ([Wilms and Oberfeld](https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/oberfeld/downloads/Wilms-Oberfeld2018_Article_ColorAndEmotionEffectsOfHueSat.pdf)). The caution comes from the one study inside a mobile game: **21 adolescents playing a 25-minute platformer showed no significant change in emotional state when hue and saturation varied by level**, and its authors conclude colour alone does not elicit emotion in mobile games ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19064)). In real photographs colour amplified both pleasure and displeasure compared with grayscale, and at half saturation negative photos were rated more unpleasant still ([Psychonomic Society](https://featuredcontent.psychonomic.org/colorful-shades-of-emotional-perception/)).

Preferences and meanings are broadly shared. People like the colours of liked objects: in 48 raters, liking peaked at blue and bottomed out at chartreuse, dark yellow and dark orange, and the liking of colour-matched objects predicted preference at **r = .893** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2889342)). Pair harmony rises with hue similarity, while a figure is liked more the more its hue contrasts with its background ([Palmer lab](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/Schloss&Palmer%282011%29.pdf)). Across **4,598 people in 30 nations, colour-emotion associations matched at r = .88** ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/169496/)): red goes with both love and anger, yellow with joy, black and grey with sadness, white with relief, though white also reads as sad in China ([BBC Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/revealing-the-psychology-of-colour-to-be-a-rainbow-rollercoaster-of-emotion), a press report). Bright reads as good and dark as bad in speeded judgements, a result replicated with 980 people ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.0963-7214.2004.01502002.x/); [Tuebingen](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/66655)). People feel more nostalgic in colder rooms and on colder days, and nostalgia makes them feel warmer ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/12/121203082050.htm)). Two popular claims are gone: a study saying sadness impairs blue-yellow perception was retracted ([PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/feeling-blue-and-seeing-blue-sadness-may-impair-color-perception/)), and physical warmth priming interpersonal warmth failed to replicate in three labs ([Lancaster](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/69546/)).

Film and game craft turns this into story. In Up, Ellie brings colour into Carl's life, the world dulls after her death and colour returns through memories of her and through Russell ([/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/503975/cool-stuff-lou-romanos-color-script-for-pixars-up/)); her pink stays through the film even in her absence, and the pink light on the house dims as Carl walks in alone ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2022/06/14/movies/up-first-10-minutes-making-of)). Wolfwalkers' rule was that every time the forest became more colourful, the town became more desaturated ([Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/director-tomm-moore-reveals-new-details-about-wolfwalkers-coming-to-apple-tv-annecy-wip-193335.html)). Makoto Shinkai puts magic-hour light at transitions and moments on the cusp ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/trading-places-makoto-shinkai-your-name)). Schindler's List keeps one red coat in a black and white crowd and later identifies the girl among the dead by it ([NoFilmSchool](https://nofilmschool.com/girl-in-the-red-coat-in-schindlers-list)); Inside keeps one red sweater, and Gris starts grey and earns its colours back ([NME](https://www.nme.com/features/colour-me-happy-colour-and-symbolism-in-video-games-2835574)). Firewatch tied a colour script to narrative moments and found the sky can fill half the screen and set most of its colour ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)), Journey's team said the contrast of emotions from level to level mattered most ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/kellee-santiago-describes-the-making-of-thatgamecompanys-journey/)), and Florence's director kept the palette very simple and said the feeling "has to come from the interactions" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)). Colourists grade memory warm and soft: Black Mirror's "Eulogy" gave each period of a life its own look, with low-contrast Polaroid nostalgia against a clean present ([Digital Media World](https://digitalmediaworld.tv/post/colourist-sam-chynoweth-walks-the-line-from-memory-to-life-for-black-mirror)), and Lupin's flashbacks were warmer and brighter than its present ([AFC](https://afcinema.com/Grading-and-finishing-Netflix-s-Lupin-with-BaseLight-by-FilmLight-at-Mikros.html)).

Inference: the colour script follows six rules. Each person owns one hue for life, a little of it sits in the world while they live (a roof, a door, a field), and it leaves the world when they die until the end. Red belongs to Mom, and only things that are red in life (the cold open's clock digits, tail lights going down the hill) share it as alarm, so the reveal can show that the red of the cold open was her all along. The present on the porch is cold petrol teal, dark and nearly still, so each warm memory lands by contrast. Memory chapters lift their blacks and warm their rims, while the present keeps near-black ink and a cool rim. Neighbouring chapters change at least two of hue family, saturation and brightness: the palettes measured for the notes behind this report had every sky top between hue 249 and 289 at similar chroma and differed mainly in lightness, which is why they read as times of day. A death lands in two steps, the moment at about half the chapter's chroma and the aftermath near grey with one accent in the lost person's colour. The send can stay at night, carried by the whitest thing on screen and a warm window, and first light can come after "You sleep fine." as a last still of the next morning, a better end after the peak. The porch horizon's small lift at each return then reads as a moon rising. The return of the dead's colours belongs at the send to Mom, and the kid's "home." gets only the warm window and the white message.

Colour also has to stay readable. The people's colours collide in places. Names on the dots stay the main cue whatever the colours do.

The order is an inference: the author's two strongest notes on the draft were that the deaths do not hurt enough and that things move too fast, so the changes that touch the losses and the pace come first. Every value is a starting point for playtests, and each change describes structure; any wording is for the author to write.

## Conclusion

Inference: the four questions turn out to share one mechanism, and Dad's thread already shows it. A repeated act the player can predict builds the bond, the same act failing once is the loss, the interface decides whether that failure is felt or missed, and colour can mark the absence afterward. Seen this way, the author's two notes on the draft are one problem. The deaths in the draft are short, which the craft evidence supports, but several happen while the years run and close by themselves, so the player watches them from above and never gets to choose how long to stay. The fix is to hand the player the tap that ends each one, and the deaths themselves can stay short. The same logic explains why the final send to Mom should stay a typed act: in one of the ritual studies performing a ritual helped after a loss and reading about one did not, so the last word of the story only resolves the life if the player's own thumb sends it.

The central number is still unknown. No study has varied how many scenes a game character gets before dying and measured grief, so the per-person budget above is a hypothesis, and an A/B on Grandpa (with and without one act the player performs, same death) would test it directly in a small playtest. Colour has the weakest evidence of the four areas for this use, since the one study inside a mobile game found no effect of colour alone; the palette is best judged by whether players can say whose colour left the world, a question that takes a minute to ask after play.
