# Hand the player the last message

The first story lands if the player does its most important act with their own thumb. Early on, the player receives "text me when you get home" as a small chore and taps a reply. On one night in the leaving-home years the message never comes. At the end the player types the same words in the same composer to someone younger, presses send and sees "Delivered", and the story ends before any reply. Research on being moved (kama muta, tested in 19 countries and 15 languages with 3,542 people) ties the lump in the throat, moist eyes, chills and a warm chest to a sudden intensification of a close bond, and an older account of sentimentality adds the order that works: an attachment is awakened, the reader comes to expect it will not be honoured, and then it is honoured all at once. That order is the spine of this story, and people often smile while it happens. The player feels the life as their own when "you" is a faceless flat figure and every tap is something a person in that memory would do; matching the hero's age or background to the player's buys little. Smiles come from running jokes planted early so they can come back changed, each placed one beat before a turn. Visual novels supply the moment-to-moment craft: the first question lands within the first few taps, each tap carries one idea, pictures change on beats one element at a time, and a small real sound such as a ticking clock can stand in for dead silence. The music is one short melody with a leaning note, heard as a music box in childhood, shifted in mode, tempo and register act by act, cut to bare room sound on the empty night and brought back slower and lower in the parents' last years, with a new voice entering under it at the final send. The colour follows the finding that brightness drives how pleasant a colour feels and saturation drives how intense it feels, moving from soft pastel at birth through saturated childhood, warm gold for love, flatter routine and near grey for loss, back to colour one hue at a time and a blue night with warm windows at the end. In Canvas 2D the look is five to seven layers tinted toward a fog colour by distance, painted once per scene to offscreen canvases and only slid at whole-pixel offsets during play, with the pixel ratio capped at 2 and no ctx.filter or shadowBlur on Safari. The numbers and palettes below are untested starting points built from the research, and the story has not yet met a reader.

## The first minute plants the message, and each tap carries one idea

The openings that work in this genre drop the player into something ordinary and plant one small thread without explaining it. Florence starts on a 25-year-old's flat routine of work and social media, and the inciting event is that her phone dies and she follows the sound of a cello ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_%28video_game%29)). A Short Hike mentions in its opening, with no urgency, that Claire is waiting for a call; players forget it among the errands, and the ending turns the whole climb into Claire working up to that call ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/05/06/i-forgot-i-was-waiting-on-a-phone-call/)). When it comes at the summit, her mother admits she had surgery and says she is proud ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)). Memory stories often start at the end. To the Moon begins with a dying man and travels back through his life to find the reason for his last wish ([YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)). Before Your Eyes opens after death, in a boat, with a ferryman who reads the soul's blinks (seen through a search summary of [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes)). Firewatch, the visual reference for this story, opens with a short text prologue because its writer needed Henry's backstory inside every player within five to ten minutes, and text let him do that without ever describing Henry ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2016/02/the-video-game-firewatch-and-its-origins-in-1980s-text-adventures.html)).

Visual novels, the closest form to a tap-through story, show the same patterns and name the failure. Ace Attorney opens each case with a short scene of the murder and only then hands the player the defence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Wright:_Ace_Attorney), seen through a search summary). Its first case was written after the second, as a quick tutorial, because the second did not work as an opener ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/ace-attorney-creator-on-initial-prototype-and-nearly-suspending-development-maya-and-mias-origins-more/2), search summary). Katawa Shoujo's prologue has its hero collapse with a heart attack just after a classmate confesses to him, then moves him to a new school ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo)). Long Live the Queen sets a clock in the first minute: a 14-year-old princess must survive 40 weeks to her coronation ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Long_Live_the_Queen_%28video_game%29)). Doki Doki Literature Club hides something wrong inside a familiar, tropey romance, and its maker expects players to start out mocking it and end up caring about the characters ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999)). The openings critics attack are the slow ones. One critic says Higurashi's mystery does not really start until about halfway through its sixth chapter and calls that long opening contentious ([Medium](https://medium.com/@primeforillusion/notes-on-higurashi-question-arcs-bb1ffc63ee4b)). Critics say Steins;Gate front-loads its setup with no payoff until the second half ([A Piece of Anime](https://apieceofanime.com/2017/12/17/the-meticulous-structure-of-steinsgate/), search summary), and PC Gamer found that VA-11 Hall-A's conversations dragged with only a few words per click ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/va-11-hall-a-review/), search summary).

The first seconds are a screening test. Across over 2 billion page visits, people judged a page quickly and, once it passed, kept reading ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Across 6.7 million viewers, people began leaving videos that took **more than 2 seconds to start, and each extra second raised the share who left by 5.8%** ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/11/121113134805.htm)). The inference is that the first screen has to appear at once and read as one image with one point of focus. Browsers block sound until the first tap ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). Safari also treats web audio as background sound that the iPhone's silent switch mutes ([WebKit bug 237322](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322)), and a WebKit engineer suggested setting navigator.audioSession.type to "playback" to fix that ([WebKit bug 251532](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=251532)). No tutorial is needed for a texting story: the maker of Bury me, my Love points out that anyone who has texted already knows how to play ([CMF](https://cmf-fmc.ca/?p=69550)).

Inference: the opening has no title card beyond the tap that turns the sound on, and no instructions. Two shapes fit the research. The direct one is a phone lock screen at night with one notification from Mom carrying the message, and the player's first tap answers it, so the thread is planted within five seconds and nothing explains it. The other is a cold open of the kind To the Moon, Ace Attorney and Katawa Shoujo use, where an event near the end comes first and a hard cut leads back to the start. The porch scene already drafted in the repo's scenes.js (an old house on a hill at night, somebody just drove away) is such a frame: it can open the story, cut to birth and come back at the end, so the last screen returns to the first image. Its risk is giving the ending away, so the frame should show only the lock-screen clock and a half-typed draft whose words stay hidden until the last scene. Either way, the research on being moved suggests the first minute should also hold one small, sudden act of love next to a joke, such as a parent's hand closing around a newborn's or a grandparent's silly name for the baby, so the player is caught by warmth as well as curiosity.

Inference, from the visual novels: Ace Attorney's opener was written last and used to teach the one interaction, so this opening can also be written once the rest exists and teach the reply inside the fiction. The first question should be planted by about the third to fifth tap, since on a phone leaving costs one swipe. If the story touches grief, the tap-to-begin card can carry one calm line saying so, as Being a DIK's store text warns up front that it is not meant for players going through a dark period ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1126320/), search summary). The slow-opening complaints and the comedy-first tear-jerkers also fit together: once the question is planted, the warm childhood scenes can take their time, because the player is already holding the thread.

Holding a player across a 25 to 30 minute story is mostly a matter of sittings. Florence tells a relationship in **20 chapters grouped into six acts in about 30 minutes** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_%28video_game%29)), and Passage tells a whole life in about five ([Rohrer](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)). GameAnalytics' 2025 report puts **median mobile sessions at 5 to 6 minutes** and top-tier games at 8 to 9 ([GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)), so many players will finish across several sittings. A 2025 meta-analysis found no general memory advantage for unfinished tasks, but a reliable urge to go back to interrupted ones ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). Safari deletes a site's saved data after 7 days of use without a visit unless the site is on the Home Screen ([browser-storage.com](https://www.browser-storage.com/browser-storage-fundamentals-quotas/storage-quotas-eviction-policies/ios-safari-7-day-storage-eviction-workaround/)). Memory of an experience is dominated by its peak and its end, with duration having essentially no effect (**r = 0.58 across 174 effect sizes**) ([OUCI](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4NG6JZvl)). Inference: about 15 to 25 memories of one to two minutes each, grouped into six or seven acts of four to seven minutes, so one act fits one sitting. The story saves after every memory and reopens on the lines view at the saved age with one "continue" tap. Each act ends on an open message, such as an unread notification or a typing indicator that stops. When the story runs long, cut a memory before adding one.

Inside a memory, the pace lives in the tap. A visual novel developer found that a pause at the end of most sentences, giving one sentence per click, is the most natural base rhythm. Pausing at every comma frustrates readers. Two sentences shown together read as one fast unit, and a pause after each short fragment mirrors halting thought ([His Own Being devlog](https://davisgsee.itch.io/his-own-being/devlog/1007030/controlling-nvl-mode-sentence-pacing-in-renpy)). A small text box of a few lines makes readers feel they are moving forward fast, while full-screen text slows the story and suits inner thought. Tricks such as one word centred on an empty screen lose their effect when used often ([Towards the End Sky](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2022/10/nvl-vs-adv-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html)). Inference: one to three short lines per tap, under about 90 characters as a first guess to test on a 390 px screen, with tap to advance everywhere and no auto-advance in normal text. A moment that matters slows down through more taps, one fragment each, and never through a timer. A held beat the player cannot tap past, of one to two seconds, belongs only at a peak, such as right after the message is sent. One empty screen with a single word, or a scene with no text at all, works once or twice in the whole story. The tap breaks are part of the author's text: the author marks them in the story files, and the code never splits a line by itself.

## A faceless you and a real phone make the life the player's own

Scott McCloud's masking idea holds that a simple, iconic face invites readers to see themselves in it while a detailed face reads as someone else. Critics note that what counts as simple is cultural and that readers also connect through plot and personality ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masking_%28comics%29)). The best direct test, a 2024 study of science comics, found that **cartoonish characters narrowed the identification gap** between Black and white US participants, and that **less detailed backgrounds raised identification in every condition**, which contradicts part of McCloud ([metatoc](https://metatoc.com/papers/180897-visual-realism-and-character-identification-in-science-comics-an-empirical-study-testing-mccloud-s-masking-effect-theory)). Games that want the player inside the hero strip identity away. Journey's figure has no name or visible arms, and other players' names stay hidden until the credits ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_%282012_video_game%29)). Alto's team keeps its characters out of marketing so players see themselves in the world ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-team-alto-s-i-alto-s-odyssey-i-)). Unpacking shows no character at all and lets players fill the gaps ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)). Florence keeps its art simple and its palette restrained so players bring their own experience to it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)). On the reader's side, a hero who resembles the reader helps only a little (**g = 0.19 across 39 studies**), and shared feelings and outlook count for more than shared age, gender or background ([Current Psychology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-023-04842-4)). One study found no difference between first and second person but more identification with present tense ([Diegesis](https://www.diegesis.uni-wuppertal.de/index.php/diegesis/article/download/214/294?inline=1)), and a small qualitative study found readers took on "you" more readily when "you" had a positive role ([Reading Digital Fiction](https://readingdigitalfiction.com/second-person-narration/)).

Inference: nobody in the story has a face, so the player never sees someone else's face beside their own, and posture does the acting: the angle of a head, the line of the shoulders, the gap between two figures, a hand held or let go. Age shows through proportion (a big head on a small body), height against others, a straighter or more bent back, and props such as a stroller, a bike or a cane. Identity cues stay ambiguous at phone size. Backgrounds are simplest at each act's key beat, where identification matters most, and the wide establishing shots carry the detail. The figure is small in the frame for reflective beats and larger only for intimate ones. The text runs in present tense, and "you" gets a life the player would be glad to own, faults included.

The verbs decide whether the life feels lived or watched. Before Your Eyes moves the story on with each blink, so players lose moments whether they want to or not, and its designers put each scene's key beat first so a player who blinks early still gets the story ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gone-before-you-blink-the-webcam-driven-gameplay-of-before-your-eyes)). Spiritfarer made care a verb with the hug ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/inside-the-thoughtful-design-of-thunder-lotus-i-spiritfarer-i-)). To the Moon split its critics over its tile puzzles, which some found paced the story and others found a distraction from it ([Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/52620/to-the-moon-switch-review); [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/to-the-moon-review/)). Inference: four or five verbs serve the whole life, each a real gesture. Hold to stay, tap to reply, swipe a notification away, press and hold to send, and do nothing to let a moment pass. They repeat until the final use lands, and there are no puzzles. One thing can be missable in the Before Your Eyes way: a call from a grandparent slides in as a banner during another memory, and if it is not tapped in time it is gone. The grandparent's line ends a few years later in the lines view, and no text judges either choice.

Real messaging habits can carry the years. Emily is Away covers four years of a friendship in one chat client, one conversation a year, with away messages, icons and bios changing between years; in the last year, for the first time, the player is the one who logs off ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)). Its maker added a typing line the original client never had, because it opened up story possibilities ([We Got This Covered](https://wegotthiscovered.com/gaming/interview-kyle-seeley-emily-is-away/2)), and the player mashes keys to type a chosen line that the character may delete and soften before sending ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2015/10/16/no-longer-if-comp-2015-emily-is-away/)). The typing indicator signals presence without content, and when it vanishes people read it as a promise left unfinished ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2016/05/20/tech/typing-awareness-indicators-and-a-certain-kind-of-chat-app-hell-4df2cd948001)).

Inference: one thread with Mom (later Dad) lasts the whole life. Each visit scrolls to the bottom under a new date separator, so the gap between separators is the time skip, and the player can scroll up to find their teenage "ok" under her long paragraph. Her typing style ages with her, from caps lock and the wrong emoji to shorter messages. Any tap on the in-story keyboard types the next few letters of a fixed line, so the player physically types words the author wrote; a line can delete itself and become something shorter before it is sent. The indicator that types for a long time and then sends only "ok", and later the one that appears and vanishes with nothing sent, are used two or three times in the whole story at most. One message is typed and never sent, and it can reappear later, untouched, in the composer. The phone stays generic and drawn in the story's own flat palette, with bubbles on the left for them and on the right for you. How iMessage's Delivered and Read labels behave was not checked, so they should be compared with a real phone before they are drawn.

Choices should be few and visibly costly. Florence is linear partly so that players with more life experience could not pick the answers they already knew to be right ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_%28video_game%29)). Passage's optional spouse makes you less agile and puts some chests out of reach, while exploring together scores double ([Rohrer](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)). In one experiment, a linear story that clearly acknowledged the reader's choices left readers feeling about as much in control as a story that truly branched ([Fendt et al.](https://www.cs.uky.edu/%7Esgware/reading/papers/fendt2012achieving.pdf)). Unpacking tells a life through about 1,000 objects over seven moves, and its team accepted that what happens between moves goes unshown and that players fill it in ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)). Visual novels add two models. Steins;Gate's in-game phone shows incoming texts with underlined words that the player taps to choose a reply; most messages need no answer, and the replies steer the plot ([Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Steins%3BGate), search summary). In Katawa Shoujo the first week is the only wide-open stretch, and a player who never commits to anyone reaches the bad ending ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katawa_Shoujo)). In an aggregate of Steam reviews of Being a DIK, well-written characters and meaningful choices are among the most cited praise, and the game lets choices change how characters treat the player ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320)). One player wrote that it made them ask whether the hurt they caused others was worth it ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1126320/discussions/0/3191364956936750205/), search summary). Planetarian has no choices at all and is still praised ([Caltrops](https://www.caltrops.com/2014/09/19/planetarian-the-reverie-of-a-little-planet/), search summary). Inference: one or two real choices across the life, each answered on the next screen and visible later in the lines view, with the rest linear. Most messages are read-only, so the few that ask for a reply feel heavy, and a reply is chosen by tapping a word in the received message before the keyboard types it out. Not answering, or answering late, can itself be the costly choice. A few hidden flags (did you text back, did you show up) are enough, each paid off once later, and a peak that grows out of the player's own earlier choice lands harder than one that only happens to the character. Time jumps show through a changed object (the same phone, a newer model; the same kitchen, a different mug) and never through a caption.

## Jokes planted early come back as the lump in the throat

Being moved has a well-mapped trigger. In Zickfeld and colleagues' study across 19 countries, kama muta held up as a distinct positive emotion set off by **a sudden increase in closeness between people**, felt as warmth in the chest, moist eyes, chills and a lump in the throat ([ISCTE repository](https://repositorio-iul.iscte.pt/handle/10071/16718)). In seven samples from five countries, viewers of touching videos felt it as mostly positive and came away feeling close to the characters. The same paper's review cites Frijda's account of sentimentality as a precise order: an attachment is awakened, the viewer is led to expect it will not be honoured, and then it is honoured abruptly ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4zv550pt)). A physiology study found that strong kama muta went with goosebumps, warmer skin, rising skin conductance and **activity in the smiling muscle**, with no clear link to tears ([ISCTE](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/tears-of-joy-aesthetic-chills-and-heartwarming-feelings-physiological-correlates-of-kama-muta/73544)). The lump itself is explained only in popular science, as throat muscles caught between an open glottis and a held-back sob ([BBC Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/the-human-body/lump-throat-cry)); no peer-reviewed study of it turned up. Labelling something as moving does nothing: in four pre-registered studies with 1,049 people, messages built to be moving had no direct effect, and every effect ran through how moved people actually felt ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1112910)). Journey, built by cutting anything that did not serve the intended emotion, made 3 of 25 testers cry at the end ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_%282012_video_game%29)).

Inference: each peak in the story is a bond becoming suddenly visible. A death by itself produces grief, and it moves people only when it reveals the love. The night nobody texts is the second step of Frijda's order, and sending the message decades later is the third. Because being moved shows in the smiling muscle, the last beat can end on a small smile and still land. A realistic goal is that a minority of readers cry, and Journey's ratio is a fair benchmark for that.

Humour belongs right beside the sadness. Watching Life Is Beautiful with separate happy and sad buttons, **about half the viewers pressed both at once**, if only briefly ([Larsen et al.](https://typeset.io/pdf/can-people-feel-happy-and-sad-at-the-same-time-5dlzxfsaku.pdf)), and a bittersweet film clip produced more simultaneous mixed feelings than a control clip even when viewers were told not to expect them ([Larsen and McGraw](https://typeset.io/pdf/further-evidence-for-mixed-emotions-1bav3o3u2c.pdf)). Excitation-transfer theory holds that leftover arousal from one moment amplifies the reaction to the next, whatever the feeling ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excitation-transfer_theory)). No experiment was found showing that a joke before a sad beat makes the sad beat stronger, so that part rests on theory and craft. The craft is consistent. Up's marriage montage runs as a light fairy tale of zoo jobs, a painted house, clouds shaped like animals and babies and a nursery being decorated, right up to a slow pan to a darkened doctor's office. It was first written with dialogue, then made silent, then stripped of its sound effects, and the director found it grew more moving each time something was removed; the team also tried cutting the miscarriage and found the film less affecting without it ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2022/06/14/movies/up-first-10-minutes-making-of)). He has said every shot in the montage sets up something used later ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/70909/15-adventurous-facts-about)). To the Moon's odd paper rabbits and toy platypus turn out to carry its ending ([Gnome Stew](https://gnomestew.com/steampowered-to-the-moon-review/)), its constant jokes read as a way of coping with its sentiment ([High Def Digest](https://www.highdefdigest.com/blog/?p=44069)), and players who named its saddest moments also said the long action sequence before the end weakened them ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/206440/discussions/0/846959520866767232)). Visual novel players report the same mix. Reviewers of Being a DIK say it moves smoothly between comedy and drama ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320)), and players of Butterfly Soup praise its awkward teenage comedy and say in the same breath that its ending made them cry (self-selected [itch.io comments](https://brianna-lei.itch.io/butterfly-soup)). A critic of Higurashi argues that its cheerful village days deserve to be read on their own terms, because the blend of everyday life and horror is what makes the tragedy ([Medium](https://medium.com/@primeforillusion/notes-on-higurashi-question-arcs-bb1ffc63ee4b)). The risk shows in Clannad, which some reviewers charge with pulling at emotions to lift an average show ([MyAnimeList](https://myanimelist.net/reviews.php?id=188118), search summary).

Inference: every running joke gets a last appearance with a new meaning, and each joke sits one beat before a turn, never inside the peak line. The message is the main one. Mom sends it when you are 15, you roll your eyes, and a long reply is typed and deleted down to "ok". Years later nobody sends it. At the end you send it. Mom's texting style is a second thread (caps lock, the wrong emoji, "ok." with a full stop), so its later silence hurts. The online friend's tell, such as an indicator that types far too long or a silly sign-off, is funny every time and appears once more when that friend shows up for you at a bad hour. A childhood handshake is half-forgotten in the teens and remembered by one of you decades later. A parent's silly name for you becomes the name you use, gently, for them. These are shapes for the author's own material, since humour drawn from real, specific habits carries into a sad moment and generic jokes do not. A small table in the code (each motif, the acts it appears in, what it means each time) keeps the payoffs from being lost, and the last act stays short and quiet. The childhood comedy has to be worth reading even if nothing sad ever followed. The turns happen inside scenes, with a joke beside a sad line, so the story never splits into a funny half and a sad half, and no sad music plays under a scene that has not earned it.

Several beats rest on findings about real lives. Tim Urban's The Tail End counts that, having seen his parents on about 90% of days until 18 and about 10 days a year since, he had **used about 93% of his in-person time with them by the end of high school** ([Wait But Why](https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/12/the-tail-end.html)). The arithmetic holds under his assumptions (about 5,913 of about 6,373 days), though it is one person's illustration. Friendships fade with less contact much faster than family ties do ([Roberts and Dunbar](https://core.ac.uk/download/131226437.pdf)), and a close friendship takes **more than 200 hours together**, with hanging out and joking counting far more than working side by side ([University of Kansas](https://news.ku.edu/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend)). Over a lifetime, regrets about things not done tend to loom larger than regrets about things done, though some of those regrets are wistful and only some are painful ([Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1998/varieties-of-regret-a-debate-and-partial-resolution)). The lifetime pattern failed one preregistered replication ([FORRT](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.357/)) and came out differently in another ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10282588/)). Daniel Pink's crowd-sourced survey of more than 15,000 regrets, which is not a random sample, puts drifted relationships among the main kinds; his advice when unsure is to reach out, because the door is often still open ([Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/what-is-the-power-of-regret-a-conversation-with-daniel-pink/)).

Inference: in the lines view, the parents' lines sit close to yours through childhood and drift thinner and further away after leaving home, long before they end. The gap between still alive and still near is the content, and the only number on screen is your age. The best friend's drift is silent and two-sided. Later, one tap lets you reach out; if you do, the friend answers warmly, and if you do not, that door closes near the end without the story punishing it. If the lines view is openly inspired by the @lessonsinline account, the credits should name it by its handle; the account exists ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/lessonsinline/)), but nothing about its maker or format could be verified.

Gerontology describes the change with ageing parents as filial maturity. After a mid-life filial crisis, the adult child sees the parent as a person with needs and becomes someone the parent can depend on ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10804-015-9207-4)), and the relationship turns more horizontal ([OasisBR](https://oasisbr.ibict.br/vufind/Record/RCAP_779e00c713e99bd8a1ead4e449ca4ded)). Role reversal is a different and mostly negative construct in that literature, about children taking on parental roles too early ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10826-019-01490-w)), and Brody argued that being depended on by a parent means something different from being depended on by a child ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/20/1/761)). Work on dementia care describes the adult child's task as grieving and letting go of old rules about the relationship ([Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/bfb2ebfd7d6c4f419d4b194143f2c363)). Inference: the story mirrors earlier gestures (you tie their shoe; you wait up for them) while the parent stays an adult with a history, a joke only they would make and a memory they correct you on. A small scene where the parent forgets the running joke and you finish it for them is a likely lump-in-the-throat beat.

Endings that land hand the last action to the player and reverse a role. Emily is Away ends with the player logging off for the first time after years of Emily doing it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)), and Brothers' last playable moment uses the dead brother's button ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/brothers-tale-of-two-sons-remake-ending-older-younger-1851302128)). After A Short Hike, one player's essay ends by telling readers to call their mothers ([blog essay](https://accordion-sprout-l88t.squarespace.com/blog/a-short-hike-or-another-great-reason-to-call-your-mom)), which is the response this story hopes for, reached without being asked. Critics punish endings that steer feelings for their own sake. PopMatters, borrowing Cocteau's phrase about sentimental blackmail, faults stories that move characters like pawns to produce tragedy, while granting that all art manipulates ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/173815--2495737897.html)). Fake-out deaths are a common reason emotional endings fall flat ([WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/gaming/10-emotional-video-game-endings-that-fell-completely-flat)), and Florence's ending drew the most criticism of anything in it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_%28video_game%29)). Kayleen Schaefer found that every woman she asked says the phrase to friends, framed as safety and even more as solidarity ([TPR](https://www.tpr.org/2018-02-11/text-me-when-you-get-home)). Doki Doki Literature Club's maker argues that a dark tone need not mean hopelessness ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/doki-doki-literature-clubs-horror-was-born-from-a-love-1819724999)), which fits a story about coming home: its peak lands on connection. Inference: the last action is the player typing and sending the message to someone whose line began late in theirs; "Delivered" appears, and the story ends before "Read" or any reply. Nothing on the ending screen tells the player to text their mother or shows statistics, and any share prompt lives elsewhere. Because the phrase also passes between friends, a friend can send it in the middle years, which widens the thread beyond the parents.

## One melody and one colour script change together, act by act

Music research points to expectation as the source of tears and chills. In Sloboda's study of 83 listeners, over 80% reported physical responses to music such as shivers, tears or a lump in the throat. **Tears went most reliably with melodic sequences and appoggiaturas** (a leaning note that resolves by a step), and shivers with new or unexpected harmonies ([Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/28754222606c17abaae30191e67bc59d)). Later work tied chills to a new voice or instrument entering, to crescendos and to a solo set against the accompaniment, with the caveat that these features arrive together. In one experiment, cutting the chill passages reduced chills only marginally (p = 0.049), and passages with the same features in a different context did not work, so **context decides** ([Bannister and Eerola](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02046/full)). A review of 167 reports models chills through arousal, expectation and emotion ([QMUL](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/75159)), and 988 tracks people named as giving them chills were **sadder than matched controls**, and in exploratory results slower, less intense and more instrumental ([CAS](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.7c30c521a2214a22a9e6c8d3dd069773)). In the brain, dopamine release splits between the anticipation (the caudate) and the peak itself (the nucleus accumbens) ([Salimpoor et al.](https://www.zlab.mcgill.ca/publications/docs/salimpoor_2011_nn.pdf)). For mood, listeners rating 200 systematically varied examples weighted **mode most, then tempo and register**, and the cues added up with little interaction, explaining 77 to 89% of the ratings ([Eerola et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3726864/)).

Composers in this genre work the same way. Up's waltz for Ellie is first heard slow and spare, filled out in the marriage montage, developed again in the cue "Carl Goes Up", and given its fullest form in the end credits ([Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/up.html?mobile=yes)). Journey has one theme that evolves through the whole game, with a solo cello standing for the player ([SixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2012/03/15/interview-journey-composer-austin-wintory/)). Florence gives each lover an instrument, a cello for him and a piano for her ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-i-florence-i-to-convey-the-ineffable-feeling-of-being-in-love)). To the Moon's maker says its atmosphere rests almost entirely on the music because the art is simple ([YourClassical](https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2013/11/07/kan-gao-and-to-the-moon-on-top-score)), and that the hard part was timing the cues ([Analog Addiction](https://analogaddiction.org/2014/09/05/to-the-moon-director-talks-about-a-bird-story-part-1)). Games follow a story with vertical layering, where synced parts are switched on and off, and horizontal resequencing, where the music jumps between loopable sections ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/blogs/pure-vertical-layering-for-game-music-composers-from-spyder-to-sackboy-gdc-2021-); [Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/how-to-use-interactive-music-to-score-gameplay/)).

Visual novels lean on music even harder, because they use few sound effects. A pause in the music lifts the tracks on both sides of it, and tracks heard too often lose their effect. A theme can return in a sombre arrangement for sad moments, as Piofiore does with its jazz ([Towards the End Sky](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2025/12/soundtrack-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html)). Some players feel that sudden silence breaks immersion, and Fate/stay night is praised for replacing music with a ticking clock, footsteps, silverware or leaves ([Fuwanovel](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/topic/4615-sound-design-in-visual-novels), search summary). One visual novel, Stretch, dropped background music entirely for over 350 ambient sounds chosen per scene ([Stretch devlog](https://kirishimashoichi.itch.io/stretch-en/devlog/966792/a-visual-novel-with-no-bgm-why-we-chose-silence)). The most common complaint in Being a DIK's review aggregate is the lack of voice acting and sound ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1126320)), so a text-only story should expect players to notice where sound is missing.

Inference: write one melody of six to eight singable notes with one appoggiatura at a phrase end, and make every act a variation the player does not consciously notice until the last act. It first appears in childhood on a music box, high and staccato in a major key, faster than it will ever be heard again. Each person who matters gets a voice of their own that answers the theme, as Florence does with its two instruments. Before each peak, spend 10 to 20 seconds building (a held drone under a rising filter, or the first half of the melody repeated) and land the resolving chord exactly with the words, never before them. The chill triggers a Web Audio synth can make (a new voice fading in, a sudden widening to a low and a high octave at once, a crescendo over four to eight bars, a solo line after the texture drops out, one unexpected chord such as a flat sixth, the childhood melody returning) are each used once or twice in the whole story. One total dropout is reserved for the night nobody texts. The music cuts to ambience a beat before the key line, never on it, and holds for a screen or two; the room tone can be the lock-screen clock ticking, which also tells the player the page has not frozen. The melody returns as a single line only when the next bond appears. That is Up's lesson applied to sound. Sound effects work as punctuation: a phone buzz, and after the early messages a key in a lock, the sound of getting home, which is missing on the empty night.

Inference, for the build: four or five layers run on one clock (a pad or drone, a bass, the melody, a soft pulse, a high sparkle), each through its own gain node faded over one to four seconds with setTargetAtTime. Acts change which layers play, and story beats switch phrases at bar lines. Synthesised timbres suit the music box and the online friend's square-wave voice. Recorded stems of the author's own guitar fit the same layering, and a guitar held back for the whole story can be the new voice that enters under the theme at the final send. On the first tap the AudioContext starts and the audio session is set to playback. Safari pauses audio when the page goes to the background ([WebKit bug 261554](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261554)), so the music has to resume cleanly on the next tap after a player comes back.

Colour research gives an equally plain rule. In Valdez and Mehrabian's ratings of Munsell colour chips, **pleasure rose mostly with brightness (0.69 brightness plus 0.22 saturation) and arousal mostly with saturation (0.60 saturation minus 0.31 brightness)** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/)). Wilms and Oberfeld found that saturated, bright colours raised arousal, red more than blue or green, and that grey briefly slowed the heart while colours sped it up ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28612080/)). Up's colour script, as /Film reads it, follows the story: colour arrives with Ellie, Carl's world turns drab grey after her death, and colour returns as he sets out with Russell, each new character adding some ([/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/503975/cool-stuff-lou-romanos-color-script-for-pixars-up/)). Gris starts in black and white and makes finding colour its core mechanic ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/what-does-gris-mean-we-talk-to-nomada-studio/)). Florence dresses its arguments in grey with red accents ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/florence/b/ios/archive/2018/02/20/florence-game-informer-review.aspx?amp)). Firewatch's team tied a colour script to key story moments with a target mood for each. Outdoors, they found, the sky often fills half the screen and sets most of the colour ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)). Passage draws a lifetime as the width of the screen: young, you see a hazy future ahead and nothing behind; later, memories pile up behind you and the view ahead narrows ([Rohrer](http://hcsoftware.sourceforge.net/passage/statement.html)).

Inference: brightness sets how good a scene feels and saturation sets how intense it feels, with hue as a second-order flavour. Loss shows mainly through drained saturation, with darkness kept for danger, as Up uses dark for danger and grey for loss. Blue stays present in the calm acts. The highest saturation and brightness are saved for a few peaks. Each cool scene gets one warm accent, such as a window or a scarf, and each warm scene one cool shadow. Colour comes back one hue at a time, and the first warm accent after the grey is an object that matters. The object a scene is about (a crib, a lit window, a bike, a phone) gets the most shapes and the strongest contrast, and the hills stay simple. Lit windows carry the home signal in every blue-hour and night scene. In the lines view between acts, the trail of the past grows to the left of "you" and the space ahead stays empty and slightly hazy, so the screen itself ages. The colour plan already written into the repo's scenes.js (dawn and morning for childhood, a bright noon for school summers, dusk and a drained blue night for leaving home, dawn for finding your feet, a golden afternoon and autumn for the ageing parents, a clear night with warm windows at the end) matches this research. Drawn as one strip of thumbnails before any scene is built, it can be read as a whole, the way Pixar reads a colour script.

The table joins the music, colour and scenery plans into one script. Every value in it is an inference from the research above: no study maps tempo ranges to life stages, and the colour directions are relative, to be tuned by eye.

## Five to seven pre-painted layers give the poster look on an iPhone

Firewatch's look began with one painting by co-director Olly Moss in the manner of New Deal era National Park Service posters, and critics compared the game to silkscreened poster art brought to life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewatch)). Write-ups of Jane Ng's 2015 GDC talk describe bold, saturated colours in distinct layers that each add depth, fog that adds distance, flat shapes with strong silhouettes and abstract inner detail, and more texture on the objects the story needs; Ng said the style would have carried over to 2D without much trouble, and the hard part was 3D ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)). She built **only 23 unique trees** and reused them thousands of times ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/environmental-artist-jane-ng-only-made-23-unique-trees-for-i-firewatch-i-)). A developer who rebuilt the fog from the talk describes a pass that picks each pixel's fog colour from a gradient by depth ([grrava](https://grrava.blogspot.com/2018/08/stylistic-fog-from-firewatch-with.html)). The physics it imitates is aerial perspective: with distance, contrast falls and colour drifts toward the background, blue in daylight and reddish near sunrise or sunset ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_perspective)). Inference: a 2D scene already knows each layer's depth, so the per-pixel pass becomes one colour per layer read from a depth gradient, and because the gradient runs between two colours, near layers can tint toward one hue and far ones toward another, which is what makes this kind of fog look painted.

The recipe below assembles separate sources into one painter. The ridge-spacing factor comes from a generative mountains sketch that draws 9 ridges with gaps shrinking toward the horizon ([Processing forum](https://discourse.processing.org/t/generative-mountains/28125)), and the ridge shapes from 1D midpoint displacement, where each pass moves every midpoint by a random amount and then shrinks that amount by a roughness factor ([somethinghitme](https://somethinghitme.com/2013/11/11/simple-2d-terrain-with-midpoint-displacement/)). The other values are inferences: starting points to tune by eye. The palettes are to be invented for this story, since no official Firewatch palette exists in any source found and sampling its stills would edge toward copying.

| Step | How | Starting values |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | One object per scene from time of day, season and mood: skyTop, skyMid, skyHorizon, glow, fogNear, fogFar, ridgeNear, accent. Everything below reads from it | Two act-level knobs, saturation and brightness, applied in OKLCH before the rest is derived |
| Sky | One vertical linear gradient with a lighter band at the horizon, drawn once to its own canvas | 3 to 4 stops; { alpha: false } on the bottom canvas |
| Sun or moon | A flat disc on a radial-gradient glow; a second wide, faint glow on the sun's side of the horizon | Glow alpha 0.5 to 0.8 at the centre to 0 at 3 to 6 radii; horizon glow alpha 0.15 to 0.25 |
| Depth fog | For depth d from 0 (near) to 1 (far): fogColour = mix(fogNear, fogFar, d); fogAmount = maxFog times d to the power g; layer colour = mix(ridgeNear, fogColour, fogAmount) | maxFog 0.85 to 0.95; g 0.6 to 1.0; mix in OKLab or linear RGB to avoid muddy greys |
| Ridges | Midpoint displacement or two sines plus two octaves of noise, seeded so a scene redraws the same | 5 to 7 layers; each gap the previous one divided by 1.2; roughness 0.5 for hills to 0.65 for mountains; strips 1.5 to 2 screens wide with equal ends if they wrap |
| Mist | Fill each ridge with a vertical gradient from its colour at the crest to its fog colour at the base; add full-width fog bands between chosen layers | 2 or 3 bands, 30 to 80 CSS px tall, peaking at alpha 0.35 to 0.6 |
| Trees | Pines as narrow zigzag triangles in the ridge's own colour, drawn into the same path so the layer is one fill | Height scaled by 1 minus d; denser near; none on the two farthest ridges |
| Light and life | A rim stroke in the glow colour on the sun side of the nearest ridges and figures; stars; birds as two quadratic curves; lit windows with a soft radial halo | Rim 1 to 2 px at alpha 0.4 to 0.7; 80 to 200 stars; halo alpha 0.2 to 0.35 |
| People | Flat silhouettes in the nearest layer's colour or one shade darker, rim-lit on the sun side | No faces anywhere |

The performance rules come from MDN and from Safari's limits. MDN advises pre-rendering static drawing to offscreen canvases and copying it with drawImage, whole-pixel coordinates, separate stacked canvases for rarely and often changing content, { alpha: false } where nothing shows through, a backing store scaled by devicePixelRatio, no shadowBlur, and drawing inside requestAnimationFrame ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas)). **ctx.filter, the canvas form of CSS blur and saturate, is disabled by default in Safari 18 on iOS** ([Can I use](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_canvasrenderingcontext2d_filter)). A single canvas over 16,777,216 pixels fails in Safari ([PQINA](https://pqina.nl/blog/canvas-area-exceeds-the-maximum-limit/)), and Safari also caps total canvas memory. Reported budgets range from 224 to 384 MB across blogs, a WebKit bug and Apple's forums, none of them official, and the suggested fix is to shrink a finished canvas to 1 by 1 pixel to release it ([PQINA](https://www.pqina.nl/blog/total-canvas-memory-use-exceeds-the-maximum-limit/)). A full-screen 390 by 844 canvas costs about 11.9 MB at pixel ratio 3 and about 5.3 MB at 2, so eight full-screen layers at ratio 3 would take about 95 MB of that budget.

Inference: every static layer is painted once when a scene opens, and during play the canvases only slide at whole-pixel offsets, at a parallax speed proportional to 1 minus d. The pixel ratio is capped at 2, as the site's menu already does in covers.js. Far layers are cropped to the strip they cover, and the canvases of a finished scene are shrunk to 1 by 1. Any blur or desaturation is baked into the colours. The painter already in the repo groups each scene into three canvases (far, middle and near) with a margin for drift, which holds memory to about three screens' worth however many ridges a scene has, and it drives colour with a single vibrance value per scene. The research suggests adding a second value for brightness, because brightness and saturation move different feelings: lowering brightness alone reads as danger, and lowering saturation reads as loss. No measured frame rates for layered canvas scenes on current iPhones were found, so the first full scene should be profiled on a real device.

### Stills move on beats, one change at a time

Visual novels make still pictures act with a small toolkit. The devlog of Of Sense and Soul uses close shots on one figure for introspection and full shots of a room for distance, quick pans between speakers for banter, and hard cuts with no pan for arguments and heavy silences. A dolly zoom, with the figure pulled closer while the background softens, heightens emotion when it lasts under a second and reads as a pause for thought over a few seconds. Blurring, darkening, desaturating or hiding the background adds intimacy, and slow moves feel leisurely while near-instant cuts feel urgent ([Of Sense and Soul devlog](https://forsythiaproductions.itch.io/of-sense-and-soul/devlog/480454/sprites-camera-action-vn-cinematography-in-of-sense-and-soul)). The writer of Complex Relations keeps full illustrations for scenes the regular art cannot carry and paces them with generous visual beats, deliberately spreading out image changes that most visual novels would fire at once ([Complex Relations devlog](https://amai-works.itch.io/complex-relations/devlog/206135/cgs-and-visual-beats)). Ren'Py, the engine most indie visual novels use, ships transitions with set lengths: a dissolve of 0.5 seconds, a fade of 0.5 seconds to black and 0.5 back, a screen shake of 0.25 seconds, and a documented flash that fades to white in 0.1 seconds and back over 0.5 ([Ren'Py documentation](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/transitions.html)). A small text box keeps the picture visible, so the art carries more of the story ([Towards the End Sky](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2022/10/nvl-vs-adv-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html)).

Inference: with faceless figures, the camera and the posture do the work faces do elsewhere. Two silhouettes moving closer on a ridge line is the flat version of a close-up, and a figure turning away can say what a line of dialogue would. A scene holds across many taps and changes on beats, one thing per tap, such as a light coming on or a figure leaving the frame. Background, figures and music never all change on the same tap. A full-detail scene, with the stars and lit windows drawn in full, works like a visual novel's rare illustration: it comes after many plain, muted screens, and its effect depends on scarcity. A starting transition grammar follows Ren'Py's lengths. A 1-second fade through black marks a jump in years and a 0.5-second dissolve a change inside a scene. A white flash (in over about 0.1 seconds, out over 0.5) is kept for a memory or a realisation, and a shake is almost never used, since in a gentle story a shake reads as a gunshot. In the painter these are cheap. A push-in is a scale of the composited canvases from 1.00 to about 1.04 over 6 to 10 seconds for inner thought, or a fast push under a second for a shock. A fade or a flash is one full-screen rectangle whose opacity changes. A softened background is a translucent overlay in the fog colour over the far and middle canvases, since ctx.filter blur is off in Safari. The in-story phone sits over the scene like a small text box, so the picture stays visible behind it.

## Conclusion

The research gives the message, the melody and the colour one shared arc. Each is taught lightly in childhood and withheld on the empty night, so its changed return at the end is felt as a return. Inference: the motif table that tracks running jokes can also hold each act's music layers and its saturation and brightness values, so one data file drives every channel and a change in pacing moves them together. The same logic shows where the story is fragile. The night nobody texts carries more of the ending than the ending scene does, because Frijda's order needs the reader to expect that the bond will fail; if that night is trimmed for length, the final send loses most of its charge. The constraints of a phone and Canvas 2D also line up with the evidence: faceless figures, few layers and simple backgrounds at key beats are cheap to draw and raise identification.

Some questions remain open. No source shows how players on mobile web react to seconds with nothing to tap (they may think the page froze, though visual novel players praise a ticking clock in place of dead silence) or whether a frame that starts at the end raises or lowers completion. Layered canvas scenes have not been measured on current iPhones, and the lump in the throat has no peer-reviewed study. The tempo and palette values are untested, and the @lessonsinline format could not be verified. Inference: the sensible order of work is to build the last scene first, with its music and palette, and then the night nobody texts, and to show both to a few readers who know nothing about the story. If those two scenes move them, the rest of the life is the work of earning them.
